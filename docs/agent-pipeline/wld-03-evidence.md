## Reference behavior (source-derived)

### A. The rift entity

1. **The rift is a custom entity outside the mob hierarchy, tracked to clients through four synced values.** `EntityFluxRift` extends the bare entity base, not a mob, with a two-block-sized shell; it registers four data-manager keys — a seed, a size (default five), a stability float, and a collapse flag — and it registers in the entity registry as `FluxRift` with a sixty-four-block tracking range, a twenty-tick update interval, and velocity updates off (`common/entities/EntityFluxRift.java:61-87`; `common/config/ConfigEntities.java:84`); the client draws it through its own renderer (`proxies/ProxyEntities.java:98`) and the language file names it the Flux Rift (`assets/thaumcraft/lang/en_us.lang:543`). It cannot be moved or pushed — the move override is an empty body (`common/entities/EntityFluxRift.java:197-198`) — and it overrides no collision hook, so entities walk through it: the hazard is its spine, not its body. Its scan page is the hidden `!FluxRift` scanning key (`common/config/ConfigResearch.java:170-171`).

2. **The creation path: the aura thread's armed position is consumed by the world tick, scattered inside that chunk, and turned into a rift sized by the local flux.** The arming side is [wld-01-evidence.md](wld-01-evidence.md)'s (finding 6): a chunk past three quarters of its base flux rolls a pending position — the chunk's base corner — into the per-dimension map. The consumption side is this lane's: the world tick's end phase, once every twenty ticks, takes whatever is pending for the dimension and calls the static creation routine — unless the misc wuss flag is set, in which case the pending entry is dropped with nothing created (`common/lib/events/ServerEvents.java:165-170`). The routine offsets the position up to sixteen blocks on each horizontal axis, resolves the precipitation height, and for a dimension without sky light walks up from y=10 in one-to-five block steps until air is found, abandoning if that passes the usable ceiling (`common/entities/EntityFluxRift.java:290-300`). It then refuses when any rift is already within thirty-two blocks, creates the entity at the surface centre with a random seed and facing, and reads the chunk's flux: the working size is the square root of three times the flux, the rift only materialises when that figure exceeds five — an effective flux above roughly eight and a third — and on success that figure is drained from the chunk as the rift's opening claim (`:301-311`). Nearby players without the `f_toomuchflux` research get the "too much flux" line and complete it (`:312-321`; `assets/thaumcraft/lang/en_us.lang:1094`) — a gate for the flux-family stages, not the rift page's own unlock, which comes from the hidden scan key (`assets/thaumcraft/research/basics.json:315-323`, the page's parents at `:319`; the scan key `common/config/ConfigResearch.java:170-171`).

3. **The shell is a deterministic function of the seed and size, so client and server build the same shape.** The size setter rebuilds the geometry and the bounding box; the rebuild seeds a fresh random from the stored seed and walks two wandering arms outward from the centre — a step count of the size over three, an initial girth of size over three hundred shrinking along each arm, and a width list ending at zero — and the entity's bounds are recomputed from the extreme points so the visual and physical shell follow the arms (`common/entities/EntityFluxRift.java:114-159`, `:408-438`); any size change re-runs the rebuild (`:202-204`). Because the shape derives from the synced seed alone, two clients and the server agree on it; the client draw, its goggle check, and the stability-keyed wobble are the renderer's (`client/renderers/entity/RenderFluxRift.java:44`, `:49`) and belong to the client lane.

4. **While open, the rift eats terrain and hurts whatever crosses its spine.** Every server tick the rift picks two neighbouring points of its own spine, ray-traces the segment between them, and destroys the first solid block hit — any block with non-negative hardness that supports collision — playing the break effect; it never picks a block it cannot break (`common/entities/EntityFluxRift.java:209-221`). Entities within half a block of that segment take two damage from an out-of-world source, dropped items are killed outright, and creative players are skipped (`:222-237`). Every three hundred ticks it plays the portal sound at a low volume, and that is the only regular audio (`:272-274`).

5. **Growth is flux-fed, capped, and gated on stability — the rift literally eats the chunk's flux to grow.** Once per six-hundred-tick window, offset by the entity id, the rift reads the chunk's flux and its own size: when the flux covers the square root of twice the size, the size is under one hundred, and the tier is not very stable, it drains that figure and grows one step (`common/entities/EntityFluxRift.java:261-267`). In between, its stability bleeds — a fifth of a point every hundred and twenty ticks — and the stored figure clamps into the range minus one hundred to one hundred (`:258-260`, `:104-112`); the tiers are very stable above fifty, stable from zero, unstable down to minus twenty-five, and very unstable below that (`:444-446`). The player-facing page states the model exactly: the rifts feed on flux, grow more unstable over time, and burst (`assets/thaumcraft/lang/en_us.lang:1296-1297`).

6. **Below zero stability the rift rolls a burst from a five-entry weighted table, and a burst quiets it.** In the same six-hundred-tick window, when the stored stability is negative, a roll under the absolute stability plus the size, out of a thousand, fires one entry (`common/entities/EntityFluxRift.java:268-270`). The table, with weights and stability refunds: spawn a wisp (weight fifty, refund five); become a taint seed prime (weight ten, no refund); infect nearby living beings (weight twenty, refund ten); cast a flux cloud at the nearest player (weight twenty, refund ten); collapse outright (weight one, no refund) (`:508-512`). A pending entry is vetoed when it is flagged not-near-taint and the position is near a taint seed; when an entry fires and declares its work done, its refund — if any — is added back to stability, and the cloud entry never declares, so its refund is dead in the table (`:403-405`) — this refund is the page's "they regain some stability when this happens" (`:332-334`, `:403-405`; `assets/thaumcraft/lang/en_us.lang:1297`). The entries in turn: the wisp entry spawns a mod wisp up to five blocks away, one in five as a flux-aspect wisp, and only where its own spawn check passes — the creature is `WLD-05`'s, the flux-typed spawn is this lane's (`common/entities/EntityFluxRift.java:336-348`). The prime entry spawns a taint seed prime up to five blocks away, refused near an existing seed, and on success kills the rift, hands the seed the rift's size as a boost, and leaves half the size as aura pollution (`:349-360`) — the mechanism by which an unmanaged rift becomes a taint outbreak. The infection entry gives every living being within sixteen blocks two and a half minutes of the infectious vis-exhaust potion at amplifier two with curatives cleared, and messages players (`:361-379`; `assets/thaumcraft/lang/en_us.lang:1093`). The cloud entry builds a focus package aimed from the nearest player within sixteen blocks — a cloud medium of radius one to three, duration between the rift's size floor and cap, carrying the flux effect — and casts it through the focus engine (`common/entities/EntityFluxRift.java:380-397`; the engine's execution semantics are [cas-02-evidence.md](cas-02-evidence.md)'s). The collapse entry simply flips the rift into its collapse state (`:398-401`).

7. **Collapse: the rift unwinds a point of size per tick, feeds the aura as it goes, and pays out pearls, void seeds, and consequences by tier.** Flipping the collapse flag records the current size as the recorded maximum, and every following tick shrinks the size by one while a coin flip adds a vis point or a flux point to the chunk — a per-tick aura credit — with a one-in-ten chance of a small explosion offset from the body; at size one the completion runs (`common/entities/EntityFluxRift.java:93-98`, `:242-257`). A rift whose geometry degenerates to fewer than three points flips itself into collapse, as does the weight-one table entry (`:239-241`, `:398-401`). Completion pays: a roll of the square root of the recorded maximum out of a hundred for a primordial pearl with a damage value of four plus up to three, the square root of the maximum as void seeds, an effect packet to everyone within sixty-four blocks, and then effects by tier — read at completion and cascading through the fall-through case labels, so a very unstable rift applies all three, an unstable one the lower two, and a merely stable one only the last (`:459-501`). The cascading tiers: very unstable inflicts the taint poison for up to two minutes, scaled by proximity — candidates gather within thirty-two blocks, but the scaling term reaches zero at a squared distance of thirty-two, so the effective radius is under six blocks; unstable inflicts weakness for up to five minutes under the same scaling; and any tier at or below stable adds up to twenty-five points each of normal and temporary warp to players in range (`:468-496`). The page warns that the energy released scales with instability, and tells the player to stabilise a rift before collapsing it (`assets/thaumcraft/lang/en_us.lang:1299-1301`).

8. **Persistence: five keys, one read-order wrinkle, and one float-to-int read — the reload probes' targets.** The entity writes the recorded maximum, the size, the seed, the stability, and the collapse flag, and reads them back on load (`common/entities/EntityFluxRift.java:181-195`). Three wrinkles a port must decide rather than copy: the read sets the size before the seed (`:191-192`), while the size setter is what rebuilds the geometry from the seed (`:118-121`, `:127-129`), so the first rebuild after a load runs against the default seed until a later size change re-runs it; the stability is written as a float and read through the integer getter (`:185`, `:193`), so any fractional stability depends on what the compound does with the mismatch — outside this tree (unverified list); and reading the collapse flag through its setter re-records the maximum from the current size (`:189-195` with `:93-98`), which changes the completion's pearl roll for a rift that reloads mid-collapse. Nothing else removes a rift: it is not a mob, has no despawn timer, and only its own collapse or an external kill (findings 10-12) ends it.

### B. Stabilization, suppression, and the counterplay devices

9. **A stabilizer block quells every rift within eight blocks, paying in aura pollution.** The stabilizer's block, its energy battery, its shield, and the pedestal/inlay interplay are [alc-03-evidence.md](alc-03-evidence.md) items 4 and 20-22 and are not re-derived here; what this lane adds is its rift-facing path. On a five-tick cadence, while it holds energy and is not resting, the tile finds rifts within eight blocks; for each one that is alive, not already very stable, and covered by the energy spend, it adds a flat 0.125 stability and extends its own delay by five ticks, bailing out when the energy runs dry (`common/tiles/devices/TileStabilizer.java:48-55`, `:66-84`; the increment `common/entities/EntityFluxRift.java:440-442`). The energy itself recovers one point per twenty ticks and pollutes a quarter point of aura per point recovered (`common/tiles/devices/TileStabilizer.java:36-47`) — so holding a rift stable is a standing flux cost, and the stabilizer is the only caller of the rift's dedicated stability-adding entry point (the burst refund of finding 6 also raises stability, but through the direct setter): with enough of them a rift sits above the burst gate of finding 6, which is what the page means by "keeping them stable" (`assets/thaumcraft/lang/en_us.lang:1297`).

10. **The void siphon is the industrial answer: leech a rift for void seeds and accept the instability and shrink.** The tile accrues progress every twenty ticks while the block is enabled and its slot can accept more seeds; per valid rift — within eight blocks, alive, size at least two, and in line of sight from the siphon's top — it adds the square root of the size to progress, subtracts a fifteenth of that from the rift's stability, and rolls one in thirty-three for a one-step shrink; every two thousand progress yields one void seed into the slot (`common/tiles/crafting/TileVoidSiphon.java:31-68`, `:70-91`). Progress is saved as a short and the slot through the shared tile save (`:93-104`). A redstone signal disables the block through the device base's enabled property, and the base flips that property on neighbour changes (`common/blocks/crafting/BlockVoidSiphon.java:24`; `common/blocks/BlockTCDevice.java:49-52`, `:68-73`) — matching the page: leeched energy becomes void seeds faster from larger rifts, at the cost of instability and shrink, and a redstone signal disables it (`assets/thaumcraft/lang/en_us.lang:1795-1796`; the page is eldritch-gated, `assets/thaumcraft/research/eldritch.json:22-39`).

11. **The causality collapser is the deliberate collapse: a thrown charge that explodes and flips every rift within three blocks into collapse.** The item consumes itself unless the thrower is creative and throws a projectile with a fixed launching speed (`common/items/consumables/ItemCausalityCollapser.java:23-34`); on impact the projectile makes a power-two explosion and flips every rift within three blocks into the collapse state (`common/entities/projectile/EntityCausalityCollapser.java:43-52`), which runs the unwind and payout of finding 7. The item is an infusion behind the "Closing Flux Rifts" page, parented on the rift page, the infusion category, and the vis battery (`assets/thaumcraft/research/basics.json:326-339`, parents at `:330`; `common/config/ConfigRecipes.java:368`), and the page's own advice — stabilise first — is a consequence of the cascading tiers (`assets/thaumcraft/lang/en_us.lang:1301`).

12. **The creative sponge is the operator's lever: drain eighty-one chunks, and delete rifts while sneaking.** On a right-click the sponge drains up to five hundred flux from each chunk in the nine-by-nine square around the player and reports the total, and while the thrower sneaks it also kills every rift within thirty-two blocks and reports the count (`common/items/misc/ItemCreativeFluxSponge.java:49-72`). It is the flux lever for the checklist below and the reason the creative profile can stage a clean baseline. The survival flux sink — the condenser's essentia-priced conversion — is [aut-04-evidence.md](aut-04-evidence.md) item 5's surface.

### C. Taint

13. **The taint is not a biome: it is the block family painted over whatever biome is there, coordinated by seeds — and no tainted biome is registered.** The tree registers no taint biome at all; a taint patch is ordinary tainted blocks plus the seed entities that justify their spread, and every block in the family consults the seed registry on its own ticks, so removing the seeds removes the patch (findings 14, 16, 20). The player page states the contract: from the seed's foul air the taint grows, and without it the growth withers and dies (`assets/thaumcraft/lang/en_us.lang:1208`). The biome registry and its magical-forest coupling are [wld-02-evidence.md](wld-02-evidence.md)'s; this lane touches biomes only where a taint block or the eldritch biome is checked (finding 16).

14. **The block family: one shared class for the four solids, one fibre, one leaf-mounted growth, one log, one goo — with a "withering" rule for each.** Registered together: fibrous taint, crusted taint, tainted soil, tainted rock, the taint geyser, the swollen-taint feature, and the taintwood log (`common/config/ConfigBlocks.java:254-260`), plus the flux goo fluid block (`:349-350`); the language file names them all (`assets/thaumcraft/lang/en_us.lang:253`, `:291-297`). The four solids share one class — hardness ten, blast resistance one hundred, gore sounds, random ticking (`common/blocks/world/taint/BlockTaint.java:44-55`). Their shared laws:
    - **Withering.** With no seed in range, each solid rolls one in ten per tick and dies: tainted rock reverts to porous stone, tainted soil to dirt, crust and geyser to flux goo, anything else to air (`:74-98`). Fibre is starker still — its tick dies immediately when no seed is near, and an isolated strand with no growth state clears itself when a neighbour changes (`common/blocks/world/taint/BlockTaintFibre.java:126-141`, `:143-148`).
    - **Drops and harvest.** The normal drop hook returns no item; the public four-argument silk-harvest test answers true while the no-argument one answers false — a wrinkle the harvest probe records rather than a derived behavior (unverified list) (`common/blocks/world/taint/BlockTaint.java:138-140`, `:210-216`); tainted rock alone adds a flux crystal on the tail of a fifteen-entry roll plus fortune (`:198-208`).
    - **Touch.** Walking a solid rolls one in two hundred and fifty for the taint poison; walking fibre rolls one in seven hundred and fifty (`common/blocks/world/taint/BlockTaint.java:142-146`; `common/blocks/world/taint/BlockTaintFibre.java:186-190`).
    - **Crust is the mobile form.** It falls like sand through a dedicated falling-taint entity — dropping a block at the landing site, or, in unloaded surroundings, teleporting the crust there directly (`common/blocks/world/taint/BlockTaint.java:102-124`, `:158-196`; `common/entities/EntityFallingTaint.java:84-127`).
    - **The geyser is the flux engine.** With a player within thirty-two blocks and no swarm already in that range it spawns a taint swarm one time in five; otherwise, whenever the local flux is under two, it adds a quarter point of flux (`common/blocks/world/taint/BlockTaint.java:125-134`) — the taint's own counter-strategy against clean air, and the reason a neglected patch keeps feeding the next rift.
    - **Fibre growth states are position hashing.** A fifty-entry roll over the block's own coordinate yields a low, a tall, a spore, or an overhead variant; the spore variant glows at light twelve and the tall and overhead at six, and the spore variant drops a flux crystal in five when broken while always adding a flux point (`common/blocks/world/taint/BlockTaintFibre.java:352-358`, `:372-399`, `:116-124`) — cleaning taint pollutes.
15. **Fibrous taint is the spreading surface, and its rate is flux-scaled.** The spread routine returns outright in wuss mode; otherwise it rolls against the configured spread rate — a hundred means one percent at that point (finding 23) — scaled by a factor of a thousandth plus twice the chunk's flux saturation, so a saturated chunk spreads hundreds of times faster than a clean one (`common/blocks/world/taint/TaintHelper.java:85-92`; the saturation read itself is [wld-01-evidence.md](wld-01-evidence.md)'s derived read). A spread only proceeds when a seed is within the configured area; it then picks one of the twenty-six neighbours of the source and applies the conversion table (`common/blocks/world/taint/TaintHelper.java:93-157`):
    - a replaceable, non-liquid, non-leaf cell adjacent to a solid becomes fibre, at a hundredth of a point of flux (`:107-112`);
    - a leaf block becomes swollen taint when a taint log touches it, a roll under three fifths, and fibre otherwise — the swollen branch drains nothing (`:113-124`);
    - a cell hemmed by taint — more tainted or non-solid faces than not — converts by material: wood becomes a taintwood log, draining nothing; mushrooms, gourds, cactus, coral, sponge, and wood materials become crust; sand, ground, grass, and clay become tainted soil; stone becomes tainted rock — the crust, soil, and rock branches each paying a hundredth of a point while the wood branch does not, and hemmed targets of hardness five or more are skipped (`:125-148`);
    - targets harder than ten, or unbreakable, are refused outright (`:103-106`).
    And at the fringe of a seed's reach — inside the configured area but outside four fifths of it — a tainted soil or rock block with air above and at least five local flux rolls the configured rate at a third for a fresh taint seed of its own, which costs five flux when the spawn succeeds (`:66-79`, `:149-156`). So the taint's stage gates are: a live seed within the area to allow any conversion, a hard-target veto, five flux for forking a new seed at the fringe, and speed proportional to flux throughout.

16. **The seed is the immobile organ of the taint: a stationary mob that spreads it, feeds on flux, and starves without it.** `EntityTaintSeed` is a mob with seventy-five health, four damage, zero movement speed, a meter-and-a-half body, eight experience, no despawn, and a flux crystal as its only drop; its own spawn check refuses within four fifths of the configured area of another seed and requires the difficulty not be peaceful (`common/entities/monster/tainted/EntityTaintSeed.java:42-55`, `:101-114`, `:209-247`). It registers itself in the per-dimension seed map on its first tick and every minute, and removes itself on death (`:116-128`; `common/blocks/world/taint/TaintHelper.java:33-40`). Each second, on a live body: when the chunk's flux saturation is zero (and it has no boost left) it starves — a half point of starve damage plus a tenth of a point of pollution — otherwise it spreads fibres around itself ignoring both the wuss gate and the rate roll, which is what makes a seed's own spread unconditional (`common/entities/monster/tainted/EntityTaintSeed.java:129-143`; the ignore flag `common/blocks/world/taint/TaintHelper.java:85-93`). Also each second it gives every living being within four blocks five seconds of the taint poison at amplifier one below its area, and, with an attack target inside sixteen blocks in sight, it spawns a small tentacle beside the target when that target stands in taint material or in the eldritch biome (the biome is `WLD-04`'s) (`common/entities/monster/tainted/EntityTaintSeed.java:144-152`, `:173-183`). Only two lines in the whole tree ever create a seed: the rift's burst table and the fringe roll of an existing patch (`common/entities/EntityFluxRift.java:350`; `common/blocks/world/taint/TaintHelper.java:150`) — so a taint outbreak always chains from a rift. The prime variant is the bigger organ: a two-block body, one hundred and fifty health, seven damage, doubled area, twelve experience, and up to three crystals on death (`common/entities/monster/tainted/EntityTaintSeedPrime.java:7-37`), and the burst that spawns it hands it the rift's size as a boost, which acts as free spreading time before it must eat flux (`common/entities/EntityFluxRift.java:352-355`; `common/entities/monster/tainted/EntityTaintSeed.java:131-139`).

17. **The rest of the cast — swarm, crawler, tentacles — is spawned by blocks and infections, not by the spawner.** The swarm is thirty health, two damage, a two-block flier that wanders and despawns, weaknesses its target on a hit, and drops a crystal half the time; its natural-spawn rule (light at or under a random seven) is unreachable because the taint cast has no spawn registration — the mod's only natural spawn additions are the brainy zombie, the Pech, the fire bat, and the nether wisp, all config-gated (`common/entities/monster/tainted/EntityTaintSwarm.java:31-44`, `:92-96`, `:196-214`, `:233-254`; `common/config/ConfigEntities.java:214-238`). Its summoned flag — which would make it ignore players and die without a target — is written by nothing in this tree (`common/entities/monster/tainted/EntityTaintSwarm.java:98-104`, `:127-130`). The swarm enters the world from tainted geysers (finding 14). The crawler is eight health, fast, a half-block arthropod with three experience; breaking a swollen-taint block spawns one about one time in three — a roll under a third — and otherwise pollutes the aura, and the crawler paints fibrous taint under itself every two seconds as it walks wherever a solid neighbour allows (`common/blocks/world/taint/BlockTaintFeature.java:43-55`; `common/entities/monster/tainted/EntityTaintCrawler.java:76-81`, `:107-117`). Its bite infects on normal about one in four and on hard four in seven (`common/entities/monster/tainted/EntityTaintCrawler.java:141-158`). The tentacles: the full one is a fifty-health, seven-damage pillar that only spawns onto taint material, starves off it, and seeds small tentacles toward a target within sixteen blocks; the small one is a five-health, two-damage spike with a two-hundred-tick life that cannot spawn on its own and drops nothing (`common/entities/monster/tainted/EntityTaintacle.java:34-43`, `:61-71`, `:82-101`, `:103-113`; `common/entities/monster/tainted/EntityTaintacleSmall.java:9-47`). Every tainted mob treats the others as allies and refuses to attack tainted classes (`common/entities/monster/tainted/EntityTaintSeed.java:93-99` and the peers' identical overrides). The taint poison itself damages non-tainted living things on its periodic effect and heals creatures carrying the taint champion mark (`api/potions/PotionFluxTaint.java:43-58`); a living being — not undead, not an owned construct, not already tainted — reduced below a heart while carrying the poison flips a coin on the damage event and, on tails, is converted into a tainted champion through the entity helper, which rewrites its champion modifier, health, and damage (`common/lib/events/EntityEvents.java:172-175`; `common/lib/utils/EntityUtils.java:396-434`) — the stake of standing in an outbreak, and the reason the conversion must not be reproduced as a plain death.

18. **The goo: flux made physical, banked in the world.** Flux goo is a finite fluid whose default state is level seven that feeds slimes, slows and poisons living things, and evaporates back into flux. Contact slows anything and gives non-slime living beings thirty seconds of the vis-exhaust potion at an amplifier a third of the goo's level with curatives cleared, while a thaumic slime below the goo's level can grow a size at a coin flip and drinks a level (`common/blocks/world/taint/BlockFluxGoo.java:45-68`; the creature is `WLD-05`'s). Each random tick, one time in four, the goo either loses a level — adding a point of flux as it goes — or, at level zero, either vanishes leaving a point of flux or turns into fibrous taint (`:70-107`). A pool of at least level two with air above births a size-one thaumic slime one time in fifty, and a pool of at least level six births a size-two one (`:72-89`). The goo's only other appearance is as the taint's residue — crust and geysers die into it, and the bottled-taint throwable seeds it in patches (a thrown bottle — `common/entities/projectile/EntityBottleTaint.java:45-74` — that inflicts the poison on non-tainted living beings within five blocks and seeds up to ten goo blocks around the impact; the item `common/items/consumables/ItemBottleTaint.java:16-35`, behind the bottled-taint alchemy page `assets/thaumcraft/research/alchemy.json:131-146`).

19. **The wood-side consequences: taintwood and swollen taint.** A taintwood log is an axis log with axe harvesting, gore sounds, and full blast resistance whose tick spreads fibres while a seed is near and dies into goo otherwise; breaking one dusts the leaves in the surrounding nine-cube as ordinary log breaks do (`common/blocks/world/taint/BlockTaintLog.java:23-35`, `:49-63`, `:99-110`). Swollen taint is the glowing growth mounted on leaves touching a taint log: hardness a tenth, light at five eighths of full; its tick spreads fibres, dies into goo without a seed at one in ten, and one in a hundred ticks converts its cell into a taint geyser when mounted on an upright taintwood log (`common/blocks/world/taint/BlockTaintFeature.java:27-37`, `:62-77`). Like the solids it answers true on the public silk test and false on the no-argument one, and drops no item (`:39-41`, `:84-90`).

### D. Cleansing and the counterplay paths, with costs

20. **The primary counterplay is killing the organ: remove the seed and the taint withers on its own clock.** Because every block and fibre checks the seed map each tick, the absence of a seed within the configured area starts the decay rolls of finding 14 — solids recede at one in ten per tick, fibre at once, and each family reverts to its specific quiet form (rock to porous stone, soil to dirt, crust and geyser to goo, the rest to air). No item, research, or device is needed; the seed is a normal seventy-five-health mob, and the page frames fighting it as the risk that cleans an area (`assets/thaumcraft/lang/en_us.lang:1208`). Two costs to record: the cleanup can spawn a crawler (breaking swollen taint, finding 17), and most conversions and the spore-fibre break pay flux into the chunk — the wood and swollen-leaf branches excepted (finding 15) — so a careless cleanup can feed the next rift.
21. **The rift's counterplay is suppression or collapse, and every path has a cost.** Suppression (the stabilizer, finding 9) stands quietly and pays in aura pollution; harvesting (the siphon, finding 10) trades instability and shrink for void seeds; collapse (the collapser, finding 11) triggers the finding 7 payout; and the creative sponge deletes rifts outright (finding 12). There is no restore-to-clean path — a rift is never repaired, only held, drained, collapsed, or deleted. The config story centres on the wuss flag (finding 23), which suppresses creation and the block-driven taint work: with it set, a pending rift trigger is dropped unconsumed so no new rift ever forms, and the spread routine's caller-driven paths return outright before any spread or conversion — only a seed's own flag-ignoring spread and existing rifts carry on as before.
22. **Warp cleansing exists, but it is a player-side bath, not a world cleanser — and this tree has no ethereal-bloom-style taint remover.** Purifying fluid blocks warp while a player stands in a source block (who is not already warded): it reads the player's permanent warp, converts the source block to air, and applies the warp-ward effect with the duration shrinking as the warp grows (`common/blocks/misc/BlockFluidPure.java:40-55`; the fluid is made by tossing bath salts into water, `assets/thaumcraft/lang/en_us.lang:1337`, named at `:430`). It does nothing to taint, rifts, or flux. A sweeping check of the block and item inventory turns up no bloom or cleansing plant — the only purifying surface is the fluid; the sole bloom traces are a never-assigned field (`api/blocks/BlocksTC.java:90`) and name remnants in two non-English language files — so a port must not invent one as "equivalent". Sibling counterplay items are cross-referenced, not derived: the verdant charm cures the taint poison and the vanilla poison for a charge (`common/items/baubles/ItemVerdantCharm.java:86-87`, `:90-91`; equipment lane), the warp-ward potion and warp systems are `PLY-01`'s, and the condenser's flux-to-essentia conversion is [aut-04-evidence.md](aut-04-evidence.md) item 5's.
23. **Config: two spread numbers, one wuss flag with five read sites, and one dead spawn flag.** The world config exposes the fibre spread rate — a percentage chance per block tick, default one hundred of a range up to five hundred, with the comment that zero effectively stops fibre spread — and the seed spread area, default thirty-two of a range eight to two hundred and fifty-six, commented as the base radius from a seed and flux-modified in practice (`common/config/ModConfig.java:342-347`, `:379-380`); the area feeds the helper's three distance tests and the seed's own spawn check (`common/blocks/world/taint/TaintHelper.java:49-79`; `common/entities/monster/tainted/EntityTaintSeed.java:101-103`). The misc wuss flag — commented as disabling "Warp, Taint spread and similar mechanics", default false — has five read sites across three areas: the fibre spread gate (`common/blocks/world/taint/TaintHelper.java:86-88`), the rift creation consumption (`common/lib/events/ServerEvents.java:165-169`), and the warp paths (`common/lib/events/CraftingEvents.java:37`, `common/lib/events/PlayerEvents.java:329`, `common/lib/research/ResearchManager.java:139`) (`common/config/ModConfig.java:411-413`, `:428`). And `allowSpawnTaintacle` is declared and defaulted true but read nowhere (`common/config/ModConfig.java:367`, `:399`) — a dead gate, so taintacle spawning is not configurable in this tree (the full tentacle's champion white-list entry is the only spawn-side knob, `common/config/ConfigEntities.java:246`).

### E. Late-game consequences, multiplayer, and reload

24. **Late-game consequences in one chain: uncleared flux grows a rift, the rift eats flux and rots until it bursts, a burst can plant a seed, the seed paints taint, and the collapse pays out in warp.** Standing flux past three quarters of a chunk's base arms a rift ([wld-01-evidence.md](wld-01-evidence.md) finding 6); the rift eats the chunk's flux to grow and bleeds stability until it bursts (findings 5-6); a burst can spawn a taint seed prime carrying the rift's size as a boost (findings 6, 16); the seed spreads taint at flux-scaled rates, forks new seeds at its fringe once flux passes five, and keeps its own neighbourhood polluted through geysers (findings 14-15); a collapse pays pearls and void seeds but inflicts poison, weakness, and warp by tier (finding 7); and the void siphon turns an open rift into a void-seed farm at the price of destabilising it (finding 10). The pages tie the same chain together — flux warning, rift model, closer, siphon (`assets/thaumcraft/lang/en_us.lang:1259`, `:1296-1301`, `:1795-1796`).

25. **Multiplayer: the rift is the only networked object in this lane; the taint is server-authoritative world state.** The rift's seed, size, stability, and collapse flag sync to clients inside its sixty-four-block tracking range, both sides derive the shell from the seed (finding 3), and the collapse effect packet is radius-bound at sixty-four blocks (finding 7) — so two clients see the same rift; the burst's scripted cloud cast and every spawn happen on the server (finding 6). The taint carries no networking of its own: blocks replicate as ordinary block changes, entities as ordinary spawns, and the seed map is server-only static memory built and queried inside server-side ticks (`common/blocks/world/taint/TaintHelper.java:31-40`, `:160-162`; the writer runs behind the remote check `common/entities/monster/tainted/EntityTaintSeed.java:123`). Observables to compare across two clients: the rift's silhouette and its stability-keyed wobble, the visible change of state when the collapse flag flips, and the visible recede of a patch after its seed dies. Divergences to hunt are in the sampling, not the state: a client at the tracking edge across a collapse sees the shell flip state at its own update cadence, and the seed map's staleness for unloaded chunks (finding 26) can make two players in different chunks disagree about whether a patch is still growing.

26. **Reload classes.** The rift's persistence is finding 8, with its read-order and type wrinkles as the T7 probes. The taint's persistence is inherent — the blocks are world state and the seeds are saved mobs — and only the helper's position map is memory: each seed re-registers itself on its first tick after load and every minute thereafter, and any query that finds no live seed for a recorded position prunes the entry (`common/entities/monster/tainted/EntityTaintSeed.java:124-128`; `common/blocks/world/taint/TaintHelper.java:49-64`) — so immediately after a reload, a query for a seed in a not-yet-loaded chunk can prune a live seed's entry and let that neighbourhood's taint behave seed-free for up to the next re-registration. The devices persist through their tiles: the siphon's progress as a short plus its slot, the stabilizer's energy through its sync compound with a clamp on read (`common/tiles/crafting/TileVoidSiphon.java:93-104`; `common/tiles/devices/TileStabilizer.java:86-95`). And the pending rift trigger is a chunk position in static memory, not an entity: like the aura statics of [wld-01-evidence.md](wld-01-evidence.md) finding 14, it survives a same-process world exit and is consumed by the next end-phase tick, so a re-entered world can consume a stale trigger and raise a rift with no player anywhere near it — a wrinkle for the T7 reload probe.

## Rank-1 capture checklist

Reference setup for every step: the clean 1.12.2 profile whose only mods are the BETA26 jar and Baubles (`REF-0002` and the "Environment feasibility" section of [reference-catalog.md](reference-catalog.md)); a fresh creative world with its seed recorded in a new `REF` entry before the session. Carry the reading pair from [wld-01-evidence.md](wld-01-evidence.md) — the Thaumometer for the numeric vis and flux lines (the base proxy is a never-drained chunk's vis figure) — and the operator levers between measurements, never during one: `/thaumcraft reload` and `/thaumcraft research <player> <list|all|reset|<key>>` (`common/lib/CommandThaumcraft.java:64-70`, `:84-107`), plus the operator's standard time command for the clock. The flux kit is the creative tab's flux sponge (`common/items/misc/ItemCreativeFluxSponge.java:49-72`) plus the documented seeding paths: a crucible filled past its cap or broken while charged, a jar emptied by hand, and a loaded tube broken ([alc-00-evidence.md](alc-00-evidence.md) items 23-24; [alc-02-evidence.md](alc-02-evidence.md) items 11 and 17) — the crucible path matters most because a charged crucible's destruction adds a quarter of its pool as aura pollution at once, which is how a single-chunk rift threshold is reached by hand. The rift kit is a stack of causality collapsers and a void siphon from the creative tab, redstone gear, and stabilizers; the taint kit is a stack of bottled taint, the taint blocks from the creative tab, and — if the operator accepts it — a `/summon` of the registered seed entity as the controlled outbreak source, recorded in the `REF` entry. Start every structure from a recorded position and a screenshot so the reload steps can compare exact cells, and read every flux figure twice at least two seconds apart (the sweep cadence and the tenth-place readout are the aura lane's, [wld-01-evidence.md](wld-01-evidence.md)).

- **T1 — The first rift.** In one chunk, seed flux past three quarters of the never-drained vis figure using the crucible-destruction path (record the computed quarter and the flux line before and after), then stand still within the chunk and record whether a rift wells up at the surface over several minutes, its opening silhouette, and the "too much flux" message and research popup if the player has not seen one before (findings 2-3; the arming threshold is [wld-01-evidence.md](wld-01-evidence.md) finding 6). Bound: one chunk, one charged crucible, ten minutes of waiting.
- **T2 — The open rift: drain, growth, and decay.** With the rift up, record the chunk's flux before and after one growth window and that the rift takes the square root of twice its size of flux to gain a step; top the chunk up with further seeding and repeat; record the rift never passing the size cap and never growing once the tier is very stable (finding 5). Record the spine's terrain eating against a built wall, the two-damage bite on a test animal or a non-creative tester, and that dropped items vanish (`common/entities/EntityFluxRift.java:209-237`; finding 4). Bound: one rift, two growth windows, one wall.
- **T3 — Bursts, tiers, and the seed prime.** Use the void siphon or plain time to drive stability negative, then park a party within sixteen blocks and record each burst's manifestation over several bursts: the wisp spawn, the cloud cast, the vis-exhaust infection, the collapse self-kill, and the seed-prime burst — with its refused case marked near an existing seed rather than forced (finding 6). Record the stability refund after a burst and that a very stable rift never bursts. Bound: twenty minutes per rift, at most two rifts; mark unobserved table entries not-run rather than rerolling beyond that.
- **T4 — The deliberate collapse and its payout.** Note the rift's tier, throw a causality collapser at it, record the impact explosion and that every rift within three blocks flips; count the primordial pearls and void seeds against the square root of the recorded maximum, watch the per-tick vis/flux feeds during the unwind, and record the tier cascade on testers — poison, weakness, and warp amounts — plus the collapse effect to everyone within sixty-four blocks (findings 7, 11). Bound: one throw, one rift, and a recorded pre-collapse stability reading.
- **T5 — Taint creation and spread.** With a seed source recorded (T3's burst or the operator `/summon`), place a tainted block or let the seed spawn one near a bounded plot of grass, dirt, stone, sand, and wood, and record the conversions against the table of finding 15 — soil, rock, logs, fibre, swollen taint, crust — plus the flux line falling a hundredth per draining conversion and the first fringe seed once the plot's flux passes five. Then kill the seed and record the recede: solids dying one in ten, fibre clearing at once, and the specific reverts (rock to porous stone, soil to dirt, crust to goo, the rest to air) (findings 14-16, 20). Bound: one plot, one seed, fifteen minutes.
- **T6 — The geyser, the swarm, the goo, and the drops.** On the patch: watch a taint geyser under low flux pollute and spawn a swarm, and record the swarm's behaviour and crystal drop; break a swollen-taint block and record the crawler's roughly one-in-three spawn versus the pollution; walk the taint and record the poison rolls (findings 14, 17). Then throw a bottle of taint onto a flat clean plot and record the goo patch, the vis-exhaust on contact, the slime births and the slime-growing-on-goo interaction, and the goo's evaporation into flux or fibre (finding 18). Finally, harvest with silk and fortune tools and record what each family actually returns against the mixed silk-harvest answers and the rock's crystal tail (findings 14, 19). Bound: one patch, one bottle, one tool pass.
- **T7 — Reload probes (the acceptance slice).** (a) With a rift open, note its size, tier, and silhouette; save, quit, and re-enter; record that the rift persists, whether its silhouette re-rolls or pops, and its stability against the pre-reload reading (finding 8). (b) Mid-collapse, reload and record the payout against the recorded maximum (finding 8, finding 7's pearl roll). (c) With a patch spreading, reload and record that the blocks and the seed persist and that spread resumes within a minute; then unload the tainted chunk (travel away and back) and record whether the patch stalls or continues (finding 26). (d) Record whether a rift appears with no player nearby in the window after a reload that follows a flux spike (the stale-trigger wrinkle, finding 26). The acceptance "Rift lifecycle and taint behavior work in server and reload scenarios." fails on any count in (a), (b), or (c) that disagrees with the findings. Bound: three reloads.
- **T8 — Second client, redstone, and the device costs.** On a LAN: A drives a burst while B watches, both recording the same events; A collapses a rift and both compare visuals; B stands at the tracking edge and records the shell update cadence (finding 25). Then the devices in isolation: a redstone signal stopping a void siphon (its block state flipping, progress halting, the seed payout pausing) and a stabilizer holding a rift's tier while its own recharge pollutes the aura — read the flux line on the chunk before and after a few minutes of suppression (findings 9-10). Bound: one burst, one collapse, two device runs.
