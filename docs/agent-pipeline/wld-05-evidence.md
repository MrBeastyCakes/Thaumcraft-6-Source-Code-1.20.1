## Reference behavior (source-derived)

### A. The spawn framework

1. **Forty-three mod entities register in one method, and the roster is the lane's population count.** The registrar's entity pass calls one initializer (`Registrar.java:71`) that registers forty-three entities in class order with ascending ids and per-entity tracking settings — sixty-four blocks at an update frequency of twenty or three for the ordinary creatures, ninety-six for the giant taintacle, with a pair of colours on most entries (`common/config/ConfigEntities.java:80`, `:82-124`). Of those, the classes this lane owns are the Angry Zombie and its giant (`:106-107`), the wisp (`:108`), the firebat and spellbat (`:109-110`), the Pech (`:111`), the two crossbow turrets (`:98-99`), and the ten registered projectile classes (`:88-97`); the rest of that list belongs to the other lanes (item 10 above).

2. **Four config-gated natural-spawn additions are the mod's only world-spawn lever, and each rides an existing biome list.** The proxy's post-init calls one spawn pass (`proxies/CommonProxy.java:77`) that lives in the entity config (`common/config/ConfigEntities.java:199`), where the warm, cool, icy, and desert biome sets are collected (`:200-213`) and then, per flag: adds the Angry Zombie at weight ten to every one of those biomes that already has a monster list (`:214-220`); adds the Pech at weight ten to every magical-dictionary biome (`:221-227`); adds the firebat at weight ten in groups of one to two to every nether-dictionary biome (`:228-229`), and — only when the calendar date is the thirty-first of October — adds it again at weight five to the merged four-band overworld list (`:230-234`); and adds the wisp at weight five to the nether biomes (`:236-238`). The four flags — the angry-zombie, firebat, wisp, and Pech entries, with the dead taintacle flag between the last two — all default true (`common/config/ModConfig.java:364-366`, `:368`, `:396-398`, `:400`). Nothing else in the roster is added to a spawn list.

3. **The mod's three biomes wire their own spawn lists at construction.** The eerie biome clears its animal list down to a single bat entry and writes witch eight, enderman four, then, behind the flags, the Angry Zombie at thirty-two, the giant at eight, the wisp at three, and the eldritch guardian at one (`common/world/biomes/BiomeGenEerie.java:22-33`). The magical forest writes its own witch, enderman, and vex entries and then, behind the flags, the Pech at twenty and the wisp at twenty (`common/world/biomes/BiomeGenMagicalForest.java:44-54`). The eldritch biome clears every one of its lists and leaves a single inhabited zombie and a single eldritch guardian in the monster list (`common/world/biomes/BiomeGenEldritch.java:18-23`); the biomes themselves are registered in the same pass (`Registrar.java:107-118`), and the forests are [wld-02-evidence.md](wld-02-evidence.md)'s surface while the eldritch one is [wld-04-evidence.md](wld-04-evidence.md)'s (its finding 22).

4. **The placement rules are per-class overrides, and two of them are local caps that a port must not lose.** The Pech refuses a spawn when four or more of its kind already stand within sixteen blocks, and it requires a magical-dictionary biome — except that outside the overworld it must be one of the mod's two biomes, and inside the overworld those two biomes satisfy the check on their identity alone (`common/entities/monster/EntityPech.java:299-317`). The wisp refuses at eight or more within sixteen blocks, requires a difficulty above peaceful, and replaces the light test with its own sky-and-block-light roll that also allows a thunder discount (`common/entities/monster/EntityWisp.java:231-256`), with a per-chunk cap of two (`:268-270`). The firebat's rule is the lightest of the three — block light at most a random zero to six (`common/entities/monster/EntityFireBat.java:240-248`) — and it declares the light test valid unconditionally (`:254-256`). The Angry Zombie carries no placement rule of its own: it is a vanilla zombie for spawning purposes and inherits the vanilla zombie's (finding 13).

5. **The operator route into the roster is the summon command and the spawn eggs, not a mod command.** The registration call's trailing colour pair is the spawn-egg pair, so every creature added this way is reachable from the creative inventory's spawn-egg tab as well as from a summon by registry id (`common/config/ConfigEntities.java:82-124`, ids as registered). The mod's own command surface carries no spawn lever at all — it is the reload, research, and warp levers, registered at server start (`Thaumcraft.java:52`; the reload, research, and warp arms at `common/lib/CommandThaumcraft.java:64`, `:89`, `:114`).

### B. The champion system

6. **Every creature gets two attributes at construction, and the champion attribute is a small state machine.** On entity construction, every creature that is not an owned construct has the champion attribute registered at minus two and the tainted attribute at zero (`common/lib/events/EntityEvents.java:219-226`; the attribute itself is declared as a ranged attribute named `tc.mobmod` with a champion-modifier description at `api/ThaumcraftApiHelper.java:34`). The value reads as a state: minus two is never rolled, minus one is rolled and refused, and zero or up is the champion modifier's id — because each modifier's attribute step is its own id plus two applied additively on top of the minus-two base, so the total lands on the id (`common/entities/monster/mods/ChampionModifier.java:18-28`, `:35`; the application `common/lib/utils/EntityUtils.java:356-358`). That is why later code tests the value against exact ids as well as against zero (`common/lib/events/EntityEvents.java:182`, `:196`, `:204`, `:231`).

7. **The roll happens once per monster, on join, on the server, and it is a subtraction race against a random hundred.** A joining mob whose champion value sits below minus one draws a random hundred and then subtracts: two more on easy difficulty or with champions switched off; two less on hard while they are switched on; three less in the configured Outer Lands dimension; two or one less in spooky, nether, or end biomes; and ten or three less at what the code calls a dangerous location — a private probe that answers false for every input in this tree, so the largest intended adjustment is inert (`common/lib/events/EntityEvents.java:275-294`, `:320-321`; the dimension id is the config default minus forty-two, `common/config/ModConfig.java:332`, `:375`). Then the whitelist decides: only a class the champion whitelist matches may ever become a champion, each matching entry contributes its stored level minus one, floored at zero, which is then subtracted from the running total — so levels zero and one are no-ops and only level two or up lowers it), and with champions switched off every non-boss entry is skipped but the whitelist match still stands (`common/lib/events/EntityEvents.java:295-306`). The champion is granted when the mob is whitelisted, the total is at or below zero, and its base maximum health is at least ten (`:307-309`); otherwise the refusal is recorded by applying the plus-one normal step, which parks the value at minus one so the roll never runs again (`:310-314`; the step itself `common/entities/monster/mods/ChampionModifier.java:36`). The whitelist is the mod's own nine entries, sent to itself through the inter-mod channel in post-init: five vanilla monsters at level zero, the witch at one, the eldritch crab at zero, the taintacle at two, and the inhabited zombie at three (`common/config/ConfigEntities.java:239-247`), parsed by the proxy's channel handler into a class-keyed map (`proxies/CommonProxy.java:105`, `:155-167`). The API documents the contract as a level roughly equal to the n of a one-in-n-hundred chance and warns that levels below about minus two produce effectively nothing (`api/ThaumcraftApi.java:540-549`); the sample line in that documentation names a wisp entry as an example, and no wisp entry is sent in this tree (`:551`), so wisp champions exist only if a third-party mod sends one.

8. **What a champion becomes: one random modifier out of fourteen, a flat health bonus, a multiplicative damage bonus, and a name.** The grant picks a random id from the fourteen-entry table, forces an id of zero for creepers, applies that modifier's attribute step, and then, for everything except a boss, adds a flat hundred bonus health and a multiplicative damage step of two — a tripled damage figure before any other multiplicative step — heals it twenty-five, and renames it to the modifier's localized name plus its own (`common/lib/utils/EntityUtils.java:343-368`; the multipliers declared at `:437-438`). Bosses rename through their own generator instead and skip the health and damage bonuses (`:369-371`). The per-id extras: the bold id adds a one-point-three speed multiplier, the mighty id adds its own multiplicative damage step of two, and the warded id grants an absorption pool of half the base maximum health (`:375-393`); the stat steps and their operations are declared with the rest of the modifiers at `:439-440`. The tainted id gets its own path — a conversion that raises a low-health living thing onto the attribute (finding 9). The forced call from the giant taintacle passes the persistence flag, so a championed giant never despawns (`common/entities/monster/boss/EntityTaintacleGiant.java:50-53`; the flag's effect `common/lib/utils/EntityUtils.java:372-374`).

9. **The fourteen modifiers sort into four effect families, dispatched from three hooks, and one family has a shared tell.** Each entry declares a type that decides where its effect runs (`common/entities/monster/mods/ChampionModifier.java:35`): negative one is stat-only (bold, mighty), zero runs every living tick (warded, undying, tainted), one rewrites the damage the mob deals (grim, warp, fiery, sickly, venomous, vampiric), and two rewrites the damage the mob takes (spined, armored, infested). The tick hook calls the type-zero effect each tick and, in a defensive branch, kills a mob whose id is out of range (`common/lib/events/EntityEvents.java:90-109`). The hurt hook runs the type-two family against the attacking living entity, then the attacker-side type-one family, and — for a warded mob or any eldritch mob with absorption left — also fires a shield-hit effect packet and a charge sound to everyone within thirty-two blocks (`:177-208`, the packet and sound at `:179-195`). The shapes inside the effects: the warded pool refills a point per twenty-five ticks while the mob is not in hurt-resistance time, up to half its base health (`common/entities/monster/mods/ChampionModWarded.java:15-24`); spined answers a hit that is not already thorns with one to three points of thorns damage and a thorns sound (`common/entities/monster/mods/ChampionModSpined.java:16-21`); grim withers its target on two of five hits (`common/entities/monster/mods/ChampionModGrim.java:16-21`); and the tainted id does not set a name at all in its own conversion path (`common/lib/utils/EntityUtils.java:396-434`). **The two dead spots a port must not copy:** the tainted modifier's name key is absent from the English language file while the other thirteen are present (`assets/thaumcraft/lang/en_us.lang:545-557`; the key does exist in the Korean file, `assets/thaumcraft/lang/ko_kr.lang:556`), so a champion that rolls that id shows a raw key as its name prefix; and the dangerous-location probe of finding 7 is a stub, which removes the ten-point adjustment from every roll.

### C. The loot-bag machinery

10. **Champions drop experience and a loot bag, and the bag is a draw, not an item.** The kill hook pays champions — a recently-hit monster that is not a boss, killed by something other than a fake player, with a champion value at or above zero and not the tainted id — five to seven points of experience in orbs and appends a loot bag whose damage value is the lesser of two and a fifth of a random zero-to-eight plus the looting level (`common/lib/events/EntityEvents.java:228-240`). The bag stacks to sixteen, carries a localized tooltip, reports its own rarity per damage value, and on use draws eight to twelve times from the rarity switch, spawning each result at the player, playing the coin sound, and shrinking itself (`common/items/curios/ItemLootBag.java:23-26`, `:28-46`, `:48-60`; registered among the curios at `common/config/ConfigItems.java:177`).

11. **The rarity switch is a small gear lottery laid over three weighted item lists.** For a rarity above zero, a two-and-a-half-percent-per-rarity chance short-circuits into the gear generator, and a failure falls back to a direct weighted draw — the switch takes the common list by default and the uncommon or rare one by damage value (`common/lib/utils/Utils.java:305-328`, the three lists `api/internal/WeightedRandomLoot.java:20-22`); a plain book drawn this way comes out enchanted at a level that scales with rarity (`common/lib/utils/Utils.java:329-331`). The gear generator builds its own quality ladder — a base roll of zero to one and five successive incremental rolls that each add one — at a fifth, fifteen hundredths, a tenth, and two more at ninety-five thousandths — maps the result onto a five-slot item table, damages the piece by up to a sixth of its durability, and adds an enchantment at a one-in-four-per-rarity rate with the same scaling (`:335-362`, the table from `:364`).

12. **The lists the bags draw are the same lists the world's crates draw, and the fills are public API.** The filler is the mod-facing API entry (`api/ThaumcraftApi.java:417-425`), and the mod's own fills cover all three tiers with gold nuggets dominant (2500, 2250, 2000), primordial pearls seeded into every tier, a nether star and a top-value pearl at weight one in the rare tier, a rare bauble entry, diamonds and emeralds weighted toward the higher tiers, enchanted books across the board, and potions for every potion type (`common/config/ModConfig.java:62-111`, the star at `:82`, the top pearl at `:81`, the rare bauble at `:93`); the call site is the proxy's post-init beside the spawn additions (`proxies/CommonProxy.java:80`). The crate and urn blocks that draw the same lists, and the tier-jitter arithmetic that fills them, are [wld-04-evidence.md](wld-04-evidence.md) findings 9 and 28 — this lane owns the bag side and the switch only.

### D. The ordinary monster — the Angry Zombie and its giant

13. **The mod's ordinary monster is a zombie subclass whose identity is three attribute writes, one task, one armor point, and one drop roll.** It extends the vanilla zombie class (`common/entities/monster/EntityBrainyZombie.java:13`), so its spawn placement, its equipment, its loot table, and its child-growth behavior are inherited; its own writes are twenty-five health, five damage, and a reinforcement chance of zero (`:15-20`), a hurt-by-target task inserted at priority one (`:22-25`), one armor point over the inherited total (`:27-29`), and a brain drop on a roll of a random zero-to-nine minus the looting level at or below four — half the time at no looting, improving per level — added before the vanilla loot call runs (`:31-36`). Its egg colours and name tag are the registration's (`common/config/ConfigEntities.java:106`; `assets/thaumcraft/lang/en_us.lang:508`), and its scan keys are the research lane's ([rsr-01-evidence.md](rsr-01-evidence.md)).

14. **The giant variant — the Furious Zombie of the language file (`assets/thaumcraft/lang/en_us.lang:507`) — is the same zombie growing as it is hit, and its flesh drop sits on a branch the loot table skips.** It adds a leap task at priority two, fifteen experience, sixty health, and seven damage, with a synced anger value that rises a tenth per hit to a cap of two and decays only above one (`common/entities/monster/EntityGiantBrainyZombie.java:14`, `:18-22`, `:42-53`, `:55-58`, `:60-65`). While it is above one, its box is re-derived from the anger — width six tenths plus six tenths per point, height 1.95 plus 1.95 per point, so at the anger cap of two the box triples both, 1.8 by 5.85 against the base 0.6 by 1.95 — and its attack damage is continuously set from the anger as seven plus five per point above the first, reaching twelve at the cap (`:24-32`; its eye height scales the same way at `:34-40`). Its flesh drop is a pair of six-pass coin-flip loops over rotten flesh (`:67-78`) — but it inherits the vanilla zombie's loot table, and the base class chooses a loot table over the flesh-drop path when one is present, so that override reads as a dead branch in this tree and the giant's meat comes from the table; the T1 kill records which flesh actually appears.

15. **The brain has two faucets, and only one of them is the mod's monster.** The brain item is its own class (`common/items/consumables/ItemZombieBrain.java`, registered `common/config/ConfigItems.java:202`), and besides the Angry Zombie's own roll (finding 13) a global drop hook gives a brain to any recently-hit zombie that is not a brainy one at a random zero-to-nine minus the looting level below one — one kill in ten, improving per looting level (`common/lib/events/EntityEvents.java:241-243`). Both faucets route to the same item; its consumption and the jar-brain consumer are the crafting and alchemy lanes' surfaces, and its scan page is the research lane's.

### E. The ambient flyer — the wisp, with the firebat beside it

16. **The wisp is a floating aspect elemental whose synced aspect tag is its whole identity.** It extends the vanilla flying base with the hostile-mob marker, which means it carries no navigator at all and steers by direct motion integration: it picks a random flight target in a small box around itself, re-picks it every thirtieth tick or when close, and bleeds vertical motion every tick (`common/entities/monster/EntityWisp.java:33`, `:98`, `:128-144`). Twenty-two health and three damage with five experience on a 0.9-block body (`:55-60`, `:45-53`), it takes no air damage and never triggers walking (`:62-68`). ### F. The Pech

Taming runs the other way: ender pearls are hard-coded as valued at fifteen (`common/config/ConfigEntities.java:125`), any other item whose aspect tags carry more than one desire counts as valued, and its value is half its desire capped at thirty-two (`common/entities/monster/EntityPech.java:650-674`); picking one up rolls a ten-sided die against that value and, on a hit, tames the Pech with the heart burst sent to clients (`:625-629`; the pickup path `:589-635`). Its despawn rule reads only its pack: a Pech carrying five or more stacks refuses to despawn — wild or tamed alike — while one carrying fewer despawns freely, and taming grants no persistence (the tree's only persistence grant is the champion path, `common/lib/utils/EntityUtils.java:373`) (`common/entities/monster/EntityPech.java:385-401`); it cannot be leashed (`:403-405`), and the spawn side of the population is the four-within-sixteen-blocks cap of finding 4.

The three trade lists are built at init as value-and-item pairs — the forager list, the mage list, and the arcane list (`common/config/ConfigEntities.java:126-155`, `:156-178`, `:179-196`) — the greatwood sapling in the forager list is the second sapling faucet [wld-02-evidence.md](wld-02-evidence.md) finding 19 records (`:144`), and the arcane list's eldritch eye is the only faucet [wld-04-evidence.md](wld-04-evidence.md) finding 10 records (`:189`). Closing the container clears the trading flag and drops whatever the five slots still hold, tagging each item with the thrower string the Pech's own collection task refuses — so it does not immediately re-absorb its own payment (`common/container/ContainerPech.java:179-193`, the tag at `:188`; the refusal `common/entities/ai/pech/AIPechItemEntityGoto.java:43-48`), and the container itself answers that it can only be used while the Pech is tamed (`common/container/ContainerPech.java:142-144`).

21. **Its collection AI and its own loot.** The collect task scans for item entities within sixteen blocks that the Pech's pickup test accepts — any item while one of its nine carry slots is empty, and anything that stacks with what it already carries otherwise — paths to the nearest at one and a half times its movement speed, and absorbs at about a block and a quarter, storing a non-valued item in a free slot while a valued one is consumed by the taming roll (`common/entities/ai/pech/AIPechItemEntityGoto.java:26`, `:31-58`, `:68-71`, `:73-106`; the pickup test `common/entities/monster/EntityPech.java:568-587`, the absorb path `:589-635`). ### I. Config gates, persistence, and the client-visible state

29. **The lane's gates are four spawn flags, one champion flag, and a dimension id — nothing else is configurable.** The four spawn flags gate the natural additions and the two biome lists (findings 2-3; `common/config/ModConfig.java:364-368`, `:396-400`); `allowChampionMobs` (default true) gates the roll's whitelist path, both directly in the difficulty adjustment and indirectly by skipping non-boss whitelist entries (finding 7; `:371`, `:402`, `common/lib/events/EntityEvents.java:279-283`, `:300-302`); the Outer Lands dimension id is read by the champion roll and defaults to minus forty-two (finding 7; `common/config/ModConfig.java:332`, `:375`); `wussMode` (default false) is a research and warp gate and never touches this lane (`:413`, `:428`); and the surviving shared surfaces are the magical-forest biome weight and the third-party capture blacklist the constructs and flyers sit on (`:220-235`, the construct entry at `:222`; [wld-02-evidence.md](wld-02-evidence.md)). 30. **What is synced, what is saved, and what a reload must re-derive.** Synced and read by clients: the wisp's aspect tag (`common/entities/monster/EntityWisp.java:258-266`), the giant's anger (`common/entities/monster/EntityGiantBrainyZombie.java:42-53`), the firebat's hanging bit (`common/entities/monster/EntityFireBat.java:228-238`), the Pech's type, anger, tamed bit, and its nine carried stacks (`common/entities/monster/EntityPech.java:365-383`), the owned construct's owner id and flag byte (`common/entities/construct/EntityOwnedConstruct.java:108-139`), the advanced turret's four filter bits (`common/entities/construct/EntityTurretCrossbowAdvanced.java:187-197`), and the Pech's trading freeze, which is a session flag cleared when the container closes (`common/container/ContainerPech.java:33`, `:179-181`). ## Rank-1 capture checklist

Reference setup for every step: the clean 1.12.2 profile whose only mods are the BETA26 jar and Baubles (`REF-0002` and the "Environment feasibility" section of [reference-catalog.md](reference-catalog.md)); a fresh creative world with its seed recorded in a new `REF` entry before the session. Use `/summon` and `/give` freely for controlled tests and record every use and every config edit in the `REF` entry, since a config flip is a restart, not a live action; the only other levers the mod itself offers are the research and warp commands, which do not touch this lane (`common/lib/CommandThaumcraft.java:64`, `:89`, `:114`). Every step below is bounded; stop at the bound even if the sample looks thin.

- **T1 — The four gated spawns and their homes.** Find and record one of each: an Angry Zombie in any monster-spawning overworld biome, a Pech and a wisp in the magical forest or the eerie biome, and a firebat in the nether. For each, record what spawned, then walk the caps: four Pechs within sixteen blocks and no fifth, eight wisps within sixteen and no ninth, two wisps per chunk, and the firebat's ceiling-hanging, its drop on approach, and its re-hang (findings 2-4; bounded to one encounter per creature and one cap test each). Then switch one spawn flag off in the config, restart, and record that its creature no longer appears naturally while the biome list entries behave as the constructor built them (finding 2-3; bounded to one flag flip). Kill one giant zombie at full anger and record which flesh drops appear, against finding 14's dead-branch prediction.
- **T2 — The champion roll through a real fight.** On hard difficulty in an ordinary dark biome, clear a bounded sweep of naturally spawned vanilla monsters and record whether any champion appears, with its name prefix and its observed effect against finding 9's four families (bound the sweep by kills, not by time). Then set `allowChampionMobs` false, restart, repeat a bounded sweep and record that no natural champion appears, and in the same session summon the giant taintacle and record that it still arrives championed with a boss name (findings 7-9; bounded to one sweep per setting, one flag flip, one summon). Finally kill a champion and record the experience burst and the bag against finding 10.
- **T3 — The loot bag's distribution.** With a bounded sample — say twenty bags split across the three tiers, earned from T2 or given by the operator with the assistance recorded — open each and record the draw count, the tier bias, what the gear branch produces including durability and enchantments, and the coin sound. Record whether a nether star or a top-value pearl appears at all in the sample, and compare the feel against the weights in finding 12's citation; record the sample size honestly, since one bag proves nothing about a one-in-three-thousand-weight entry (findings 10-12; bounded to twenty bags).
- **T4 — The Pech, wild to tamed to trading.** Summon a wild Pech, throw ender pearls at it, and record the taming attempts, the heart burst, and its picking up anything it values; throw it a worthless item and record it being absorbed into a free carry slot — a refusal is not expected, since only valued items feed the taming roll. Punch it and record its anger, the charge cycle, and whether a second summoned nearby Pech also turns hostile — then let the anger lapse and re-tame. Trade once per type if a wand- or bow-armed one can be obtained, recording the payout contents, the value jitter's visible spread over several offers, the untame on a good offer, and the drop on close with the Pech refusing to re-collect its payment (findings 18-21; bounded to two Pechs, one taming, three trade offers). Record the despawn behavior against finding 19's carrier rule, noting a summoned mob may be persistent regardless.
- **T5 — The two turrets.** Place one of each turret placer variant as one player, then have a second player try to interact and record the not-owned line; record the dispenser reload with a dispenser of arrows placed underneath, the arrow consumption per shot, and the fire cadence from a bounded count of shots. Toggle each advanced filter bit off in turn and record that its class stops being attacked, and record the PvP gate on a dedicated server with PvP disabled (a player target is dropped). On the basic turret, park it on a powered activator rail and record that it stops firing; sneak-interact as the owner and record the ammo and the un-damaged placer coming back (findings 23-24; bounded to one of each turret, one dispenser, one rail, one PvP setting).
- **T6 — The projectiles.** Throw alumentum at a wall and record the blast and the fire; fire the grapple and record the pull, the release on sneak, and that a second throw kills the first; have the cult-side bosses and the eldritch construct and guardian fire golem orbs and eldritch orbs at a recording player and record the homing, the red-versus-plain damage difference if raw numbers are visible, and the weakness on the eldritch splash. Then attempt to summon the homing shard and the rift blast by id and record that no such id resolves — finding 26 predicts the attempt fails, and a resolution would overrule the source read and should be filed back as a correction (findings 26-28; bounded to one of each projectile, one of each orb type).
- **T7 — Reload, unload, and a second client.** With a champion mid-fight, a tamed Pech mid-trade, a giant zombie above anger one, a hanging firebat, a wisp, both turrets with ammo, and an anchored grapple live, save and reload; then walk away until the chunk unloads and return, and record for each what survived — the champion's name and bonuses, the anger and size, the hang, the ammo, the turret filter, the construct validation, the Pech's trade session and carried pack — against finding 30. Second client on a LAN: compare the wisp's colour, the champion's name prefix, the shield-hit effect from finding 9's packet, and the turret filter toggles across both clients (bounded to one reload, one unload, one co-op pass).
- **T8 — The dead paths and the negative checks.** This note's source-side negatives are: the homing shard and the rift blast having neither a registration nor a constructor anywhere (finding 26), the tainted name key missing from the English language file while present in the Korean one (finding 9), the dangerous-location probe answering false for every position (finding 7), and the giant's flesh-drop override sitting behind the zombie loot table (finding 14). T8 records each as an attempt: attempt both dead-projectile summons and record the failure; force a champion onto the tainted id if the roll can be coaxed and record the rendered name; walk a champion into a place that ought to be dangerous and record that nothing changes; kill a giant and record which flesh drops. Any outcome that contradicts the source notes above is the point of the step, not a failure of it (bounded to one attempt per item).
