## Target behaviors to capture

From the evidence-index queue row for `ALC-00` (Crucible recipe matching, input validation, aspect costs, and output economy) and the workboard acceptance line ("Valid Crucible recipes consume exact inputs and aspects and produce expected outputs; invalid inputs are rejected without consumption"):

1. **Recipe model** — the crucible recipe type, its catalyst form, its required aspect list and amounts, and its output stack and count.
2. **Matching semantics** — which inputs are consulted, how strict the comparison is, what happens when several recipes match, and what a near-miss does.
3. **Input validation** — how items are accepted (thrown or used), the water and heat conditions, and what is refused.
4. **Aspect storage and cost** — where the crucible keeps Essentia, how a dissolve tops it up, and exactly what a successful craft removes.
5. **Output economy** — what is produced, where it appears, and what failures, overfill, and destruction cost.
6. **Out of scope** — the smelter and item-aspect attribution (`ALC-01`), jars, alembics, and tubes (`ALC-02`), and the Thaumatorium (`ALC-04`) are cross-referenced only.

## Reference behavior (source-derived)

### A. The recipe type and its registry

1. **A crucible recipe is a research key, a catalyst ingredient, a required aspect list, and an output stack.** The type holds those four fields plus a name and an integer hash (`api/crafting/CrucibleRecipe.java:10-17`); the constructor stores them, converts the catalyst argument into an ingredient through the API helper, throws when that conversion yields nothing, and then computes the hash (`:20-33`).
2. **The catalyst is any ingredient form, not just an item.** The helper returns a supplied ingredient unchanged, wraps an NBT-bearing stack in the mod's NBT-aware ingredient, and otherwise defers to the Forge crafting-helper conversion (`api/ThaumcraftApiHelper.java:346-353`) — which is where the ore-dictionary string form resolves. The shipped registry uses both: ore-dictionary names such as `nuggetQuartz` (`common/config/ConfigRecipes.java:131`), `ingotIron` (`:141-142`) and `oreIron` (`:148`), and concrete stacks including a wildcard-damage item (`:140`) and ones carrying an explicit meta value (`:552`). The NBT variant compares item, damage value, and relaxed NBT equality (`api/crafting/IngredientNBTTC.java:19-26`).
3. **The requirement and the output are plain data.** A requirement is an aspect list with per-aspect amounts — from one unit (hedge tallow, `common/config/ConfigRecipes.java:144`) up to multi-aspect lists such as bath salts at 40 each of four aspects (`:165`) — and the output is an ordinary stack carrying its own count, such as two gunpowder from one (`:549`) or one nitor (`:134`); the vis-crystal recipes produce a crystal essence charged with the aspect through the crystal builder (`:131` with `api/ThaumcraftApiHelper.java:314-319`).
4. **Recipes live in one shared catalog and are registered in two passes.** `addCrucibleRecipe` puts the recipe into the catalog keyed by resource location (`api/ThaumcraftApi.java:219-221`, `:85-87`; the map itself is `api/internal/CommonInternals.java:24`), and the mod's config populates it: one crystal recipe per registered aspect in registration order (`common/config/ConfigRecipes.java:128-132`), then the named recipes (`:134`, `:140-146`, `:163-166`), a config-gated metal-purification family (`:147-161`), and golem seals (`:168-179`); a later pass adds recipes whose requirement lists are derived from item aspects (`:548-556`). The catalog is a plain hash map, so iteration order is not registration order (`api/ThaumcraftApi.java:85-87`). The crucible block itself is obtained through a dust trigger on a vanilla cauldron under `UNLOCKALCHEMY@1` (`common/config/ConfigRecipes.java:75`), and the same recipe type also backs the Thaumatorium, which resolves recipes by hash through the catalog (`api/ThaumcraftApi.java:241-247`) and counts the crucible among its multiblock parts (`common/config/ConfigRecipes.java:113-116`); that device is `ALC-04` scope.

### B. Matching semantics

5. **One method performs all crucible matching, and it picks the recipe with the largest requirement.** It walks every catalog entry, keeps those whose research gate and match test pass against a single-item copy of the candidate stack, and returns the one with the greatest total required aspect amount, replacing the incumbent only on a strictly greater value (`common/lib/crafting/ThaumcraftCraftingManager.java:54-74`, with the count normalization at `:60-61` and the comparison at `:65-70`). Two consequences fall out of that shape: ties and equal-size requirements resolve to whichever entry the hash-ordered catalog reaches first, and a null player short-circuits every candidate, so no recipe can match without a player (`:62`).
6. **The research gate is the strict knowledge test, with staged and conjunction keys in use.** The plain form of a key demands completed research; an `@`-suffixed form accepts in-progress research at the given stage; `&&` and `||` forms combine entries (`api/capabilities/ThaumcraftCapabilities.java:70-87`). The shipped crucible recipes use plain keys (`ALUMENTUM`, `common/config/ConfigRecipes.java:140`), staged keys (`METALLURGY@1` and `@2`, `:141-142`; `UNLOCKALCHEMY@3`, `:134`), and conjunctions (`SEALCOLLECT&&MINDBIOTHAUMIC`, `:168`). Stage and prerequisite semantics themselves belong to the research notes and are not derived here.
7. **Matching is an at-least test per required aspect over the stored pool.** A candidate matches when the catalyst ingredient accepts the candidate stack and, for every aspect in the requirement, the stored amount is at least the required amount (`api/crafting/CrucibleRecipe.java:50-57`); a null pool fails outright (`:52`), extra stored aspects do not block, larger stored amounts do not block, and nothing in the test depends on the order in which aspects were collected (`:53-55`). There is also a catalyst-only test that the crucible never calls (`:59-61`).
8. **Two recipes can share one catalyst, and the largest-requirement rule decides.** The nitor recipe and the hedge-glowstone recipe both use the catalyst name `dustGlowstone` (`common/config/ConfigRecipes.java:134`, `:551`) with different requirement sizes, so with both known and both affordable the larger requirement wins (item 5). Whether the shipped release resolves these two the same way is **unverified — needs rank-1 observation** (`REF-0001` records that sources were not compared against the compiled classes).
9. **Success subtracts exactly the requirement and leaves the surplus.** Removing a match copies the stored pool, subtracts each required amount, and returns the copy (`api/crafting/CrucibleRecipe.java:63-71`); the subtract-and-delete-at-zero semantics of that list are the ones described in [fnd-04-evidence.md](fnd-04-evidence.md) item 9, so a matched craft can never drive a stored amount negative, deletes aspects it consumes to zero, and returns everything the recipe did not ask for.

### C. Input validation: throwing, using, water, and heat

10. **There are two entry paths and one hard threshold pair.** An item entity that touches the block is smelted when the tile is hotter than 150 and its tank is not empty, and any other colliding entity is ignored; a living entity that collides is instead hurt for a single point of damage on a throttled cadence, with the throttle counter living on the shared block instance rather than per crucible (`common/blocks/crafting/BlockCrucible.java:51-67`). An item that touches a cold or dry crucible is simply not processed — nothing is consumed and no aspect is added (`:54`, `:57-67`).
11. **Right-clicking the top face submits one unit of the held item.** With a hand item that is not a caster, from a non-sneaking player, on the top face, the block smelts a single-item copy and removes one unit from the inventory only when the call reports the item gone; otherwise the interaction is handed to the parent block and nothing is consumed (`common/blocks/crafting/BlockCrucible.java:125-136`). Sneak right-click with an empty hand is the manual empty command (`:137-144`), and item entities spawned by the crucible's own output are excluded from the smelt path (`:54`, item 21).
12. **Heat is generated by what sits below the crucible and only while it has water.** The tile raises heat by one per tick up to 200 while the block directly below is lava, fire, a nitor block, or magma and the tank holds fluid, and lowers it by one per tick otherwise, including when the tank is empty (`common/tiles/crafting/TileCrucible.java:85-110`); the nitor test consults the dye-keyed nitor block map (`api/blocks/BlocksTC.java:108`). The working threshold of 151 is what the two entry paths test, and the tile forces a sync both when heat crosses it and when it falls back through it (`common/tiles/crafting/TileCrucible.java:94-97`, `:102-105`), so the difference between 150 and 151 is observable in the synced value but no more.
13. **Water is a 1000-unit tank that starts empty and is filled from held fluid containers.** The tile builds a water tank of capacity 1000 with no initial content and exposes it as a fluid-handler capability (`common/tiles/crafting/TileCrucible.java:53`, `:63`, `:340-350`); the block fills it only from a held item that contains water, only when the tank is not already full, and plays a bottle-fill sound on success (`common/blocks/crafting/BlockCrucible.java:103-124`). Each successful craft drains 50 units (`common/tiles/crafting/TileCrucible.java:185`), so a full tank covers twenty crafts, and the craft branch only requires the tank to be non-empty rather than to hold the drain amount (`:179`).
14. **There is no input whitelist: an item either crafts or melts.** Every unit that reaches the tile is tested against the recipe set and, failing that, dissolved into aspects when its computed aspect list is non-empty; an item with no computed aspects leaves the loop untouched, is returned unchanged, and the block then declines to consume it (`common/tiles/crafting/TileCrucible.java:177-204`, `:215-219`; `common/blocks/crafting/BlockCrucible.java:131-134`). The practical consequence for parity is that a wrong or premature catalyst is destroyed and its aspects join the pool instead of triggering a rejection, and a recipe that matches while the tank is dry falls to the same dissolve path (`common/tiles/crafting/TileCrucible.java:179`).
15. **Thrown items are attributed to the player who dropped them, and that attribution gates crafting.** The drop event stamps the thrower name onto the item entity's data (`common/lib/events/PlayerEvents.java:338-341`); the entity-side smelt reads it back, resolves the player by name, and delegates (`common/tiles/crafting/TileCrucible.java:222-234` with `:176`). An item that was not dropped by a player, or whose thrower is no longer online, therefore has no player at matching time and can only dissolve (item 5). On completion the entity is removed, and otherwise its stack count is rewritten to the remainder (`:227-233`).
16. **A single pass processes only part of a stack.** The loop advances an index while decrementing the remaining count on every success, so a fresh stack of N is finished after about half of its units in one call, with the remainder left on the item for the next collision tick (`common/tiles/crafting/TileCrucible.java:175-204`, `:215-219`). In play this reads as a throttled dissolve rather than a loss, but the exact rate is **unverified — needs rank-1 observation**.

### D. Aspect storage and consumption

17. **The crucible's Essentia pool is a single aspect list that dissolved items add into.** The tile owns one list, and the dissolve branch merges the item's computed aspects into it, amount by amount, on the same object (`common/tiles/crafting/TileCrucible.java:45`, `:192-201`); those amounts are the computed item aspects described in [fnd-04-evidence.md](fnd-04-evidence.md) items 13-16, so what actually accrues is that note's derived data rather than a table this note restates.
18. **The tile declares the aspect-container interface but is inert as a container and is not a source.** It reports the live pool from the getter while every mutator is deliberately dead: the add returns zero, both takes fail, the contain tests answer false, the contains-count is zero, and only the accept test answers true (`common/tiles/crafting/TileCrucible.java:42`, `:305-338`). It does not implement the source variant of that interface (`api/aspects/IAspectSource.java:13-19`), so the Essentia transport that drains sources never drains a crucible (the contract is described in [fnd-04-evidence.md](fnd-04-evidence.md) items 20-21). The live pool is, however, what the goggles overlay draws when it is pointed at a block with a non-empty container (`client/lib/events/RenderEventHandler.java:395-400`), which makes the pool directly observable in play.
19. **Capacity is a total-amount cap of 500, and the declared field is unused.** The tile declares a maximum-tags field of 500 that nothing in the tree reads (`common/tiles/crafting/TileCrucible.java:46`); the enforced number is the pooled total, checked on the server every tick, and the client mirrors the same threshold for its froth effect and for the fluid-height math (`:111-113`, `:130-137`, `:236-246`). Heat, tank, and the pool all travel in the tile's sync data (`:67-83`), and the comparator output is derived from the same pooled total as a signal from 1 upward while the pool is not empty (`common/blocks/crafting/BlockCrucible.java:150-163`).
20. **A successful craft deducts aspects first, then water, then emits the output.** In the craft branch the tile copies the recipe output, fires the player crafting event with a fake inventory wrapping the input, replaces the pool with the surplus left by the removal, drains 50 units of water, and only then ejects the result (`common/tiles/crafting/TileCrucible.java:179-190`). The pool deduction and the water drain are therefore both completed before the output exists, and a failure after that point would already have paid both.

### E. Output economy and failure costs

21. **The output is emitted as a special item entity thrown upward from the block.** The result is ejected at the block center just above the rim with a small upward velocity, split into several entities when its count exceeds a maximum stack size, and spawned as the mod's special item type (`common/tiles/crafting/TileCrucible.java:155-170`); that type is excluded from the crucible smelt path, so a produced item cannot be re-smelted by the crucible it came from (`common/blocks/crafting/BlockCrucible.java:54`), and it refuses explosion damage (`common/entities/EntitySpecialItem.java:36-38`).
22. **A failed attempt costs only the item.** When nothing matches, the unit is consumed and its whole computed aspect list joins the pool (`common/tiles/crafting/TileCrucible.java:192-200`); no aspects are removed, no water is drained, and no output is created, so a failed attempt never partially pays the recipe (`:179-190`). If the pool is over its cap at that moment, the addition itself is not refused: the every-tick cap check removes one unit at a time as pollution until the total no longer exceeds the cap (item 23).
23. **Overfill and idling both bleed the pool off as pollution.** Each spill event removes one unit of a randomly chosen aspect and adds aura pollution — one unit of pollution for flux and a quarter per unit otherwise (`common/tiles/crafting/TileCrucible.java:248-256`). Two triggers run it: the every-tick cap check while the pooled total exceeds 500 (`:111-113`), and a timer spill once an internal counter reaches 100, where that counter is reset to -250 by a craft and -150 by a dissolve (`:114-117`, `:189`, `:200`) and starts at -100 when the tile is constructed (`:59`) — so the interval is not fixed: the spill fires 100 update ticks after a spill (which resets the counter to zero), 350 ticks after a craft, and 250 ticks after a dissolve.
24. **Destroying or emptying a charged crucible is a pollution event, not a refund.** Breaking the block calls the spill-remnants path (`common/blocks/crafting/BlockCrucible.java:92-99`), as does a sneak right-click with an empty hand (`:137-144`); that path clears the water and the pool and adds pollution equal to a quarter of the pooled total, plus three quarters of any stored flux on top of it (`common/tiles/crafting/TileCrucible.java:258-272`). Nothing stored in a crucible is recoverable, and the aura cost of clearing one is proportional to what it held.
25. **The client-visible hooks are block events and ambient effects.** A dissolve syncs the tile and fires block event 2 with a parameter, a craft fires event 99, and the client answers 99 with an effect burst and a spill sound and answers 2 with a spill sound plus boil effects (`common/tiles/crafting/TileCrucible.java:205-213`, `:274-298`); above the working heat the client also draws froth and bubbling tied to the pool (`:127-153`) and plays ambient pops when hot and wet (`common/blocks/crafting/BlockCrucible.java:166-173`). These are the observables a rank-1 pass can watch while the checklist below records counts.

## Rank-1 capture checklist

Reference setup for every step: the clean 1.12.2 profile whose only mods are the BETA26 jar and Baubles (`REF-0002` and the "Environment feasibility" section of [reference-catalog.md](reference-catalog.md)); record the profile, world seed, and game mode in a new `REF` entry before starting. The established session world is **creative**, which supplies every item, block, and bucket these steps need without operator commands; only the inventory-count assertions are survival claims and those are noted per step. A crucible is obtainable without commands from the mod's creative tab (the dust-trigger route on a vanilla cauldron needs `UNLOCKALCHEMY@1` to be known already, so it cannot fire for a fresh player) (item 4); grant research with the operator command only between measurements, never during one.

- **A1 — One end-to-end craft.** With lava under the crucible, a filled tank, and heat past the threshold, dissolve the aspects a known recipe requires, then submit its catalyst and record: the output item appearing above the block, the pooled total before and after, and the water left in the tank. Compare the observed deduction against the recipe's own requirement list (items 3, 9, 20, 21).
- **A2 — Surplus and exactness.** Repeat A1 with one required aspect deliberately over-supplied and one unrelated aspect present, and record that the surplus and the unrelated aspect both remain and that only the required amounts were deducted (item 9).
- **A3 — Near-match and wrong catalyst.** Submit an item whose recipe the player cannot yet meet — either an unknown research key or a pool short one aspect — and confirm the item melts into aspects, that nothing is produced, and that no water is consumed; record whether anything on screen distinguishes this from a successful submission, since the source shows no rejection path (items 5-7, 14).
- **A4 — Heat gate.** With the tank full but the block below not a heat source, confirm a valid submission does nothing; place lava below and record how long heat needs before submissions start working and that removing the source lets it fall back below the threshold (items 10, 12).
- **A5 — Water gate and consumption rate.** With a valid pool and a valid catalyst but a dry tank, confirm nothing is crafted and the item melts instead; then fill the tank from a bucket and count the crafts one tank supports — the source predicts twenty at 50 units each, with the last craft working from a partly-drained tank (items 13, 14).
- **A6 — Thrown versus used.** Compare submitting a single catalyst by right-clicking the top face against dropping a whole stack of the same item onto the crucible: record the inventory count drop for the first, the dissolve rate for the second, and whether the stack disappears fully or lingers (items 11, 15, 16). The inventory-count half is the survival-relevant observation.
- **A7 — Cap and spill.** Drive the pooled total past 500 with cheap items and record the spill behaviour (a random aspect lost, aura pollution) plus the comparator signal rising while the pool is non-empty; then leave a charged crucible idle and record whether the periodic spill is noticeable (items 19, 23).
- **A8 — Destruction cost and transport check.** Read the pool with goggles, break the crucible, and record that nothing is returned and how the aura and flux change; then park an alembic or a tube against a charged crucible and confirm the transport does not drain it (items 18, 24). The transport half is the `ALC-02` cross-check.
- **A9 — Multiplayer attribution.** With two clients, have one player throw a catalyst while only the other knows the required research, and confirm the craft fails and the item melts; repeat as the researcher to confirm it crafts, recording which client sees the output (items 5, 15).
