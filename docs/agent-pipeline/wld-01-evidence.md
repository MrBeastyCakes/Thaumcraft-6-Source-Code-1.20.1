The capture checklist therefore uses a never-drained chunk's vis figure as the base proxy (finding 8) — whether that proxy holds through every generation path is the observation.
- The regeneration cadence as felt: the arithmetic says one sweep per second per dimension with vis steps of up to a quarter of a point (`common/world/aura/AuraThread.java:73`, `:36-37`, `:56-58`) — whether a two-minute observation can see the steps above the readout's rounding is the measurement (T2, T4).
- The moon-phase mapping in play: the phase tables are indexed by the world's moon phase (`common/world/aura/AuraThread.java:56-58`) — setting the world clock to a chosen phase and watching the direction of change is the observation (T2).
- The crash pair: which side of a drain survives a hard kill (the stored figures or the drain's product) depends on vanilla's save schedule as much as on this tree, so the outcome is an observation, not a derivation (finding 20a; T4).
- Whether the twenty-tick dirty flush can force-load a chunk whose aura record was dropped: the flush marks a position inside the chunk as dirty (`common/lib/events/ServerEvents.java:157-164`), and what vanilla does when that position's chunk is not loaded is outside this tree — a pending check for the port's mapping of the flush, not a claim of this note.
- The thread-fault hazard of finding 5 (a dimension whose world disappears between two reads on the regeneration thread) is a race that cannot be observed deterministically; it is listed as a hazard to design out, not as a defect to reproduce.
- Multiplayer readout classes: how stale a second client's figure looks around a drain, and whether two observers ever disagree on a settled chunk, are the measurements (T7).
- The flux leaves — taint, rifts, crystals, and the condenser's conversion — are other lanes' surfaces; this note only fixes the store they act on (findings 18-19; [aut-04-evidence.md](aut-04-evidence.md) item 5; `WLD-03`).

## Reference behavior (source-derived)

### A. The store, the APIs, and the drain semantics

1. **The substrate is a static per-dimension map of chunk records, and a record is a four-field value with a weak handle on the live chunk.** Three static maps hold the whole subsystem — the aura worlds by dimension id, the per-dimension dirty-chunk lists, and a per-dimension pending rift position — all built in a static initializer (`common/world/aura/AuraHandler.java:21-24`, `:184-188`). A dimension's world is a concurrent map from a two-int chunk key to a record (`common/world/aura/AuraWorld.java:8-14`, `:24-31`; the key type `common/lib/utils/PosXY.java:6-7`, with equality over the two coordinates and a hash mixing them, `:23-28`, `:32-34`). A record carries the chunk position, a short base, a float vis, a float flux, and a weak reference to the vanilla chunk (`common/world/aura/AuraChunk.java:9-13`); two constructors exist — the chunk-binding one used by every in-tree creation site and a position-only one with no callers (`:15-17`, `:19-27`). Both value setters clamp into the range zero to 32766, a bound far above the generation ceiling of five hundred (finding 8), so it is a clamp of last resort rather than an in-play limit (`:45-47`, `:53-55`). The base is written by the constructors and by a setter that nothing calls, so a chunk's base never changes after it is written (`:33-39`).

2. **Every lookup goes through the same arithmetic — the block coordinate shifted right four bits — and missing data reads as zero.** Each accessor resolves the chunk record from the dimension id and the position's x and z shifted right four (`common/world/aura/AuraHandler.java:69-72`, `:74-77`, `:79-82`, `:84-87`, `:89-92`, `:103`, `:114`, `:123`, `:136`), and a dimension with no cache entry has one created on the spot before the lookup is retried (`:30-39`). When the record is missing, the read APIs answer zero rather than fail (`:69-92`), and the drain APIs reach the same zero by exception swallowing: they dereference the record ahead of any null test and catch everything, so a missing record yields a zero drain (`:120-131`, `:133-144`). Two derived reads sit on top: the total aura is vis plus flux, and the flux saturation is flux divided by base with no zero guard (`:69-72`, `:74-77`) — the divisor is a few hundred units in ordinary play (finding 8). The taint lane's consumers read that saturation (`common/blocks/world/taint/TaintHelper.java:89`; `common/entities/monster/tainted/EntityTaintSeed.java:132`), a boundary noted here and derived there (`WLD-03`).

3. **The drain contract: clamp to what the chunk holds, subtract with a floor at zero, and report the clamped figure — with a simulate flag and a discouraged flux twin.** drainVis folds the requested amount down to the record's vis, subtracts through a helper that floors the result at zero and answers whether a record existed, and returns the folded figure or zero (`common/world/aura/AuraHandler.java:120-131`, `:146-154`). The fourth parameter is a simulation flag: with it set, the helper is called with its write suppressed, so the caller learns what would move without moving it (`:127`). drainFlux mirrors the shape (`:133-144`, `:156-164`), and the public API's documentation for it advises against using it at all and points callers at instability instead (`api/aura/AuraHelper.java:23-34`). One asymmetry to carry into the port: the add path refuses negative amounts (`common/world/aura/AuraHandler.java:98-101`, `:109-112`), while the drain path inverts whatever it is handed, so a negative drain adds to the chunk and reports a negative figure (the inversion at `:127`, mirrored at `:140`). The sampled call sites pass positive figures (`api/items/RechargeHelper.java:36-38`; `common/tiles/devices/TileVisGenerator.java:50-55`), so this is an API-contract hazard to preserve or tighten rather than a live defect — and it is the mechanism behind finding 20d.

4. **The low-water read: consumers back off when the local vis is under a tenth of the base and the player holds the preservation research.** shouldPreserveAura answers true when the caller passes no player or the player's knowledge includes the AURAPRESERVE research, and the local vis sits under a tenth of the local base (`common/world/aura/AuraHandler.java:94-96`) — the item-recharge helper is the example consumer, returning without draining (`api/items/RechargeHelper.java:36`). This is the substrate's only player-state-dependent read; everything else is player-agnostic.

### B. The regeneration thread

5. **The update is off-thread: one dedicated Java thread per dimension, launched from the world tick's start phase, looping on a fixed one-second cadence gated by the world clock, and stopping itself when its dimension's cache is gone.** The world tick returns early on a client and, in its start phase, starts a thread whenever the dimension has no thread recorded and an aura cache exists, storing the thread in a static map (`common/lib/events/ServerEvents.java:105-118`, the map `:61` and its static initializer `:419`). The thread logs its start and then loops: it abandons the loop entirely when no aura world exists anywhere (`common/world/aura/AuraThread.java:44-48`); it resolves its own dimension's cache and stops itself when that cache is gone (`:50-51`, `:65-67`); otherwise it performs one sweep only when the world clock has advanced since the previous pass and then sleeps out the remainder of a one-thousand-millisecond interval, whose named constant beside it is never read (`:19`, `:49-53`, `:68-75`, the sleep's literal at `:73`); when it exits it removes its own entry from the thread map (`:77-83`). Two hazards are visible in the text. First, the world handle is dereferenced for the clock comparison ahead of the null guard beside it, so a dimension whose world disappears between the cache check and the clock read would fault the thread (`:52-59`). Second, because the thread-map entry is removed only after the loop, a thread that dies on an uncaught fault leaves its entry behind, and the start-phase test — which only checks for a recorded thread — would never start a replacement (`common/lib/events/ServerEvents.java:112-117`), leaving that dimension's aura unregenerated until the server restarts. Both are races a port should design out rather than reproduce (unverified list).

6. **The sweep's arithmetic: a moon-phased baseline, one-step neighbour flows, and the vis-flux ladder.** Before the pass, three phase figures are recomputed from the world's moon phase — a vis step from one table, a baseline multiplier of one plus a second table, and a flux step that is a quarter minus the vis step, so the two steps trade off across the lunar cycle (`common/world/aura/AuraThread.java:36-37`, `:56-58`). Per chunk: the baseline is the record's base times the phase multiplier (`:91`); the four horizontal neighbours are examined in a shuffled order (`:87-88`, `:99-113`); vis moves toward the lowest-vis neighbour, one point at most, only when that neighbour is itself below its own scaled baseline and holds under three quarters of this chunk's vis (`:114-120`); flux moves toward the lowest-flux neighbour, one point at most, when this chunk holds more than the larger of five or a tenth of its base and the neighbour holds under about four-sevenths of this chunk's flux (`:121-127`); then the ladder runs: a shortfall of the chunk's vis plus flux against the baseline raises vis by the vis step, capped at that shortfall (`:128-132`); otherwise, vis above one and a quarter times the baseline converts a flux-step's worth of vis into flux on a one-in-ten roll (`:133-137`); otherwise, vis at or under a tenth of the baseline and not below the flux figure adds a flux-step's worth of flux outright on the same roll (`:138-141`). The pass writes the record back and dirty-marks it when anything changed — and the shortfall branch raises that flag even when the vis step is zero and nothing moves (`:128-132`, `:142-146`). Finally, a chunk past three quarters of its baseline in flux rolls a chance scaled by its flux figure to arm a rift position for the dimension (`:147-149`); that trigger is consumed by the world tick and rifts are the other world lane's (`common/lib/events/ServerEvents.java:165-170`, `WLD-03`).

7. **The dirty list is the bridge to vanilla saving: a de-duplicated concurrent list of chunk positions, guarded by the chunk's own modified flag.** The thread records a mutated chunk's position in the dimension's copy-on-write list, skipping any chunk whose bound vanilla chunk already reports itself modified (`common/world/aura/AuraThread.java:152-164`; the modified test `common/world/aura/AuraChunk.java:29-31`), and de-duplicates by containment before adding (`common/world/aura/AuraThread.java:160-163`).

### C. Generation and the dimension lifecycle

8. **A fresh chunk's aura is a five-biome average with about ten per cent gaussian noise, clamped to a five-hundred ceiling; it starts at full vis with no flux.** The generator samples the biome at the chunk's centre column and at the same height in each of the four horizontal neighbours, averages the five aura modifiers, multiplies by five hundred and by a gaussian factor of about one plus or minus a tenth, clamps the product into the range zero to five hundred, and creates the record with base and vis both at that figure and flux at zero (`common/world/aura/AuraHandler.java:166-182`). A biome on the blacklist is refused at the centre check — the centre biome is fetched first and then tested (`:167-170`) — so a blacklisted chunk gets no record at all and later reads answer zero (finding 2). Because vis starts equal to base, a never-drained chunk's vis figure is also its base figure — the working base proxy for the capture checklist, since no readout prints the base numerically (finding 16).

9. **The modifier table keys on biome dictionary types, and the weights run from about a tenth to three quarters.** getBiomeAuraModifier averages the registered weight over every dictionary type the biome carries, falling back to one half if a lookup fails (`common/world/biomes/BiomeHandler.java:32-44`). The registry registers the weights a type at a time — from a tenth for the dead type and an eighth for the nether and end types, through a half for the lush, spooky, forest and swamp types, to three quarters for the mushroom and magical types — with the dry type registered twice, its later entry standing (`common/world/biomes/BiomeHandler.java:104-137`, the duplicate at `:114` and `:133`, entry shape `:28-30`).

10. **Generation is triggered from two places, and the config gates that exist are dead.** The world-generator callback generates a new chunk's aura right after the mod's own world generation, handing the aura generator the chunk it fetched from the provider (`common/world/ThaumcraftWorldGenerator.java:48-51`); a chunk loaded from disk without aura data gets a fresh generation in the chunk-load handler instead (`common/lib/events/ChunkEvents.java:55-67`). Neither call consults the world config: the generation flag's only reader is a node helper that computes a position and discards it (`common/world/ThaumcraftWorldGenerator.java:96-104`), and the regeneration flag's reader is the chunk-load marker queue (`common/lib/events/ChunkEvents.java:78`), whose regeneration path never calls the aura generator (finding 14) — so neither flag affects the substrate in this tree (finding 18). The blacklist families — dimension and biome — are consulted by the structure, vegetation, and ore paths but never by the aura path (`common/world/ThaumcraftWorldGenerator.java:72-73`, `:106-110`, `:217-223`; `common/world/aura/AuraHandler.java:166-170`), so a dimension blacklisted for structure generation still receives aura.

11. **The dimension lifecycle: cache created on world load, dropped whole on unload, recreated by any lookup.** The server-side world-load hook creates the dimension's aura cache, and the unload hook removes it along with the seal map (`common/lib/events/WorldEvents.java:20-25`, `:32-38`); the regeneration thread notices the disappearance and exits (finding 5). Any lookup for a dimension without a cache creates one (`common/world/aura/AuraHandler.java:30-39`, `:41-46`), and inserting a chunk record into a dimension without a cache builds one there too (`:53-60`). The load hook never runs on a client (`common/lib/events/WorldEvents.java:20-25`), so a dedicated client's aura maps are never populated by any path in this tree; nothing under `client/` reads the aura maps at all, and the client's only aura surface is the packet-fed record (findings 15-16).

### D. Persistence

12. **What is written and read back: three keys in a per-chunk compound on the save event, three reads on the load event, and a marker that records that world generation ran.** The save handler writes a Thaumcraft compound into the chunk's saved data on every chunk write, sets the world-generation marker under the configured marker name, and — when a record exists — writes the base as a short and the vis and flux as floats; seals ride the same compound (`common/lib/events/ChunkEvents.java:27-52`). When the chunk being written is not loaded, the in-memory record is dropped at that moment (`:38-42`). The load handler rebuilds the record from the three keys when the base key is present, binding it to the loaded chunk, and otherwise generates a fresh one (finding 8) (`:55-67`). The marker is the world-generation bookkeeping: a loaded chunk without it is queued for regeneration when any regeneration option is on (`:78-89`). The world-level save hook is an empty body (`common/lib/events/WorldEvents.java:27-30`), so no part of the substrate's persistence is a world-level save.

13. **The flush cadence: mutations are not written when they happen; the world tick marks the affected chunks dirty every twenty ticks and vanilla's own chunk writes carry the values.** Every twentieth world tick, the tick's end phase iterates the dimension's dirty list and marks each listed position's chunk dirty at a fixed sample block, then clears the list (`common/lib/events/ServerEvents.java:157-164`). The write itself happens later, inside whatever vanilla chunk write follows (finding 12). The same list is also the seal system's dirty carrier, so one flush serves both (`common/golems/seals/SealHandler.java:210-217`). Three consequences for the acceptance's reload clause: the aura's on-disk state can lag its memory state by up to the flush interval plus vanilla's save schedule; the aura's state and a player's state are written at different moments (chunk write versus player save), so a hard kill can pair them inconsistently (finding 20a); and the per-dimension tick counter that drives the cadence restarts at zero only with a fresh process — nothing clears it on a world unload, so a same-process re-entry resumes the prior phase (`common/lib/events/ServerEvents.java:120-122`, `:174`; the unload hook `common/lib/events/WorldEvents.java:32-38`).

14. **What a reload restores, and what is re-derived or lost.** A reload restores the exact stored triple — base, vis, flux — for every chunk that had aura data, and generates anew only for chunks without it (findings 8 and 12). Everything else is rebuilt or reset: the regeneration thread restarts at the first world tick after load (finding 5), its phase figures recompute from the moon phase on the first sweep, and the statics only reset with a fresh process: nothing clears the dirty list or the pending rift position on a world unload (`common/world/aura/AuraHandler.java:184-188` is the only initializer; `common/lib/events/WorldEvents.java:32-38` removes only the aura world and the seal map), so a same-process quit-and-re-enter can carry a stale dirty list and a pending rift position into the reloaded world, both consumed by the next end-phase block — at most twenty ticks later (`common/lib/events/ServerEvents.java:157-170`). The regeneration machinery never re-rolls a saved chunk's aura: the queued-regeneration path calls the world generator with no aura call in it, so an exhausted chunk stays exhausted across the regeneration of its features (`common/lib/events/ServerEvents.java:178-203`; `common/world/ThaumcraftWorldGenerator.java:53-69` versus the aura call at `:48-51`).

### E. Networking and the client readout

15. **One packet carries aura to a client, it goes only to a player actively holding a reading tool, and nothing else about the substrate crosses the wire.** The payload is the three stored figures — base as a short, vis and flux as floats — and the handler installs them as the client's single static aura record (`common/lib/network/misc/PacketAuraToClient.java:12-37`, `:39-47`; the client field `client/lib/events/HudHandler.java:47`, initialized to a zero record in the class initializer `:511`). Registration is client-bound and sits in the channel's registration order (`common/lib/network/PacketHandler.java:51-65`; the channel `:96-98`). The two senders: the Thaumometer's update pass, once every twenty ticks while the stack is selected or sitting in the first hotbar slot, sending the holder's own chunk record and starting the FLUX research popup on unhealthy flux reads (`common/items/tools/ItemThaumometer.java:56-60`, `:92-101`); and the caster's update pass, once every ten ticks whenever its holder holds any caster, sending the same single-chunk record (`common/items/casters/ItemCaster.java:260-269`, `:271-318`). Watching a chunk sends seals, never aura (`common/lib/events/ChunkEvents.java:92-97`), and no client-to-server aura message exists anywhere in the channel's registration (`common/lib/network/PacketHandler.java:51-93`). The pollution effect is a separate one-shot fx packet to clients within thirty-two blocks, sent only from the flux-add path (`common/lib/InternalMethodHandler.java:129-138`; `common/lib/network/fx/PacketFXPollute.java:12-33`, `:48-53`).

16. **What the client readout is built from, and its one scale.** The client's record is overwritten by each packet and by nothing else (finding 11). The Thaumometer's HUD divides each of the three figures by a literal five hundred twenty-five and clamps, renormalizing once the vis-and-flux total passes the scale, then draws a vis bar, a flux bar, and the numeric vis and flux figures while the holder sneaks (`client/lib/events/HudHandler.java:207-213` — the literal at `:209-211` — `:214-219`, `:239-247`, `:248-271`; the named scale constant at `:48` is never read, and the one-decimal format is `:60`). The caster's dial paints the same record against the stored base rather than the HUD's scale, taking the stored base as the dial's full range and the stored vis as the fill (`:345`, `:372-381`); that dial's player-facing contract, including its numeric sneak line, is [cas-03-evidence.md](cas-03-evidence.md) item 21 and is not re-derived here. The readout renders only while one of the reading items is held (`:87-110`), so a player holding neither keeps whatever record the last packet left, and a freshly launched client starts at the zero record (`:511`).

17. **The dormant aggregation: the caster's multi-chunk readout and multi-chunk drain split exist in code but no caster uses them.** The update pass switches on the item's area figure — one chunk by default, the four horizontal neighbours at area one, a three-by-three at area two — and the cast-cost drain mirror-images the same neighbourhood split (`common/items/casters/ItemCaster.java:106-131`, `:133-200`, `:275-317`). The only caster the tree registers is area zero (`common/config/ConfigItems.java:275`; the field set twice in the constructor with the argument winning `common/items/casters/ItemCaster.java:56-65`), so both aggregate paths are unreachable in BETA26 and a port should not reproduce them expecting them to be exercised.

### F. Config, dormancy, and crash surfaces

18. **The config surface for the substrate is two flags and a marker key — no maxima, no rates, no decay.** The world config exposes an aura-generation flag (default true), an aura-regeneration flag (default false), and the marker string (default "DEFAULT") (`common/config/ModConfig.java:348`, `:356-357`, `:381`, `:388-389`). As finding 10 derives, the generation flag's only reader is the dead node helper, so on and off behave identically in this tree; the regeneration flag's reader is the chunk-load marker queue (finding 12), whose regeneration path re-runs features and never the aura generator (finding 14). There is no aura maximum, regeneration rate, or flux-decay setting anywhere: the ceiling is the generator's own clamp of five hundred (`common/world/aura/AuraHandler.java:180`), the declared ceiling constant and the mod's aura-size field have no readers at all (`common/world/aura/AuraHandler.java:21`; `common/config/ModConfig.java:50`), the thread's cadence is a literal in its sleep call beside an unread named constant and both phase tables are private constants (`common/world/aura/AuraThread.java:19`, `:36-37`, `:73`), and the one misc flag that touches this lane only suppresses rift creation when a trigger is pending (`common/lib/events/ServerEvents.java:165-170`; `common/config/ModConfig.java:412-413`, default `:428`).

19. **Dormant and edge paths a port must not copy blindly.** The position-only record constructor and the base setter have no callers (`common/world/aura/AuraChunk.java:15-17`, `:37-39`). The generator's aura-node helper is never called (`common/world/ThaumcraftWorldGenerator.java:96-104`). The shortfall branch dirty-marks a record even when its step is zero (finding 6). The world-save hook is empty (finding 12). The caster's aggregate paths are unreachable (finding 17). And the generator callback fetches the chunk it is generating through the provider inside the same callback (`common/world/ThaumcraftWorldGenerator.java:48-51`) — a re-entrancy surface to check when the port maps this hook, because a second generation pass over a chunk that already has a record would overwrite both base and vis with fresh figures (`common/world/aura/AuraHandler.java:53-60`, `:181`).

20. **Crash and persistence surfaces the acceptance's reload clause should probe.** (a) Decoupled save moments: aura debits reach disk only when the chunk is next written, while the debit's product — an item charge, an essentia conversion, a crafted output — may be saved elsewhere in the same period, so a hard kill can keep one side and lose the other; that asymmetry, not either outcome alone, is the defect (`common/lib/events/ChunkEvents.java:27-52` with the flush cadence of finding 13; the recharge example `api/items/RechargeHelper.java:34-45`). (b) Unload drops: a record leaves memory at the unload save, so values can be lost but never duplicated on that path; the next load either restores the stored triple or generates a fresh base (findings 12, 14). (c) A dirty position can outlive its record for up to one flush interval when a chunk unloads just after being marked, and the flush marks a block position inside that chunk — what vanilla does with a position in an unloaded chunk is outside this tree (unverified list; `common/lib/events/ServerEvents.java:157-164`; `common/world/aura/AuraThread.java:152-164`). (d) The sign inversion of the drain API (finding 3): any caller that can compute a negative amount turns a drain into a source, and the recharge helper's amount is the headroom under an item's own maximum charge (`api/items/RechargeHelper.java:36-38`). (e) The substrate keeps no conservation ledger: the thread moves at most a point per pass per direction with a zero floor on every write, so a total audit must come from the counts the checklist takes, not from the code's own bookkeeping (`common/world/aura/AuraThread.java:114-127`, `common/world/aura/AuraHandler.java:146-154`).

## Multiplayer and divergence classes

21. **What crosses the wire, and who can see what.** The only aura payload is the three-figure packet (finding 15); it is a point-to-point message to the holding player rather than a broadcast, and its two senders tick at different cadences (twenty ticks for the Thaumometer, ten for a held caster). Nothing else announces aura state: chunk watch sends seals (`common/lib/events/ChunkEvents.java:92-97`), the pollution fx packet is one-shot and radius-bound (`common/lib/InternalMethodHandler.java:136`), and no client ever receives a neighbouring chunk's records — a client's copy is one record for one chunk (finding 16).

22. **Divergence classes to hunt at rank one.** (a) Two observers of one chunk read the same server figures but sample them at different instants — the Thaumometer path refreshes once a second per holder and the caster path twice a second, and two players' item tick phases need not be aligned (`common/items/tools/ItemThaumometer.java:56-60`; `common/items/casters/ItemCaster.java:260-268`) — record both readouts together around one drain. (b) A draining player's own readout lags the drain by a tick and the observer's by up to a packet interval; a device that reports its own consumption is the second reference point (the levitator's top-up is [aut-04-evidence.md](aut-04-evidence.md) item 8). (c) A player holding neither tool sees no HUD at all, and one who just put the tool away keeps a stale figure (finding 16) — compare a stale readout against a live one on the same chunk. (d) A freshly connected client starts from the zero record until its first packet (`client/lib/events/HudHandler.java:511`), so two clients can disagree purely on connection time. (e) Singleplayer versus a dedicated client: in singleplayer the client shares the static maps with the integrated server, while on a dedicated server the client's maps are never populated (finding 11) — any port code that reads the aura on a client would behave differently across the two setups, which is a property to preserve deliberately, and the tree's own client code reads none of it (finding 16).

## Rank-1 capture checklist

Reference setup for every step: the clean 1.12.2 profile whose only mods are the BETA26 jar and Baubles (`REF-0002` and the "Environment feasibility" section of [reference-catalog.md](reference-catalog.md)); a fresh creative world with its seed recorded in a new `REF` entry before the session. Carry a Thaumometer — its readout is fed from the chunk's aura and it prints the numeric vis and flux lines while the holder sneaks (`common/items/tools/ItemThaumometer.java:92-101`; `client/lib/events/HudHandler.java:239-247`, `:248-271`) — and a vis-caster with a focus for the drain steps ([cas-03-evidence.md](cas-03-evidence.md) records the creative-tab supply of caster and blank foci); the creative-only flux sponge is the flux lever, draining a nine-by-nine chunk area and reporting the total (`common/items/misc/ItemCreativeFluxSponge.java:49-72`). Because the base is never printed (finding 8), use a never-drained chunk's vis figure as the base proxy. Set the world clock with the operator between measurements when a moon phase matters — the vis and flux steps key on it (`common/world/aura/AuraThread.java:36-37`, `:56-58`) — and use `/thaumcraft reload` and `/thaumcraft research <player> <list|all|reset|<key>>` between measurements, never during one (`common/lib/CommandThaumcraft.java:64-70`, `:84-107`). Start every structure from a recorded position and a screenshot so the reload steps can compare exact cells, and take every reading as a pair at least two seconds apart, since the sweep runs about once a second and the numeric line prints to a tenth (`client/lib/events/HudHandler.java:60`).

- **T1 — The fresh baseline.** In a fresh world, stand still in one biome with the Thaumometer held and record the vis figure (the base proxy) and the flux figure; walk thirty-two blocks into a second biome and repeat, recording that the figure differs (finding 9); then quit and re-enter the world, read the same spot again, and record that the figures are the stored triple unchanged (findings 8, 12, 14). Bounded to two chunks and one reload.
- **T2 — Drain and regeneration against the moon phase.** Note the world clock; drain the local chunk with a fixed number of cheap casts (record the focus cost each time) and read the vis after each; then stand and read every thirty seconds for two minutes, recording the recovery increments. Repeat with the clock set to a different moon phase and compare the direction and step of the change (finding 6). Bounded to one chunk, two minutes of observation.
- **T3 — Reload conservation (the acceptance slice).** After T2's drain, record the triple; save and quit (and repeat on a stop/start of a dedicated server if available); re-enter and re-read: the drained figures must persist rather than reset to a fresh base, and the flux must persist too (findings 12-14). Bounded to one chunk, one reload.
- **T4 — The crash pair.** Drain a fixed amount, then hard-kill the game process before the next save wave — the client's integrated server in singleplayer, the server process on a dedicated run — restart, and record both sides: whether the drained figures came back and whether the drain's product (the cast's effect on the world or inventory) survived; the pair — not either side alone — is the measured quantity (finding 20a; bounded to one kill).
- **T5 — Flux, its spread, and the sponge.** Seed flux with the crucible spill or a jar emptied by hand ([alc-00-evidence.md](alc-00-evidence.md) items 23-24; [alc-02-evidence.md](alc-02-evidence.md) item 11), read the flux line, and record that it does not fall on its own while the neighbouring chunk's line rises when the gap is large enough (finding 6); then remove it with the flux sponge and compare the reported total against the figures read (bounds: two chunks, one minute). Flux leaves the world only through consumers outside this lane — taint and rifts (`WLD-03`), crystals, and the condenser ([aut-04-evidence.md](aut-04-evidence.md) item 5) — so the sponge is the neutral sink.
- **T6 — The player-state read.** With the AURAPRESERVE research granted, drain the chunk below a tenth of its base and run the recharge lever the charge lane uses ([aut-03-evidence.md](aut-03-evidence.md)); record that nothing is drawn, then reset the research, repeat, and record the drain (finding 4).
- **T7 — Multiplayer and staleness.** On a LAN with a second client (or mark not-run), hold the Thaumometer on client A and a caster on client B, drain the chunk from B, and compare both readouts plus a third client holding neither; then put the tool away on A and record the stale figure; note whether a freshly joined client reads zero first (findings 16, 22).
- **T8 — The dimension sweep and the dedicated clause.** Carry the Thaumometer through a portal and record each dimension's own figures and their survival across a round trip; then repeat T3's conservation on a dedicated server with a remote client if the EULA step in [reference-catalog.md](reference-catalog.md) is taken, and otherwise mark the dedicated-server clause not-run (findings 11, 14). The acceptance "Fresh and reloaded worlds maintain stable aura values." fails on any count in T3, T4, or T8 that disagrees with the stored triple.
