## Target behaviors to capture

From the evidence-index queue row for `ALC-01` / `ALC-02` (Smelter aspect extraction, alembic output, jars, tube transfer, and filters) and the workboard acceptance line ("Valid items smelt into correct Essentia; invalid inputs do not consume items"):

1. **Smelter family** — the three smelter blocks, the shared tile, and how the variants differ.
2. **Interaction and state** — the GUI slots, the hopper/pipe surface, the burning state and its visuals, and what the tile persists versus syncs.
3. **Fuel and timing** — what burns, when fuel is consumed and when it is not, the cook gate, and the smelt-time formula including bellows.
4. **Acceptance and extraction** — which items are accepted, how their aspects are computed and rolled into amounts, and what "lost" means.
5. **Buffer** — where the produced Essentia lives, how it merges, its cap, and which paths can read or drain it.
6. **Output path** — the export cadence, the alembic as receiver plus its surface, and the jar-side receiving interface at the contract level.
7. **Out of scope** — tubes, suction, filters, and transfer semantics (`ALC-02`), the crucible (`ALC-00`), and item-aspect lookup itself (`FND-04`) are cross-referenced only.

## Reference behavior (source-derived)

### A. The smelter family and its block surface

1. **Three smelter blocks share one block class and one tile class, and the aux and vent are markers.** The basic, thaumium, and void smelters are registered alongside the smelter aux and smelter vent (`common/config/ConfigBlocks.java:309-313`); the aux and vent carry no tile of their own and exist only for the tile's neighbor checks (`common/blocks/essentia/BlockSmelterAux.java:21-29`, `common/blocks/essentia/BlockSmelterVent.java:22-30`). The tile derives its variant from the block it belongs to: efficiency 0.8 for basic, 0.9 for thaumium, 0.95 for anything else (the void smelter), and an export period of 15 ticks for basic and void versus 10 for thaumium (`common/tiles/essentia/TileSmelter.java:209-227`).
2. **The interaction surface is a GUI, and every number in it travels as a window property.** Right-clicking opens the smelter container server-side for a non-sneaking player (`common/blocks/essentia/BlockSmelter.java:61-66`, GUI id 9); the container holds an aspect-gated input slot and a plain fuel slot above the player inventory (`common/container/ContainerSmelter.java:27-39`), shift-click routes by the same acceptance test (`:101-143`), and the cook, burn, current-burn, smelt-time, and buffered-total values are pushed as five window properties that the screen renders as three bars (`:41-95`; `client/gui/GuiSmelter.java:40-49`). The tile is usable only within eight blocks of a player (`common/tiles/TileThaumcraftInventory.java:193-195`).
3. **The lit state is the burning flag, and redstone is not wired to it.** The block's enabled property is written only through the tile's state setter (`common/blocks/essentia/BlockSmelter.java:89-102`, called from `common/tiles/essentia/TileSmelter.java:129`, `:146`, `:150`), raises block light to 13 while on (`common/blocks/essentia/BlockSmelter.java:68-70`) and drives flame and smoke particles on the front face (`:114-139`); the smelter overrides the parent's add, neighbor, and placement hooks (`:41-43`, `:46-51`, `:54-59`), so the parent's powered-based state toggle (`common/blocks/BlockTCDevice.java:68-75`) never runs for a smelter — placement forces the state off (`common/blocks/essentia/BlockSmelter.java:49`) and only burning turns it on.
4. **The block's readouts are inventory-based, and the buffer is visible only in the GUI.** The comparator mirrors the item inventory, not the Essentia (`common/blocks/essentia/BlockSmelter.java:72-74`, `:81-87`), and goggles cannot see the buffer because the tile declares no aspect-container interface (`common/tiles/essentia/TileSmelter.java:29-30`; the overlay draw is gated on that interface, `client/lib/events/RenderEventHandler.java:395-400`), leaving the GUI's fill bar as the only view of stored Essentia (`client/gui/GuiSmelter.java:47-49`; `common/tiles/essentia/TileSmelter.java:322-325`).

### B. Fuel, heat, and job timing

5. **The smelter's "heat" system is fuel, not temperature.** Any vanilla furnace fuel is accepted (`common/tiles/essentia/TileSmelter.java:274-276`); one unit is consumed when the burn timer has run out and the queued item is ready to smelt, with container items replaced (`:123-152`), and alumentum is given a 4800-tick burn time by the mod's own registered fuel handler (`common/lib/events/CraftingEvents.java:21-32`, registered at `proxies/CommonProxy.java:62`). There is no lava/fire requirement and no heat threshold — the crucible's 151-degree gate and heat sources ([alc-00-evidence.md](alc-00-evidence.md) item 12) have no counterpart here.
6. **Alumentum also sets an internal speed flag, and the boost arithmetic in this tree does not do what it says.** Fuel matching alumentum sets the tile's boost flag (`common/tiles/essentia/TileSmelter.java:134-136`); the application multiplies the export period by zero point eight cast to an integer, which is zero in this decompiled text (`:98-101`), so the periodic export check would evaluate a remainder against a zero period and raise on every server tick while the flag is set. The intent is plainly a shorter period; the shipped jar must be checked before a port adopts either reading — **unverified — needs rank-1 observation**.
7. **Cook progress is a strict two-condition gate, and one job consumes exactly one unit.** Progress only advances while the block state is enabled and the queue can smelt, and it resets to zero the moment either stops holding (`common/tiles/essentia/TileSmelter.java:153-163`); a completed job removes one unit from the input stack and empties the slot at zero (`:267-270`). A started burn keeps draining even when the queue stalls, because the burn timer was never re-checked against the gate (`:91-93` against `:123-152`).
8. **Job duration is a function of the queued item's own aspects and the bellows count.** The duration is twice the queued stack's computed aspect total, scaled by one minus one eighth per counted bellows and truncated to whole ticks, recomputed from the current queue on every check (`common/tiles/essentia/TileSmelter.java:185`); bellows are counted among the three horizontal sides other than the smelter's front, sampled on placement and on neighbor change, and only while the bellows block itself is enabled (`:95-97`, `:189-207`; `common/tiles/devices/TileBellows.java:82-94`). A bellows is enabled when it is not powered (`common/blocks/BlockTCDevice.java:68-75`; `common/blocks/devices/BlockBellows.java:48-58`), so powering one removes it from the count.
9. **A smelter with no fuel is permanently idle, and an empty smelter never spends fuel.** Without a burn timer the block state stays off and cook progress cannot accumulate (`common/tiles/essentia/TileSmelter.java:153-163`), and fuel is consumed only to start a burn when an item is queued (`:123-152`), so both the queued item and the fuel slot are left untouched until then.

### C. Acceptance, extraction, and the buffer

10. **One acceptance rule gates every insertion path: the stack's computed aspect list must be non-empty.** The tile's slot validator, the GUI input slot, and the container's shift-click logic all consult that single test, while the fuel slot separately requires a valid fuel (`common/tiles/essentia/TileSmelter.java:278-287`; `common/container/slot/SlotLimitedHasAspects.java:9-19`; `common/container/ContainerSmelter.java:107-118`). There is no per-item whitelist, and an item whose computed aspects are empty is refused everywhere rather than consumed. What "computed" means is [fnd-04-evidence.md](fnd-04-evidence.md) items 13-16 and is deliberately not restated here.
11. **The pipe surface is a plain sided inventory.** Insertion is possible from the four sides only, never the top; the side faces expose the input slot and the bottom exposes the fuel slot, while the top exposes no slot at all even though its permission check would allow only buckets out of the fuel slot (`common/tiles/essentia/TileSmelter.java:289-302`, `:364-368`), and the tile hands out per-face item-handler wrappers (`common/tiles/TileThaumcraftInventory.java:225-231`, `:256-281`). Contents persist with the chunk (`common/tiles/TileThaumcraftInventory.java:170-187`), and no item-slot data is ever synced to clients: the inherited synced-slot list is empty by default (`:40`) and only four other tiles in the tree ever assign it, so the smelter sends no inventory sync message (the network payload is the burn time only, item 17).
12. **The readiness test is three-part, and it is what protects invalid inputs and the cap.** The tile refuses to smelt an empty slot, a null or empty computed list, or a queue whose incoming total does not fit under the buffer cap (`common/tiles/essentia/TileSmelter.java:173-184`) — so an item that fails is never consumed and never partially paid, which is the source-side shape of the workboard's "invalid inputs do not consume items".
13. **The buffer is one aspect list with a cached total and a 256-unit cap.** The tile owns a single list, an integer cache of its total, and the private cap of 256 set in its constructor; the cap is enforced by the readiness test and the cache is refreshed after each extraction and each export (`common/tiles/essentia/TileSmelter.java:34-53`, `:173-184`, `:266`, `:304-312`; the list's size and total accessors are `api/aspects/AspectList.java:42-58`).
14. **Extraction is a per-unit retention roll, and the losses become pollution.** For every unit of every computed aspect, one random draw decides whether the unit survives — retained when the draw is at or below the variant's efficiency, and at or below efficiency times 0.66 for the flux aspect; each lost unit increases the count handed to the vent and pollution path (`common/tiles/essentia/TileSmelter.java:233-243` with the efficiencies at `:213-222`). The expected retained share is 80, 90, or 95 percent for basic, thaumium, or void, and roughly 53, 59, or 63 percent for flux; the realized total per item is **unverified — needs rank-1 observation**.
15. **Surviving amounts merge additively into the one buffer, and never land anywhere else.** Retained amounts are merged with accumulating add semantics (`common/tiles/essentia/TileSmelter.java:242`, semantics per [fnd-04-evidence.md](fnd-04-evidence.md) item 9), so successive items accrue into a single pool whose iteration order is insertion order ([fnd-04-evidence.md](fnd-04-evidence.md) item 8) — the order the export loop later walks (`common/tiles/essentia/TileSmelter.java:103`, `:113`).
16. **The tile declares no Essentia interfaces, so the buffer has exactly one outflow.** The class implements neither the container nor the transport contract (`common/tiles/essentia/TileSmelter.java:29-30`), and the only callers of its take method anywhere in this tree are the two export loops inside the same class (`:105`, `:115`, `:304-312`); no tube, mirror, goggles, or comparator path reaches the buffer.
17. **The tile persists more than it syncs.** The chunk save carries the buffered aspects under the aspect-list compound, the burn time, the cook time, and the boost flag, and the load path recomputes the cached total; the network payload carries only the burn time, and landing it forces a light recheck (`common/tiles/essentia/TileSmelter.java:56-83`; `common/tiles/TileThaumcraft.java:42-74`; the aspect-list key and amount shape is [fnd-04-evidence.md](fnd-04-evidence.md) item 11).

### D. The export path and the alembic

18. **The export is a periodic server-side push that is not gated on burning.** Every 10 or 15 ticks, while the buffer is non-empty, the smelter tries to push one unit — the first aspect in buffer order that a receiver accepts — into the column above its own block, and then one unit into the column above each adjacent, correctly facing aux block (`common/tiles/essentia/TileSmelter.java:102-122`). A cold or idle smelter therefore drains into a placed receiver, because the loop sits outside the burn and cook branch (`:94-122` against `:123-163`).
19. **The receiver search prefers alembics that already hold the aspect, then the lowest alembic that will accept it.** The search walks upward while the tiles are alembics, taking the first that already holds the same aspect and can take one unit; if it meets any other tile first, it restarts from the bottom and hands the unit to the lowest alembic that its filter will let accept it (`common/tiles/essentia/TileAlembic.java:189-215`, called from `common/tiles/essentia/TileSmelter.java:104`, `:114`). Only alembics directly above the smelter or above its aux blocks are reachable, and the aux must face the smelter for its column to be used (`common/tiles/essentia/TileSmelter.java:109-121`; `common/blocks/essentia/BlockSmelterAux.java:51-53`).
20. **The alembic is a 128-unit single-aspect store whose only filling path is that search.** It is constructed with a 128 cap and a downward label face (`common/tiles/essentia/TileAlembic.java:27-34`), and its add path refuses an aspect that contradicts its label filter, filling only when empty or matching and capped at 128 (`:76-89`). It refuses transport input outright — input faces are declared unwritable, the transport add returns nothing, and suction is always zero (`:140-143`, `:150-162`, `:179-182`) — and it is not an aspect source (`:17`), so the transport's source search cannot select it (`common/lib/events/EssentiaHandler.java:44-46`, `:210-251`; **borrowed for direction only** — transport semantics are `ALC-02`). Its output direction is any face except the label face and the bottom (`common/tiles/essentia/TileAlembic.java:136-148`), and its contents are read through the container accessors and the transport take call (`:36-39`, `:91-123`, `:174-182`).
21. **The alembic's player surface is a label, an empty command, and a comparator.** Applying a label to a non-vertical side sets the filter to the label's aspect, or to the alembic's current aspect when it already holds one, and marks that side the label face (`common/blocks/essentia/BlockAlembic.java:111-141`); sneaking on the label face returns the label and clears the filter (`:62-76`), sneaking elsewhere empties the alembic into aura pollution with the identical unit count (`:77-89`), and the comparator scales with the fill fraction from one while non-empty (`:101-108`). Its tile factory instantiates only for metadata zero (`:39-44`), and breaking it spills its contents through the generic transport spill path because the alembic is a transport (`common/blocks/BlockTCTile.java:54-65`).
22. **Two item-side paths can also draw from an alembic.** An empty phial used on an alembic holding at least its base amount fills a phial with that unit count and takes it from the alembic (`common/items/consumables/ItemPhial.java:31-33`, `:83-98`), and a jar item used on an alembic draws the whole contents up to the jar item's own 250 cap, then restores them into the jar when the item is placed (`common/blocks/essentia/BlockJarItem.java:66-104`, `:109-117`). These are item-container paths; the item-side contract is [fnd-04-evidence.md](fnd-04-evidence.md) item 22.

### E. The jar as a receiver (interface level only)

23. **A fillable jar is a 250-unit single-aspect receiver that gates on its label.** The capacity is 250 (`common/tiles/essentia/TileJarFillable.java:14`), an addition is accepted only when the jar is empty or the aspect matches with room left and is capped at 250 (`:69-82`), and the accept test honours the jar's filter so a labeled jar refuses other aspects (`:122-124`); sealing is the blocked flag of the shared source contract ([fnd-04-evidence.md](fnd-04-evidence.md) item 21) exposed at `:216-219`. These are the receiving-side facts a smelter chain needs.
24. **The jar's own filling motion is a suction-gated pull from the tile above it.** Every five ticks, while below capacity, the jar looks at the connectable tile above and takes one unit down from it when that tile permits downward output and its suction is below the jar's own — 64 with a filter and 32 without, falling to zero once full, with the same values as its minimum (`common/tiles/essentia/TileJarFillable.java:186-214`, `:146-164`; the connectable-tile helper is `api/ThaumcraftApiHelper.java:69-75`). **Borrowed for direction only:** which tiles can supply that pull, and every tube and filter rule around it, is `ALC-02` scope and is not derived here.
25. **Within this scope, a smelter-to-jar chain is smelter buffer, then the alembic stack, then a transport, then the jar.** The alembic never outputs downward (`common/tiles/essentia/TileAlembic.java:136-148`), and the jar's pull needs the tile above it to allow downward output (`common/tiles/essentia/TileJarFillable.java:193-199`), so an alembic cannot fill a jar beneath it directly; the connecting transport, its filters, and its suction are the `ALC-02` half of the queue row and are cross-referenced, not derived.
26. **What a full or lost buffer costs.** A stalled queue leaves the fire burning and the item waiting, never partially consumed (`common/tiles/essentia/TileSmelter.java:91-93`, `:123-163`, `:173-184`); the per-unit losses of item 14 are offered to adjacent, correctly facing vents with a one-in-three chance per attempt, and each unit that no vent takes is accumulated into an amount handed to the aura pollution helper (`:244-264`, with the vent visual fired as a block event at `:254` and answered at `:343-362`). The decompiled control flow of that loop, however, spins an unconditional retry loop whose only exit is a vented unit, so neither its per-smelt rate nor its termination can be read from this tree (**unverified — needs rank-1 observation**; the second source-level oddity of the status list). Breaking the smelter drops both item stacks and converts the whole buffered total into aura pollution with no Essentia refund (`common/blocks/essentia/BlockSmelter.java:104-112`; the drop path is `common/blocks/BlockTCTile.java:54-55` with `common/lib/utils/InventoryUtils.java:163-169`).

## Rank-1 capture checklist

Reference setup for every step: the clean 1.12.2 profile whose only mods are the BETA26 jar and Baubles (`REF-0002` and the "Environment feasibility" section of [reference-catalog.md](reference-catalog.md)); record the profile, world seed, and game mode in a new `REF` entry before starting. The established session world is **creative**, which supplies the smelter family, the alembic, jars, and bellows from the mod's creative tab without research (every Thaumcraft block sets that tab, `common/blocks/BlockTC.java:20`), so the survival recipes behind `ESSENTIASMELTER@2` and its siblings are irrelevant here; research grants, if used at all, happen between measurements, never during one.

- **S1 — One end-to-end smelt.** Place a basic smelter, fuel it with coal, and queue one item with a small scanned aspect set; record the burn lighting the block, its light level and particles, the GUI cook and fill bars, and the fill total after completion — repeat the smelt several times and compare the average against the expected 80 percent of the item's scanned total, since the retention draw is per unit (items 3, 7, 13, 14).
- **S2 — Invalid input refusal.** Queue an item with no displayed aspects and record that neither the GUI slot nor a hopper accepts it, that no burn starts, and that both the input stack and the fuel are untouched (items 10-12).
- **S3 — Extraction yield and loss.** Smelt a fixed count of one aspect-rich item on a basic smelter, then on a thaumium smelter, recording the fill total after each completion; read the aura and flux at the block with a Thaumometer before and after to see whether the losses register (items 1, 14, 26).
- **S4 — Vent accounting.** Attach a smelter vent facing the smelter and repeat S3's measurement, recording whether any visible venting (sound, particles) accompanies the smelts and whether the aura change differs from S3; this is the direct rank-1 check on the item 26 oddity.
- **S5 — Fuel and timing.** Measure one job's duration precisely (a stopwatch is sufficient; each tick is one twentieth of a second) with a known aspect total and no bellows, then with one, two, and three bellows, then power a bellows and re-measure; compare against twice the aspect total scaled by one minus one eighth per counted bellows (items 5-8).
- **S6 — Export path and alembic.** Place one alembic directly above the smelter and record Essentia arriving in it (the goggles overlay reads the alembic's contents) while the smelter is cold, then a second alembic above the first and record which one fills first; repeat with a filtered alembic in the lower position; then interrupt the column with a non-alembic block and confirm the path stops (items 18-20). The cold half of this step assumes the smelter is unfueled.
- **S7 — Cap, stall, and destruction cost.** With no receiver, fill the buffer toward 256 and record the boundary the fill total stops at, the stalled cook bar, and whether the fire keeps burning; then place a receiver and record the buffered Essentia draining; finally break the charged smelter and a charged alembic and read the aura and flux change, comparing the smelter's cost against its fill total (items 13, 18, 21, 26).
- **S8 — Jar-side numbers.** Fill a jar from a smelter chain until refusal and record the capacity number (read with the goggles overlay), the label's effect, and the sealed-jar behavior; then use a jar item on a charged alembic and record the transfer and the restore-on-placement (items 22-24). Record jar-side numbers only — the transport half of that chain is `ALC-02` scope.
