## Target behaviors to capture

From the evidence-index queue row for `FND-04` (shared aspect attribution for item/block/entity sources, aspect container capacities, and lookup consumed by scanning and Essentia) and the workboard acceptance line ("item aspects, container capacities, and shared lookup resolve consistently for scanning and Essentia handling with unit coverage"):

1. **Item and block attribution** — what a scanned or smelted object is credited with, and which registered form (exact stack, wildcard damage, ore dictionary, generated from recipes) supplied it.
2. **Entity attribution** — what a scanned entity is credited with, including NBT-conditioned variants and players.
3. **Container contracts** — what "capacity" means at the shared interface level, and how a container signals refusal.
4. **Lookup consumers** — the paths scanning and Essentia take to the same data, so both read one authority.
5. **Divergence check** — whether scanning and Essentia handling can disagree about one item's aspects (the defect sentence in [deferred-issues.md](deferred-issues.md)).

## Reference behavior (source-derived)

### A. The aspect model

1. **An aspect is a tag-keyed singleton with a fixed parentage.** The type holds a tag, an optional two-element component array, color, chat color, icon location, and a blend mode; construction registers the instance in a static insertion-ordered map keyed by tag and rejects a duplicate tag outright (`api/aspects/Aspect.java:16-51`, `:88-110`, `:157`). Static lookup is a map read by tag (`:108-110`), and display names are derived from the tag rather than stored (`:80-86`).
2. **Construction has two registration side effects.** Every aspect, including addon aspects, is added to the scanning manager as a scan thing for the key formed by prefixing its tag with an exclamation mark, and an aspect built with two components also writes an entry keyed by the combined component-tag hash into a static mix map (`api/aspects/Aspect.java:46-50`).
3. **Primal versus compound is a shape test.** An aspect counts as primal when it has no component array or when that array does not have exactly two entries; that predicate drives the split between the primal list and the compound list (`api/aspects/Aspect.java:120-122`, `:126-145`). The tree seeds six primals first — air, earth, fire, water, order, entropy — then the compounds in tiers, each with its two parents (`:160-165`, `:168-211`).
4. **Both split lists are cached on first use and never invalidated.** The list getters fill a static collection once and return it thereafter, so an aspect registered after the first call to either getter never appears in it (`api/aspects/Aspect.java:125-145`). Any port that mirrors this caching must decide whether late registration is legal.
5. **Blend resolution exists twice and is not called from anywhere in this tree.** The mix map written at construction is never read (its only occurrences are the declaration and the write, `api/aspects/Aspect.java:28`, `:49`), and the order-insensitive combiners that scan registered aspects by component identity — one on the helper, one duplicated on the research manager — have no call sites in the tree (`api/aspects/AspectHelper.java:110-121`, `common/lib/research/ResearchManager.java:222-230`). Parentage is therefore data that matters to primal reduction and to the culling weight (items 6, 7), not to a live "blend two aspects" path in this build.
6. **Primal reduction is recursive and random draws are frequency-weighted.** Reduction replaces each compound by both parents and recurses until only primals remain, summing amounts at the primal level (`api/aspects/AspectHelper.java:139-158`); the random-primal helper expands the reduced list into one slot per unit and draws uniformly, so higher amounts are likelier (`:123-137`). The random-draw helper has no call sites anywhere in the tree, and the reduction helper is reached only through it and its own recursion (`api/aspects/AspectHelper.java:128`, `:149`).
7. **Culling trims a list to a capped number of entries by weight.** The culling helper copies the input, then repeatedly drops the entry with the lowest weighted amount, weighting primals down and nested compounds up; the default cap is seven (`api/aspects/AspectHelper.java:19-66`). It is applied at the end of item-aspect computation (`common/lib/crafting/ThaumcraftCraftingManager.java:362-367`).

### B. The aspect list

8. **An aspect list is an insertion-ordered map of aspect to amount.** Reading an absent aspect yields zero; copying adds each entry into a fresh list; one accessor counts distinct aspects and another sums every amount (`api/aspects/AspectList.java:12`, `:32-58`, `:126-128`).
9. **The mutators differ in ways that matter to parity.** Add accumulates into an existing amount; merge keeps the higher of old and new; remove subtracts and deletes the entry once the result is at or below zero; a single-argument remove deletes unconditionally; reduce refuses and changes nothing when the stored amount is insufficient, so it never produces a negative from a short read (`api/aspects/AspectList.java:136-202`). The list-valued overloads loop the matching single-aspect form (`:204-220`).
10. **Two ordering helpers exist for display.** They are bubble sorts returning a new array — one by tag text, one by descending amount — each with a fallback to registration order if the sort raises (`api/aspects/AspectList.java:63-120`).
11. **Serialization is key/amount pairs and reads clear first.** Writing emits one entry per present aspect with its tag and amount, including amounts that are zero or negative; reading clears the list, then re-adds each entry that carries a key, so a persisted negative amount survives a round trip (`api/aspects/AspectList.java:227-282`). The list also has a constructor that snapshots an item stack's computed aspects (`:19-27`), which is the shape used when objects hand their contents to Essentia containers. `REF-0010` corroborates the released constructor's call to the public helper and its copy into constructor-owned storage; this is rank-2 routing evidence, not a numeric gameplay observation.

### C. Attribution and registration

12. **Item and block aspects live in one shared map keyed by an identity hash of the stack.** Registration normalizes the stack to a count of one, hashes its serialized NBT, and stores the list under that integer in a shared map (`api/aspects/AspectEventProxy.java:18-23`, `api/internal/CommonInternals.java:27`, `:44-49`). The ore-dictionary form expands the dictionary into matching stacks, reduces each to a count of one, and registers each copy separately (`api/aspects/AspectEventProxy.java:30-42`). Blocks are not a separate registry: a world block is converted to a stack with its metadata before its aspects are queried (`api/research/ScanAspect.java:44-47`).
13. **Lookup precedence is a fixed ladder of fallbacks.** Item-aspect lookup tries the exact stack identity, then the wildcard-damage form, then — only when the query itself is the wildcard damage value — the first sixteen damage values, then both forms again with NBT stripped, and only then generates aspects from recipes; bonus tags are merged on top, the merged list is culled to seven entries inside the bonus-tag computation, and the result is then clamped to five hundred per amount (`common/lib/crafting/ThaumcraftCraftingManager.java:125-182` with `:367` and `:168`). An empty input stack has no aspects (`:130-132`).
14. **Recipe-derived generation is recursive, guarded, and cached back into the map.** Generation walks the crucible, infusion, then crafting recipe categories, refuses to revisit a stack already on its history list, gives up past one hundred history entries, and registers the generated list so later lookups hit the map directly (`common/lib/crafting/ThaumcraftCraftingManager.java:398-433`, `:593-605`).
15. **There is a source-level mismatch between registration and the public existence test.** Registration writes integer identity hashes into the shared item map, while the public existence test reads that same map with a serialized-NBT string key even though the map is typed for integer keys; in this source the read therefore cannot match a registered entry and always reports absence (`api/ThaumcraftApi.java:259-281` against `api/internal/CommonInternals.java:27`, `:44-49`). Two consequences inside this tree: the "already registered" branch of complex registration is unreachable from that call site, and that branch merges each incoming aspect against the value already in the map, so it would be a no-op even if reached (`api/aspects/AspectEventProxy.java:53-69`). **Unverified against the shipped jar** (see the status list) — a port must not adopt either behavior before the rank-1 or rank-2 check.
16. **Bonus and derived tags can replace, not just add to, the base list.** For an item that implements the Essentia-container-item interface and does not opt out, the stored contents replace the base tags; for items generally, contributions from armor, swords, bows, tools, shears and hoes, dye-named ore entries, and enchantments are merged in (`common/lib/crafting/ThaumcraftCraftingManager.java:184-256`, `:362-367`; the interface and its reference implementation are `api/aspects/IEssentiaContainerItem.java:14-22` and `api/items/ItemGenericEssentiaContainer.java:17`, `:41-59`). This is why a filled phial and an empty one are different attribution problems.

    `REF-0009` now corroborates this container-precedence branch against the shipped BETA26 bytecode, and [fnd-04-container-lookup-evidence.md](fnd-04-container-lookup-evidence.md) records the narrow port repair and deterministic GameTests. This raises only that branch to rank-2 inspection support. FND-04 remains `ACTIVE`; rank-1 gameplay, other attribution paths, capacities, and scanning/smelter consumer agreement remain pending.

    `REF-0010` separately corroborates the released public routing: `AspectHelper.getObjectAspects` delegates through the internal handler, whose item method delegates to `ThaumcraftCraftingManager.getObjectTags`. [fnd-04-public-lookup-evidence.md](fnd-04-public-lookup-evidence.md) records the port's raw/computed split and deterministic consumer checks. This does not promote registration fidelity, generated recipes, or scanning progression to parity.
17. **Entity aspects come from a name-plus-optional-NBT table, and the scan does not stop at the first match.** The shared entity list is populated from the entity registration API and the mod's own config table; the lookup scans the list, skips entries whose NBT conditions do not match, and keeps assigning, so with overlapping registrations the last matching entry supplies the list, and no match yields nothing at all (`api/aspects/AspectHelper.java:76-108`, `api/internal/CommonInternals.java:23`, `api/ThaumcraftApi.java:343-361`, `:375-378`, `common/config/ConfigAspects.java:44-128`).
18. **Players are special-cased and deterministic per name.** A player entity gets a fixed amount of the human aspect plus three draws of a fixed amount from the entire aspect registry in registration order, seeded from the player name hash; the three draws are independent, so a repeated pick accumulates (`api/aspects/AspectHelper.java:79-86`). A port that randomizes per session would diverge observably.
19. **The registration lifecycle and the two API surfaces.** The mod's post-init clears the shared item-aspect map, registers its item and entity tables, and then posts the aspect registry event carrying a fresh registration proxy (`common/config/ConfigAspects.java:35-42`; the event and its single proxy field are `api/aspects/AspectRegistryEvent.java:10-17`). The proxy's methods are the supported surface for addons; the equivalent static helpers on the public API are marked deprecated and not uniform: most construct a new proxy per call, one is an empty crash-guard stub, and the entity-registration helper appends directly to the shared entity list (`api/aspects/AspectEventProxy.java:18-69`, `api/ThaumcraftApi.java:283-341`, `:300-301`, `:364-378`).

### D. Container contracts (interface level only)

20. **The aspect container contract is amount-based and states no capacity.** `IAspectContainer` exposes read and write of the whole list, an accept test for one aspect, an add that returns the amount that could not be inserted, takes that return success, a contain-amount test and a contain-count read, plus two deprecated bulk variants (`api/aspects/IAspectContainer.java:13-78`). There is no numeric cap anywhere in the interface, so "capacity" is entirely implementation-owned and observable only in game (item F5 below).
21. **An aspect source is a container with one extra gate.** `IAspectSource` adds a single blocked flag on top of the container contract (`api/aspects/IAspectSource.java:13-19`). That flag is what the Essentia source search checks before every take and every contain test, so a blocked source is skipped rather than drained (`common/lib/events/EssentiaHandler.java:44-58`, `:73-83`, `:170-180`, `:195-205`).
22. **The item-side mirror is NBT-backed and has an opt-out.** `IEssentiaContainerItem` reads and writes a list on an item stack and exposes a flag for items whose stored fill must not count as their aspects — labelled containers being the motivating case (`api/aspects/IEssentiaContainerItem.java:14-22`). Device code also reads labels and tube filters through this interface; that usage is device scope and is left to the `ALC` notes.
23. **The transport contract is face-based and suction-driven.** `IEssentiaTransport` declares per-face connectability, input and output permission, typed suction with a type, an amount, and a minimum threshold, take and add calls that return how much actually moved, and per-face type and amount reads (`api/aspects/IEssentiaTransport.java:11-91`). Twenty files in the tree reference it; the readers are the transport device tiles and the tubes, which are out of scope here and belong to the `ALC-02` audit.

### E. Lookup consumers

24. **Scanning reads item and entity aspects through the same helpers, and only the sign of one amount gates it.** The aspect scan thing resolves the observed object to entity aspects or item aspects and succeeds only when the amount of the one aspect it represents is positive; on success it grants observation knowledge in three categories (`api/research/ScanAspect.java:31-62`). The scan pipeline around it is described in [rsr-01-evidence.md](rsr-01-evidence.md) and is not repeated here.
25. **The client overlays are client-computed reads of the same data.** With goggles worn, the pointed block's container is read and drawn; the Thaumometer's client tick records the pointed entity so the same drawing routine can show its aspects; a separate block-tag display list is initialized but never populated in this tree (`client/lib/events/RenderEventHandler.java:328-345`, `:770`, `:359-400`, `:446-456`, `common/items/tools/ItemThaumometer.java:56-68`). Nothing in the overlay path is authoritative, which is why the client/server agreement item is listed as unverified.
26. **The Essentia entry points are five calls with a cached, distance-ordered source list.** Draining moves exactly one unit from the nearest source that accepts the take and reports whether anything moved; a failed or exhausted pass removes the cached list and suppresses re-search for ten seconds; adding fills an accepting source that already holds Essentia first (`:116-138`, success when the container add leaves no leftover, `:132-134`) and defers accepting but empty sources (`:128-130`) to a second pass used only when no non-empty source accepted (`:139-155`); presence and accept-ability are separate queries, though the accept query's uncached fallback answers from the presence lookup (`:187-208`); sources are found by a spatial scan around the caller, ordered by distance, and can be invalidated per tile; there is also a two-phase drain-and-confirm path used by output devices (`common/lib/events/EssentiaHandler.java:31-62`, `:64-106`, `:108-159`, `:161-185`, `:187-208`, `:210-285`).
27. **The API bridge to that machinery is broken in this tree.** The helper reflects on the handler for four-parameter drain and find methods, while the handler declares only five- and six-parameter forms, so the reflective lookup fails, is caught, logs a warning, and returns false; nothing in the tree calls the helper (`api/aspects/AspectSourceHelper.java:24-57` against `common/lib/events/EssentiaHandler.java:31-35`, `:161`). **Unverified against the shipped jar**; any port that wires Essentia through the documented helper would need the signature corrected.

## Rank-1 capture checklist

Reference setup for every step: a clean 1.12.2 instance whose only mods are the BETA26 jar and Baubles (see `REF-0002` and the "Environment feasibility" section of [reference-catalog.md](reference-catalog.md)); record the profile, world seed, and game mode in a new `REF` entry before starting. Single-player first, then the multiplayer pass. No operator commands are needed for this work item; the FND-04 observations are ordinary play with a Thaumometer, goggles, and Essentia apparatus.

- **F1 — Attribution of a fixed item set.** Scan a short fixed list covering different registration forms — cobblestone, sand, a log, an ore-bearing stone, a dye, a filled potion — and record the aspect names and amounts shown for each, then compare them against the seeded amounts the source lists for those keys (items 12-16). Re-scan one of them: the display should not change, which is the aspect-side echo of the scan rules in [rsr-01-evidence.md](rsr-01-evidence.md).
- **F2 — Shared registration forms.** Scan several items that share one ore-dictionary registration (all log types, then all plank types or all dyes) and record whether they display identically; this is the observable half of the wildcard and dictionary fallbacks (items 12-13).
- **F3 — Entities and players.** Point the Thaumometer at a cow, a zombie, and a second player and record each display; repeat the player observation in a second session under the same name to test the name-seeded determinism (items 17-18).
- **F4 — Scanned-versus-smelted agreement.** Scan one item and record its displayed aspects, then smelt the same item and record the Essentia produced in the same session. This is the direct rank-1 counterpart of the defect sentence "scans and Essentia handling can disagree on item aspects"; the smelter half is `ALC-01` scope, so record only what is needed for the comparison (items 13-15, 26).
- **F5 — Container refusal.** Fill a jar or alembic from a source until it will accept no more and record the amount shown at refusal — the capacity the interfaces deliberately do not state (item 20). Jars and alembics are `ALC-02` scope; this is the one number FND-04 needs from them.
- **F6 — Transport mechanics.** Watch a jar-to-altar or smelter-to-jar chain for a fixed interval and record units moved per second and whether a jar sealed with a jar brace is skipped rather than drained — sealing sets the blocked flag (`common/blocks/essentia/BlockJar.java:178-179`, tested at `common/tiles/essentia/TileJarFillable.java:217-219`), a jar label instead sets an aspect filter without blocking (`common/tiles/essentia/TileJarFillable.java:122-124`), and mirrors are never blocked (`common/tiles/devices/TileMirrorEssentia.java:289-291`) (items 21, 26). Rate and blocking are the only transport facts FND-04 needs; full tube semantics belong to `ALC-02`.
- **F7 — Client and server agreement.** With two clients in front of the same filled container, confirm both overlays agree with each other and with the contents after a re-log, and confirm that a client showing an aspect cannot make the server credit that aspect to a scan (item 25).
