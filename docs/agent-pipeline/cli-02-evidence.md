## Reference behavior (source-derived)

### A. The caster item's client surface

1. **The caster has no custom client renderer or render layer; its on-screen identity is a model-predicate variant plus an item-colour tint.** The item's constructor adds a `focus` property override that reports one when a focus is fitted and zero when not, and the override body is client-annotated (`common/items/casters/ItemCaster.java:68-77`); the client colour handler then tints every tint index above zero with the fitted focus's colour (`client/ColorHandler.java:190-195`), while the focus items themselves are tinted by their own colour (`client/ColorHandler.java:196-203`; the colour comes from `common/items/casters/ItemFocus.java:38`). A whole-tree sweep for an item renderer or tile-entity-item renderer binding for the caster finds none — the only references to the caster item in the client tree are the colour handler's import and its tint lambda (`client/ColorHandler.java:33`, `:191`), and no class anywhere calls the item-stack renderer setter (zero hits tree-wide). The caster's geometry and textures are therefore the asset lane's, while the wiring that makes one caster look different from another with a different focus fitted is this lane's.

2. **The first-person story is: held as a 3D item, no casting animation beyond the arm swing, and a bow use-action that is never entered.** The item renders in 3D (`common/items/casters/ItemCaster.java:101-104`). The right-click cast path is instantaneous: it stamps the cooldown from the focus's activation time, and on the server consumes vis and runs the package, swinging the arm (`:379-403`, the swing at `:399`; the picker branch swings too, `:352`). The bow use-action and its long duration (`:405-411`) are unreachable: nothing in the caster or foci packages ever puts the item into an active-hand state (whole-package search for the active-hand and using-tick hooks finds none), so the vanilla bow pose never plays. There is no hand glow, no charge overlay on the item, and no first-person beam anchor drawn by the item; the only persistent caster readout is the HUD dial and its radial focus picker, which are [cas-03-evidence.md](cas-03-evidence.md)'s surfaces (its item 21 records the aura push behind the dial; [cli-01-evidence.md](cli-01-evidence.md) item 7 handed the radial HUD and the plan overlay to that note as well).

3. **Every cast's visual origin is a hand-height, side-offset point, and the bolt's colour and width are computed per package.** The cast source vector is built from the caster's position plus a yaw-rotated lateral offset of about two- to three-tenths of a block (the sign flips with which hand holds the caster) and eye height minus four-tenths (`common/items/casters/ItemCaster.java:360-377`), and it is consumed as the trajectory source of the root medium (`api/casters/FocusMediumRoot.java:54-56`). The bolt medium then averages the package's effect colours and sends the average RGB together with the package power times two-thirds as the beam's width (`common/items/casters/foci/FocusMediumBolt.java:56-69`) — so beam thickness is a direct function of the focus's power setting, not a constant.

### B. Beams

4. **The caster's bolt beam is a three-tick polycylinder with a fixed tessellation, two concentric cones per draw, and a sine-jittered path whose step count comes from the distance.** The bolt class builds a polyline at construction: it stores the source-to-target delta, sets a three-tick life (`client/fx/beams/FXBolt.java:59`), derives a length figure as the target vector's length times pi (`:62-63`), and walks one point per integer step with three sine displacements at different divisors plus a per-step random jitter of one-tenth (`:66-81`). Each render re-derives the points from a stored seed so the jaggedness is stable per bolt, with the sine amplitude growing with the particle's age and one point in four thinning its radius (`:94-118`). The draw binds `textures/misc/essentia.png` (`:45`), disables depth writes, uses additive blending and no culling (`:134-137`), clamps alpha to fade from one down to a floor of one-tenth (`:139`), scales the stored widths by one-tenth for the outer cone and a third of that for an inner cone, and pushes both through the bundled polycylinder helper with tessellation set to five (`:152-160`). The packet that fires it carries only source, target, colour, and width (`common/lib/network/fx/PacketFXZap.java:23-28`), and its handler hands the colour straight to the dispatcher's bolt entry point (`:49-52`).

5. **Arc lightning is a different particle: a gravity-curved path of up to fifty segments, each spawning its own trail puff with a two-to-three-tick delay, drawn as two crossed ribbons.** The arc binds `textures/misc/beamh.png` (`client/fx/beams/FXArc.java:40`), also lives three ticks (`:53`), and marches from source toward target under a fixed gravity constant (`:57`) with noise jitter (`:58`, `:69`), stopping at fifty steps (`:66`). Per step it also spawns a generic trail particle with a thirty-to-fifty-tick life, colours boosted threefold and clamped, a randomised alpha ramp, additive blending, and a small gravity (`:71-88`), added through the engine's delayed list with a two-to-four-tick stagger (two plus a three-step random, `:89`). The draw builds two perpendicular ribbon strips through the same point list with a lightmap override and a linear alpha fade (`:110-159`). The arc is fired by `PacketFXBlockArc`, whose two constructors accept either an entity or a block as the far end (`common/lib/network/fx/PacketFXBlockArc.java:26-48`) and whose handler draws from the entity to the block centre with a fixed hatch height of one half (`:74-77`).

6. **Both wand-era beam classes are dead weight in this tree and a port must not resurrect them as features.** The two beam particles — the entity-anchored one and the position-anchored one — carry the four classic beam textures and a half-height-plus-quarter offset for the entity variant (`client/fx/beams/FXBeamWand.java:48-80`, textures at `:71-74`, offset at `:75`; `client/fx/beams/FXBeamBore.java:46-80`, textures at `:68-71`), but their only instantiations sit inside the dispatcher's two beam entry points (`client/fx/FXDispatcher.java:795-835`), and a whole-tree search shows no caller of either method and no other reference to either class (`grep -rn "beamCont\|beamBore\|FXBeamWand\|FXBeamBore"` returns only the dispatcher and the two class files). The caster era has no wand beam: the live beams are the bolt (finding 4) and the arc (finding 5).

7. **The homing-shard beam trail is orphaned: the shard entity is neither registered nor ever constructed, so its six-point trailing beam never appears.** The shard class keeps a list of its last six positions and pushes a new head entry every update (`common/entities/projectile/EntityHomingShard.java:129-132`), and its renderer draws that list as a billboard sprite tinted a fixed magenta first, then the chained beam segments from the entity toward the player's view point (`client/renderers/entity/projectile/RenderHomingShard.java:44-85`, the billboard at `:44-58` and the segment chain at `:60-85`, texture `textures/misc/beaml.png` at `:104`). But the entity registry carries no homing-shard entry (the registration list in `common/config/ConfigEntities.java:90` names the eldritch orb, `:92` the golem orb, and `:110` the spellbat, and no shard), the renderer is referenced by nothing but itself, and no code constructs the entity (whole-tree search for the class name returns only the class and its renderer, ten lines total). The "warden beam" that a port might expect is therefore not a live BETA26 surface: the warden's ranged attack is the eldritch orb entity (finding 8's sibling, spawned in `common/entities/monster/boss/EntityEldritchWarden.java:330-346`, with its arm-lift status bytes at `:334` and its client reaction at `:362-369`) plus the sonic ripple (finding 9).

8. **The bore's beam is alive, but it is drawn by the bore's entity renderer rather than by the dead beam particle.** The renderer gates on the entity's client digging flag, activity, and a valid inventory (`client/renderers/entity/construct/RenderArcaneBore.java:50`), positions the beam origin at the eye plus a rotated lateral offset (`:58-63`), stamps a rotating node-texture billboard at the origin (`:64-67`), and then draws the beam itself from `textures/misc/beam1.png` (`:74`) as three quads rotated sixty degrees apart around a fixed five-block length with a scrolling V coordinate and a green additive tint at four-tenths opacity (`:97-110`). The bore entity's own beam fields are the inverse case — two fields are declared, nulled in the constructor, and never read or written again (`common/entities/construct/EntityArcaneBore.java:77-78`, `:99-100`), which is the same class of dead field [aut-03-evidence.md](aut-03-evidence.md) already records for the bore's base model — so the digging feedback a port must reproduce is the renderer's beam plus the dig packets: the bore sends a dig packet per target (`common/entities/construct/EntityArcaneBore.java:177`), whose handler re-issues the particle burst once per delay tick through the client runnable list (`common/lib/network/fx/PacketFXBoreDig.java:76-83`; the runnable list refuses to queue on the server, `common/lib/events/ServerEvents.java:410-415`), and the burst itself mixes a sparkle one time in four with block-textured particles otherwise, at fifty emissions divided by the delay (`client/fx/FXDispatcher.java:837-850`). The dead bore-trail entry point (`client/fx/FXDispatcher.java:857-860`, zero callers) is a leftover of the same cut feature as the beam classes.

9. **The remaining two beam-family visuals are the sonic ripple and the wisp zap.** The sonic effect is a single quad sampled from the bundled hemisphere OBJ (`client/fx/other/FXSonic.java:103`) that binds a ripple texture whose frame steps through fifteen images over its life (`:70-71`), anchors itself at the target's eye each tick (`:90-100`), orients by the target's head yaw and pitch, pushes itself two body-heights forward, and scales by target size with a mirrored Z (`:77-80`); its life is the constructor's age plus up to half again (`:38`), and both the warden and the guardian send it on their sonic and screech attacks with a thirty-two-block radius (`common/entities/monster/boss/EntityEldritchWarden.java:348`, `common/entities/monster/EntityEldritchGuardian.java:264`; handler `common/lib/network/fx/PacketFXSonic.java:34-42`). The wisp's zap is a plain bolt with the wisp's aspect colour (`common/lib/network/fx/PacketFXWispZap.java:41-59`, colour at `:50-55`), sent when the wisp attacks, at a thirty-two-block radius (`common/entities/monster/EntityWisp.java:181`); its death and first-tick bursts and its every-other-tick ambient motes are client-side (finding 15).

### C. The particle pipeline

10. **There is exactly one client FX gateway, and it is bound to the client world.** The dispatcher is a single-instance class whose instance is created in its static block (`client/fx/FXDispatcher.java:55`, `:1017-1020`); its world accessor resolves through the client handler's client object (`:60-62`), which is the structural reason every call into it has to be client-side (finding 22). It exposes seventy public entry points — a scripted count over the class — ranging from single-particle helpers to the beam and stream factories, and its inner part-specification class defaults to a sixty-four-cell sprite grid, no age, unit alpha and scale, a one-percent-per-tick slowdown, and layer zero (`:1022-1066`).

11. **The mod ships its own particle engine rather than using vanilla's, with six layers, per-dimension lists, a hard cap, and a frame-rate-aware skip.** The engine is a client event subscriber (`client/fx/ParticleEngine.java:33-34`) holding two parallel system sets: six layer maps of per-dimension particle lists (`:314-318`) and a delayed list (`:317`), with its own atlas, `assets/thaumcraft/textures/misc/particles.png` (`:315`). World-space layers zero through three draw on the render-world-last event (`:124-210`): layer zero additive and layer one normal, layers two and three the same pair with the depth test switched off in between and restored after their draw (`:136-158`, `:195-201`), each layer flushing its own draw call. The two GUI layers draw on the render-tick event instead (`:48-120`), with layer four additive and layer five normal (`:76-85`) — that pair is the pipeline a screen-space effect uses (finding 13's last entry). Ticking runs on the client tick (`:249-312`), where the delayed list is counted down and released only into its own dimension (`:259-273`), and each layer's particles update with a crash-report wrapper that expires a throwing particle instead of crashing the game (`:284-303`). The admission gate is threefold (`:216-241`): a cap of two thousand, one thousand, or five hundred particles by the vanilla particle setting (`:216-218`), an increment when the debug frame rate drops below thirty (`:224-228`), and a random drop whose odds follow the threshold in thirds (`:229`) — none while the frame rate holds at the default particle setting, one new particle in three at the reduced setting, and two in three at the minimal one; below thirty frames per second the threshold steps up one, so each setting gains a third more discards — one in three at the default, two in three at the reduced, and the minimal setting admits nothing new (`:229`, with the delayed path re-entering the same gate on release at `:243-245` and `:259-273`). At the cap, the oldest particle of that layer and dimension is evicted (`:236-238`).

12. **Nearly every effect is one generic sprite-sheet particle whose look is assembled from keyframe arrays, and its frame, alpha, and scale are interpolated from those arrays before every draw.** The generic particle's defaults are a sixty-four cell grid, unit alpha and scale, a ninety-eight-hundredths slowdown, and a tenth-block size (`client/fx/particles/FXGeneric.java:43-110`); on its first tick it expands the alpha and scale key arrays into per-age frame tables by linear interpolation (`:112-140`). Motion is the vanilla-plus schedule minus a gravity term scaled by the particle's gravity factor, with a per-tick gaussian random movement, a wind bias, and a ground friction pair (`:142-168`). Sprite selection either loops over the frame count at the configured increment or walks it once across the life, and a final-frame override list can replace the last frames before expiry (`:171-184`); the drawn cell is the sprite index over the grid size (`:198-202`), the tint lerps from start to end colour over the life (`:216-218`), and an angled mode draws the quad in world-oriented rotation instead of the billboard path (`:225-230`). The setters used by every recipe — age, sprite range, scale keys, alpha keys, slowdown, gravity, grid, layer, and no-clip — are defined in one block (`:274-376`), with the flip setter defined outside it (`:194`).

13. **The dispatcher's recipes are where the felt numbers live: each entry point fixes its own sprite, count, spread, life, blend layer, and colour rules.** Representative derivations, all read at the source: the taint puff fades from three-quarters alpha over an eighty-to-hundred-tick life with gravity pulling it down and a near-unit slowdown (`client/fx/FXDispatcher.java:77-90`); the lightning flash is a five-to-ten-tick particle using sprite 108 and its neighbours, with colour and scale supplied by the caller (`:92-103`); the pollution puff drifts with near-zero horizontal motion, lives a hundred to a hundred-sixty ticks, and carries a magenta tint with a wind bias (`:193-209`); the focus cloud puff fades in and out at a two-thirds peak over twenty to thirty ticks while scaling from five-plus to ten-plus units (`:332-346`); the bamf — the mod's general-purpose block poof — emits eight to ten clustered puffs of sprite 123 fading from full to one-tenth alpha at a three-to-seven-unit scale, and its flair half adds two to four wispy motes, a sprite-77 ring that scales from ten units to zero, and two to four curly wisps (`:481-527`; the wispy mote recipe itself is a sixty-four-grid, five-hundred-twelve-frame loop with a two-part alpha ramp, `:356-369`; the curly wisp bends its random walk toward a face when one is given, `:529-554`); the generic burst is a single thirty-one-tick, thirty-one-frame sprite-208 particle (`:781-788`); the block sparkle rains twenty times the box's average edge length, doubled, of sparkle particles per exposed face, with a delay proportional to distance from the cast point (`:211-251`); the sparkle pair is a plain sprite-512 or sprite-320 particle with a randomised alpha ramp (`:253-290`); the vis sparkle flies a green mote toward its target, shrinking inside two blocks and expiring within two-tenths of it (`client/fx/particles/FXVisSparkle.java:17-38`, `:62-96`), and the dispatcher's wrapper colours it by aspect (`client/fx/FXDispatcher.java:615-620`); the streaming essentia, bore, and void trails each own a class (`client/fx/other/FXEssentiaStream.java`, `client/fx/other/FXBoreStream.java`, `client/fx/other/FXVoidStream.java`), as do the void-streak call the siphon makes (`common/tiles/crafting/TileVoidSiphon.java:131`) and the flat mist and vent recipes; and the one screen-space recipe, the GUI sparkle used by the research table and the HUD, routes through the generic particle's GUI subclass on layer four (`client/fx/FXDispatcher.java:292-307`), which keeps a GUI particle's world by the same engine but draws it in the GUI pass ([cli-01-evidence.md](cli-01-evidence.md) owns those screens; this lane owns the particle itself).

### D. The packet family, its handlers, and the trigger cadence

14. **Sixteen FX packets are registered, all clientbound-only, and each handler drives one client visual — most through a dispatcher entry; the sonic and shield packets hand theirs to the vanilla effect renderer, the scan adds straight to the mod engine's delayed list, and the two source packets register into maps the per-tick loops consume.** The family is registered in one block, all sixteen with the client side (`common/lib/network/PacketHandler.java:78-93`), on the same simple-channel instance the mod uses for everything else (`:97`). The mapping, handler by handler: bolt — `PacketFXZap` carries source, target, colour, and width to the bolt particle (`common/lib/network/fx/PacketFXZap.java:49-52`); wisp zap — the same bolt with the wisp's aspect colour (`common/lib/network/fx/PacketFXWispZap.java:41-59`); arc — `PacketFXBlockArc` draws from the sender to the block centre at half block height (`common/lib/network/fx/PacketFXBlockArc.java:74-77`); slash — `PacketFXSlash` draws a short-lived plane between two entity ids (`common/lib/network/fx/PacketFXSlash.java:38-48` and `client/fx/FXDispatcher.java:917-920`), with a self-lookup branch for the player id (`common/lib/network/fx/PacketFXSlash.java:50-53`); sonic — `PacketFXSonic` spawns the ripple at the source entity (`common/lib/network/fx/PacketFXSonic.java:34-42`); shield — `PacketFXShield` spawns the rune model, with three sentinel target values selecting fixed yaw or pitch orientations (`common/lib/network/fx/PacketFXShield.java:39-79`); ### E. Focus visuals

17. **The delivery mediums split the visual load between a point burst and a line, and the split is readable straight off the packets.** Touch sends the focus-effect packet at the trajectory source with half the direction vector as motion (`common/items/casters/foci/FocusMediumTouch.java:96-107`, send at `:104`); its handler splits the part list, divides ten by the number of parts with a floor of one, and calls each effect element's packet-side render hook once per unit with the motion jittered by a twentieth (`common/lib/network/fx/PacketFXFocusEffect.java:76-87`). Projectile, cloud, mine, and spellbat each spawn an entity and own their per-tick look on the entity side (findings 19-21). ### F. Client-only discipline, gating flags, multiplayer, and reload

22. **Every FX call site is client-side by construction, and the only unguarded ones resolve to client-only callbacks.** A scripted classification of all one hundred forty direct dispatcher call sites outside the dispatcher itself (eighty-nine files) sorts them as — with the buckets overlapping at the edges, so the counts describe accesses rather than a partition: one hundred within the classifier's eighty-line scan window of an explicit remote-side check, sixty-eight of them inside the enclosing method body itself; nine in the FX packet handlers, which are registered clientbound (finding 14); six in classes under `client/` (the client-only packages); thirteen inside the vanilla random-tick display callbacks, which the client invokes only; fourteen reached only from the champion-modifier effect hooks (called from a client living-tick event, `client/lib/events/RenderEventHandler.java:607-613`, and the event subscribers themselves are client-annotated); three inside methods whose single client gate sits at or beyond the scan window before the call (`common/tiles/crafting/TileInfusionMatrix.java:262-263`, its effect pass; `common/tiles/misc/TileHole.java:45`; `common/entities/EntityFluxRift.java:205`, whose drawing branch is the else of that remote check); and five that are unguarded in place but never reachable on a server — the bottled-taint status callback, which is the vanilla entity-status receipt, and the three focus-element render hooks invoked only from the FX paths (finding 18); the same shape recurs in the golem's status-byte handler, the homing shard's own receipt, and the focus projectile's particle callback (`common/golems/EntityThaumcraftGolem.java:311-324`, `common/entities/projectile/EntityHomingShard.java:121`, `common/entities/projectile/EntityFocusProjectile.java:245`), all client-annotated or reached only from render paths. The dispatcher itself has no side annotation and its static instance is constructed at class load (`client/fx/FXDispatcher.java:55`, `:1017-1020`), so the port's risk is not the tree's pending crashes but a new call in a common path; the acceptance's "no client-only crash" is exactly this property, and T8 exercises it.

23. **The flag named in the graphics config for stress is a sound switch, not an FX switch, and the particle pipeline is gated by the vanilla particle setting and the frame rate instead.** The graphics configuration class declares, with its own explanatory comments, the large-tag text toggle, the shader disable switch, the no-stress switch (documented there as turning off anxiety triggers such as the heartbeat sound), the crooked-label switch, the dial-position switch, the tag-display switch, the blue-biome switch, and the golem-emote switch (documented there as controlling whether golems show emote particles when they receive orders or hit trouble), all defaulting off except the crooked labels and the emotes (`common/config/ModConfig.java:290-292`, `:295-309`, defaults at `:312-319`). What each actually gates for this lane: no-stress suppresses the warp-anxiety sounds and the heartbeat, not particles (`common/lib/events/WarpEvents.java:71`, `:76`; `common/lib/network/misc/PacketMiscEvent.java:69`, whose type-zero case is the heartbeat and is the one place the flag is read on the client); the golem emote switch gates the golem's status-byte emotes, which are particle-side effects (`common/golems/EntityThaumcraftGolem.java:452-459`, `:630`); the shader switch gates the shader helper and the screen shader pass (`client/lib/ender/ShaderHelper.java:51`, `client/lib/events/RenderEventHandler.java:298`) — those two are other lanes' surfaces but are the config rows a visual-parity pass will toggle; and the actual particle gates are the engine's own (finding 11): the vanilla particle setting changes both the cap and the drop odds, and a debug frame rate below thirty shifts the drop threshold up a step, adding a third more discards at every setting — the default starts thinning at one in three, and only the minimal setting ends fully dark (finding 11).

24. **For visuals, nothing is authoritative and nothing is stored: the packets are transient broadcasts and the per-tick half is reconstructed per client.** Every FX packet is sent to all players within a radius of the event — sixty-four blocks for the focus cast and impact packets (`common/items/casters/foci/FocusMediumBolt.java:69`, `common/items/casters/foci/FocusEffectFire.java:53` and the six sibling impact senders), the slash (`common/lib/events/ToolEvents.java:93`), the bamf senders (`common/entities/EntityFluxRift.java:467`), and the scan, which is sent to the scanning player only (`common/lib/events/ToolEvents.java:123`); thirty-two for the wisp zap (`common/entities/monster/EntityWisp.java:181`), the arcs (`common/entities/monster/boss/EntityEldritchWarden.java:234`, `common/entities/monster/boss/EntityCultistPortalGreater.java:142`, `common/tiles/crafting/TileInfusionMatrix.java:473`), the mist (`common/tiles/devices/TileLampGrowth.java:115`), the shield (`common/lib/events/EntityEvents.java:169`), the essentia source (`common/lib/events/EssentiaHandler.java:55`), and the infusion source (`common/tiles/crafting/TileInfusionMatrix.java:521`). A second client inside the radius therefore sees the same one-shot event with its own particle settings and its own frame-rate gate (finding 23); a client outside the radius sees nothing at all, and no packet carries persistent state — the only FX state that outlives a tick is the client-side source map for the essentia trail, and it is emptied as it is drawn (`client/lib/events/RenderEventHandler.java:159-170`). The FX packets never carry gameplay authority: they are draw instructions whose reception changes no inventory, aura value, or damage.

25. **Reload tells the same story: no effect state is persisted or replayed, and the engine's stores are per-dimension and per-process.** The particle lists are dimension-keyed maps held in static fields (`client/fx/ParticleEngine.java:38`, `:314-318`) that only the current dimension is ticked and drawn for (`:258`, `:267`, `:73`, `:137`), and the delayed list releases an entry only if its dimension matches the current one (`:266`). Because the client only creates particles from packets it receives live (finding 14) or from state it re-derives each tick (finding 15), a player who logs in after a cast sees nothing of it, and a re-logged player sees no replay; whether the maps are also cleared when a client switches dimension inside one session is a capture question, as is whether a stale entry keyed by a dimension id can survive a switch and redraw (checklist T8).
