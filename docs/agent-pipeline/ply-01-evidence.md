## Reference behavior (source-derived)

### A. The warp capability: three pools, one counter, one sync

1. **The player's warp is one capability holding three integers and a gain counter, and every pool clamps at five hundred.** The warp capability is registered at pre-init beside the knowledge capability, through the proxy's pre-init call into the capability class (`proxies/CommonProxy.java:59`; registration and storage adapter at `common/lib/capabilities/PlayerWarp.java:22-34`), backed by a default implementation whose state is an integer array sized by the warp-type enum and a counter field (`common/lib/capabilities/PlayerWarp.java:36-43`). The enum is the three pool names in order — permanent, normal, temporary (`api/capabilities/IPlayerWarp.java:53-55`) — and the read, set, add, and reduce operations all index that array, with set, add, and reduce each clamping the result into the zero-to-five-hundred range (`common/lib/capabilities/PlayerWarp.java:51-69`). The interface documents that callers should prefer the internal method handler's add-warp call over touching the capability directly (`api/capabilities/IPlayerWarp.java:9-16`). Access from gameplay code is a static capability lookup on the player (`api/capabilities/ThaumcraftCapabilities.java:97`, `:102-105`).

2. **The pool and counter state is what persists, and it persists whole.** The serialized form is the three-integer array plus the counter under two keys (`common/lib/capabilities/PlayerWarp.java:76-81`), and the deserializer clears first, copies whichever prefix of the array the saved data holds, and restores the counter (`:83-99`). The counter's accessors are its own pair (`:101-109`), and the capability's provider exposes exactly the one capability, named for the mod and the warp role (`:112-143`, the name at `:140-142`).

3. **Every change is pushed to the owner's client, on a full-state packet at the end and a delta packet per change.** The capability's own sync sends the whole serialized state to the player's client (`common/lib/capabilities/PlayerWarp.java:71-74`), and the client applies it by deserializing into its local copy of the same capability (`common/lib/network/playerdata/PacketSyncWarp.java:37-47`, the sender's read of the source state at `:24-27`). The delta path is the internal method handler, described next; nothing in the tree sends another player's warp anywhere.

4. **The one write choke point pays the counter and wakes the book.** Every gameplay write goes through the internal method handler's add-warp entry, which refuses remote-side and zero-amount calls, floors a reduction so a pool can never go negative rather than over-subtracting, writes the pool, and then sends a per-type change packet to the player — one packet type each for permanent, normal, and temporary (`common/lib/InternalMethodHandler.java:40-58`). On any positive change it resets the counter to the sum of all three pools — so the counter is a gain stamp, not an independent accumulator (`:59-61`) — and on any non-temporary change it force-completes the warp research for a player who already knows the first-steps research and not the warp one, with a status line (`:62-65`); the final step pushes the full-state sync (`:66`). The handler also exposes the "actual warp" figure as normal plus permanent, used by the items that gate on how deep a player has gone (`:171-174`).

5. **The change packet is the player-facing feedback, and its three branches are asymmetric.** The packet carries a signed amount and a type byte; on the client, a positive permanent change plays the whisper sound but sends no message (the string it builds is never used), a normal change always sends the sticky gained-or-lost line whichever sign, and a temporary change sends its gained line only when positive — so a temporary loss is silent (`common/lib/network/playerdata/PacketWarpMessage.java:57-85`; the six line keys at `assets/thaumcraft/lang/en_us.lang:1036-1041`). The whisper sound is the same both times it plays (`common/lib/network/playerdata/PacketWarpMessage.java:63-64`, `:72-73`).

6. **The counter arithmetic in the event is the whole felt-frequency model: a gain stamps the counter, and each fired roll spends at least five of it.** Because every positive warp change resets the counter to the current pool sum (finding 4), the gate that follows sees the largest pool total the player ever held at their most recent gain, decaying by at least five, and usually more, per event roll (`common/lib/events/WarpEvents.java:57-62`). The two decay terms the tree uses are named below in finding 8.

### C. The warp event: the scheduler and the roll

11. **The event runs once per two thousand player ticks, server-side, and four gates can stop it.** The player tick handler, inside its server-side branch, calls the warp check at a cadence gated on the player's own lifetime tick counter, and only when wuss mode is off and the player does not hold the warp-ward effect (`common/lib/events/PlayerEvents.java:114-131`, the warp branch at `:328-331`). The same handler runs the death-gaze glance every twenty ticks while that potion is active (`:332-334`; the glance itself `common/lib/events/WarpEvents.java:318-343`). The scheduler's own first act, before any roll, is to spend one temporary warp point through the write choke point — so under the ward the temporary pool does not drain at all, and neither does the counter move (`common/lib/events/WarpEvents.java:47-49`).

12. **The roll: gate on the counter's square root, re-derive the warp figure, spend the counter, and index an effect table with a random value shifted by the carried gear.** After the temporary spend, the check reads the three pools, sums them with the carried figure, and takes the two-pool normal-plus-permanent subtotal as the "actual" warp used by the research grants below (`common/lib/events/WarpEvents.java:50-56`). The gate draws a hundred-sided value and fires only when the counter is positive, the summed warp is positive, and the draw is at most the counter's square root (`:58-59`). When it fires, the warp figure is re-derived as a third of twice the summed warp plus the counter, capped at a hundred (`:60`); the counter then loses the larger of five and twice its own square root less twice the carried gear — so carried gear slows the counter's decay as well as feeding the effect roll (`:61-62`); and the effect index is a random draw below the re-derived warp plus the carried gear, minus, for a fortress helm carrying the mask whose data value is zero, between two and five more (`:63-67`). A client packet signaling the event precedes the effects, and the ambience's creeper and explosion sounds are both gated by the no-stress graphics flag (`:68`, `:70-79`).

### D. Warp Ward

17. **The ward is a beneficial potion that exists only to close the event gate, and its registration says so.** The potion registers with the beneficial flag, a fixed colour, and a registry name; its bad-effect answer is false, its tick effect is empty, and its icon comes from the potion sheet (`Registrar.java:85-100`, the assignment at `:90`; the class `common/lib/potions/PotionWarpWard.java:10-42`, the beneficial answer at `:25-27`, the empty tick effect at `:35-36`). The effect carries twenty-five percent effectiveness and no in-world behavior of its own (`common/lib/potions/PotionWarpWard.java:16-23`).

18. **The ward's every reader in the tree is a gate or a bonus, and its one application point is the bath.** The event scheduler refuses to run while the effect is held (`common/lib/events/PlayerEvents.java:329`), which — per finding 11 — also suspends the temporary drain and the counter, making a bath the only way to pause the whole machine. The bath's own block reads the effect to avoid re-applying to an already-warded player, and applies the effect with a duration of two hundred thousand ticks divided by the square root of the player's permanent warp (the root floored, the divisor floored at one), capped at the potion maximum of thirty-two thousand ticks (`common/blocks/misc/BlockFluidPure.java:43-54`). Worked: the duration divisor is the floor of the square root of the permanent pool, so the thirty-two-thousand-tick cap binds for every permanent warp of forty-eight or below — an untainted or lightly touched player always receives the full ward — and the shrinkage only becomes visible from forty-nine up: at a permanent warp of forty-nine the ward is about twenty-eight thousand five hundred ticks, at a hundred twenty thousand, at two hundred fifty thirteen thousand three hundred, and at the five-hundred pool ceiling about nine thousand ninety (arithmetic from `:44-52`). The third reader is the soap's bonus, below (finding 20), and the fourth is the block's own already-warded check just cited. There is no potion-item path to the ward in this tree: no recipe produces the effect and no other call site applies it, so the bath is its only faucet (the whole-tree reference sweep for the potion class holds the four sites listed, plus the registration and the class itself).

### E. The bath's player-side application, and the soap

19. **The bath is the block lane's derivation; what this lane adds is its application arithmetic and its two interactions.** The purifying fluid blocks warp while a player stands in a source block who is not already warded: it reads the permanent pool, converts the source block to air, and applies the ward with the duration shrinking as the warp grows ([wld-03-evidence.md](wld-03-evidence.md) finding 22, re-read at `common/blocks/misc/BlockFluidPure.java:40-55`). Read from the player's side, three details matter that the block-side derivation does not: the bath's application is the one in the family that does not clear the effect's cure list first — the event's custom-potion applications clear it, while its blurred-vision and vanilla-effect bands do not, and the hunger branch then registers flesh and brains as its replacement cures — so whatever the base potion class lets a player use to cure an effect still works on a bathtub-acquired ward, an engine contract this tree only leans on and the T6 milk probe observes (compare `common/blocks/misc/BlockFluidPure.java:52` against `common/lib/events/WarpEvents.java:84-87`); the duration divisor is the rounded-down square root of the permanent pool, floored at one, so pools that round to the same root receive identical durations (finding 18's arithmetic); and an already-warded player who re-enters the fluid is skipped by the gate, so the bath is not a duration-refresh loop (`common/blocks/misc/BlockFluidPure.java:43`).

It then reduces the normal pool by that amount, and wipes the temporary pool to zero in one call (`:75-80`). The permanent pool is never touched by the soap, and the normal reduction cannot go below zero because the write choke point floors it (finding 4: `common/lib/InternalMethodHandler.java:46-48`). Worked cap: with both bonuses active the soap removes three normal warp per use, and it always zeroes the temporary pool in the same use. The client side plays an effect sound and bubbles over the use and a burst on completion (`common/items/consumables/ItemSanitySoap.java:49-56`, `:82-87`).

### I. Displays, sync, and what a reload must show

33. **The warp gauge is the sanity checker's, not the thaumometer's — held in either hand, it draws a three-segment bar over the summed three pools, saturating at a hundred.** The hand-held HUD dispatch draws from the mainhand and the offhand, in that order: the first slot holding a casting item gets the caster dial, the first holding a thaumometer gets its aura dial, and the first holding the sanity checker gets the warp gauge (`client/lib/events/HudHandler.java:67-112`, the three tests at `:89-107`). The gauge reads the three pools from the client's synced capability, sums them, scales the sum against a hundred when it exceeds it so the drawn bar saturates, and draws three stacked segments in distinct colours — the temporary segment first in a light pink, the normal segment in mid purple, and the permanent one in dark purple — above a gap that shrinks as the total grows; a total at or above the display ceiling adds a jittered overlay icon (`client/lib/events/HudHandler.java:286-334`, the three reads at `:293-295`, the saturation at `:296-302`, the segments at `:303-322`, the overlay at `:327-333`). The sanity checker item itself is a plain one-per-stack uncommon item whose recipe is an arcane crafting gated on the warp research, which is itself force-completed on a player's first non-temporary warp gain (finding 4; `common/items/tools/ItemSanityChecker.java:7-17`; its registration `common/config/ConfigItems.java:215`; the recipe `common/config/ConfigRecipes.java:189`). The thaumometer's own hand-held dial is the aura display and carries no warp figure ([wld-01-evidence.md](wld-01-evidence.md)'s surface).

34. **The operator display surface is the warp command, which can set one pool at a time and add to one pool at a time, with its own quirks.** The command's warp branch takes a player, an add-or-set verb, an amount, and an optional pool word for permanent or temporary, defaulting to normal; add clamps the amount into the minus-hundred-to-hundred range, and the help text describes the shape (`common/lib/CommandThaumcraft.java:114-127`, the clamp at `:120`, the help lines at `:77-79`). Set writes the chosen pool, syncs, and tells both sides; add calls the pool's own additive path, syncs, and additionally fires a permanent-typed change packet with the amount regardless of which pool it mutated — so an operator's temporary or normal addition shows the permanent-warp presentation (`:140-153` for set, `:155-169` for add, the packet quirk at `:166`). Neither verb touches the event counter directly: a set leaves whatever counter the player had, and only a positive non-command write resets it through the choke point (finding 4). The panel that shows a research page's warp warning is the research UI's surface, not this lane's (`client/gui/GuiResearchPage.java:403-411` reads the stage's warp cost, the difficulty names at `assets/thaumcraft/lang/en_us.lang:1043-1047`; cross-referenced to the research lane).

35. **What syncs and what a second client sees.** The whole warp state syncs to its owner on join, beside the knowledge sync — the join handler pushes both capabilities' full state server-side (`common/lib/events/PlayerEvents.java:373-386`) — and again after every change through the choke point and the delta packet (findings 3-5). The delta presentation is client-local: the status lines and the whisper sound play for the changing player only, and the gauge reads only the local player's capability, so a second client sees nothing of another player's warp on any screen the tree provides (`client/lib/events/HudHandler.java:293-295` reads the local player; `common/lib/network/playerdata/PacketSyncWarp.java:42-44` applies to the local player).

36. **A reload, a death, or a dimension change must keep the pools and the counter.** The pools and the counter are serialized with the player's data (finding 2); the dimension-change and respawn path re-copies the serialized knowledge and warp state from the old player entity onto the new one, catching and logging on failure (`common/lib/events/PlayerEvents.java:388-399`). Because the copy is unconditional in the handler, a death that clones the player keeps all three pools, delivered to the new client by the join sync. Nothing in the tree decays a pool except the event's temporary spend, its clarity refund's single normal point, and the soap, and nothing at all decays the counter except the event's own arithmetic — so a player who never triggers the event keeps their counter indefinitely. The temporary pool's one-point spend is itself gated by the ward and by wuss mode (finding 11), so those two states freeze the machine entire.

## Rank-1 capture checklist

Reference setup for every step: the clean 1.12.2 profile whose only mods are the BETA26 jar and Baubles (`REF-0002` and the "Environment feasibility" section of [reference-catalog.md](reference-catalog.md)); a fresh creative world with its seed recorded in a new `REF` entry before the session. The levers this lane's own machinery offers are the reload, research, and warp commands (`common/lib/CommandThaumcraft.java:64`, `:89`, `:114`), alongside vanilla's give and summon; because every command write is itself a measurement input, use them BETWEEN measurements only, and record every use, every amount, and every config edit in the `REF` entry — note in particular that neither warp verb arms the event counter — both write the capability directly — so a genuine gameplay gain (a zombie brain, the eldritch curio, or the brain-in-a-jar craft) must stamp the counter before any event can fire (findings 4, 34). Every step below is bounded; stop at the bound even if the sample looks thin.

- **T1 — The gauge and the three pools.** Give yourself a sanity checker (its recipe unlocks with the warp research, so give it directly and note the use), then set each pool to a distinct value in turn — for example permanent ten, normal twenty, temporary thirty — and read the bar after each write: three segments, their order and colours, and which segment moves when you change which pool. Then drive the summed total past a hundred and record the saturation and the overlay (finding 33). Bound: four readings.

- **T2 — The temporary drain and the cadence gate.** With the checker in hand and a temporary pool of five, record the temporary segment shrinking by one roughly every hundred seconds while the ward is absent, then bathe for the ward and record that the segment stops decaying entirely for the ward's duration (findings 11, 18). Bound: two ten-minute windows — one unwarded, one warded.

- **T3 — The event roll through a real wait.** Set normal warp to a large value with an add, then take a genuine gain to stamp the counter — eat a zombie brain or use the eldritch curio, per the setup note — and hold position for a bounded window, recording every event that fires: sounds, heartbeat, fog, status lines, potion icons, and any spawned creature. Compare each against the table in finding 13 and record which indices you can identify, then note which you cannot and why (some lines are rare; the table is wide). Bound: twenty minutes or six fired events, whichever first.

- **T4 — Carried warp, the scan slots, and the gear families.** Read the warping tooltip on one item from each family — a void tool, the void robe, a cultist piece, the crimson blade, the primal crusher, and the voidseer charm — and record each figure against finding 10; then raise permanent warp and record the charm's tooltip figure changing as its discount scales (finding 24). Finally hold a warping item only in the offhand and record that nothing changes on the gauge or in the next event window — the source scan skips the offhand (finding 9; an outcome that contradicts it overrules the source read and is the point of the step). Bound: one item per family, one scaling check, one offhand attempt.

- **T5 — The effect table's player-side effects, sampled deliberately.** In a second bounded window, record specifically: the creeper-primed and distant-explosion sounds and the heartbeat; the fog burst and how long it lasts; at least one potion icon identified from the status bar and its duration; the night-vision band if it appears; the illusion-spiders moment against the real-spiders moment (the illusion variant should not harm the viewer — record what actually attacks); and the count of guardian escorts against the table's one-versus-fraction rule (findings 13-15). Bound: thirty minutes or one observation per listed class.

- **T6 — Warp Ward from the bath.** Obtain bath salts, pour the purifying fluid, and stand in a source block: record the ward icon, the block converted to air, the duration (at zero permanent warp the full thirty-two-thousand ticks — about twenty-seven minutes — is expected), that no event fires and no temporary decay happens inside the window, and that re-entering while warded is skipped (findings 17-19). Then test the cure question: drink milk during the ward and record whether the ward survives — the bath's application leaves the base class's default cures in place where the event's custom-effect applications clear them (finding 19), so removal is the expected observable, and a ward that survives is a source-read correction worth logging. Bound: one bath, one ward window, one milk test.

- **T7 — The soap, the brains, the curios, and the jar.** With a normal pool of at least five: use a sanity soap plainly; use one while warded; use one while warded and standing in the fluid — recording the normal reduction and the temporary wipe each time; eat a brain repeatedly enough to see both its branches, recording which pool moved; use the eldritch-meta curiosity and record the normal-and-temporary grant; use the rites curiosity below the twenty-warp gate and record the refusal plus that the item survives, then at or above the gate and record the grants including whether the permanent point came; craft the brain in a jar and record the normal point arriving with its presentation (findings 8, 20-22). Bound: three soaps, three brains, one curiosity below and one above the gate, one jar.

- **T8 — Reload, death, dimension change, second client, and the negatives.** Set one pool, relog, and read the gauge; die and respawn and read it again; change dimension and read it again — all three should keep the pools and the counter (finding 36). On a LAN pair, have the second client watch while the first gains warp and record that the other screen shows nothing of it, while the first hears the whisper and sees the lines (finding 35).
