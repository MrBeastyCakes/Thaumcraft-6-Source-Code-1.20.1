## Reference behavior (source-derived)

### A. The Arcane Ear

1. **Two variants, one block class, and a tone bound by the block it is mounted against.** The ear is a wood device of hardness one with ten instruments to listen for; the plain variant registers as `arcane_ear`, the toggle as `arcane_ear_toggle`, the latter a bare subclass that only renames the constructor call, and both share one tile class registered beside them (`common/blocks/devices/BlockArcaneEar.java:33-41`; `common/blocks/devices/BlockArcaneEarToggle.java:4-9`; registration `common/config/ConfigBlocks.java:301-302`, tile `:374`). Placement takes the clicked face as the ear's facing — any solid face works, and the placement hook keeps that face verbatim and starts with the output bit off — and the bounding boxes follow the facing (`common/blocks/devices/BlockArcaneEar.java:61-66`, `:112-114`, `:116-138`). The tone it listens for is the instrument grade of the block it is mounted against, recomputed on placement and on every neighbour change: rock reads as the bass drum, sand as the snare, glass as the click, wood as the bass, clay as the flute, gold as the bell, wool as the guitar, packed ice as the chime, bone as the xylophone, anything else as the default harp (`common/tiles/devices/TileArcaneEar.java:101-138`; the ten vanilla instruments in listen order `common/blocks/devices/BlockArcaneEar.java:156-158`). Contradistinction worth carrying into the port: unlike every other device in the set, the ear's placement and neighbour-change hooks are overridden without the super call, so the shared device base's powered-mirror never runs on it (`common/blocks/devices/BlockArcaneEar.java:68-74`, `:76-86` versus the base `common/blocks/BlockTCDevice.java:44-52`) — a lever on the ear never disables it, because its output bit is its own pulse/latch state. A right-click cycles the pitch through twenty-five steps and immediately sounds the mounted block's instrument at the ear; losing the support drops the ear back as its item (`common/blocks/devices/BlockArcaneEar.java:88-98`, `:76-86`; the pitch step `common/tiles/devices/TileArcaneEar.java:140-143`). As a redstone source it reports fifteen on both the weak and the strong query while its output bit is set, on every side (`common/blocks/devices/BlockArcaneEar.java:100-110`; the bit's metadata position `common/blocks/BlockTCDevice.java:91-115`, the read helper `common/lib/utils/BlockStateUtils.java:28-30`).

2. **The listening loop: a per-tick mailbox of note-block plays, matched by tone and pitch within sixty-four blocks, then a half-second pulse or a latch flip.** Every server-side note-block play is recorded by the mod's world-events hook as a position, instrument, and note entry in a static per-dimension list — the hook refuses the client side, keys by dimension, and appends (`common/lib/events/WorldEvents.java:60-70`; the map's declaration and initialization `common/tiles/devices/TileArcaneEar.java:26`, `:184-186`) — and the same world tick's end clears the dimension's list, so the mailbox lives exactly one server tick (`common/lib/events/ServerEvents.java:152-156`). Each ear, every server tick, scans that list for an entry whose instrument equals its tone, whose note equals its pitch, and whose block sits within 4096 squared distance (a sixty-four-block radius), and on the first match re-plays the note at the ear and either sets a ten-tick countdown plus the output bit (plain variant) or flips the output bit (toggle variant), then notifies and re-renders; the countdown's expiry clears the bit and notifies again (`common/tiles/devices/TileArcaneEar.java:53-99`; the replay block event and its client half — instrument sound at the pitch ratio and a note particle — `common/tiles/devices/TileArcaneEar.java:145-182`, `common/blocks/devices/BlockArcaneEar.java:147-154`). The scan stops at the first match per tick, so several simultaneous matching notes collapse into one trigger, and because the mailbox is dropped at tick end, delivery is same-tick only: a note an ear never scans in its own tick is lost rather than delivered late (T1 and item 16 hunt the observable side of that window).

3. **What the ear persists, and what clients see.** The tile writes and reads exactly the pitch and the tone, and clamps the pitch to the valid range on load; the tone is itself a world-derived value that the placement and neighbour hooks refresh (`common/tiles/devices/TileArcaneEar.java:34-51`, `:101-138`). The output bit rides the block metadata, so it survives a reload with the block state, while the pulse countdown is a live field that no save hook carries — a reload mid-pulse drops the remainder of the pulse (a T7 observation). Clients see the block-state flips and the block event's sound and note particle; the pitch and the countdown never cross the wire as data — the ear's tile extends the plain base tile entity rather than the shared tile, so it carries no save-and-sync pair at all (`common/tiles/devices/TileArcaneEar.java:21`; the shared convention the sibling notes derive `common/tiles/TileThaumcraft.java:42-56`, `:58-74`).

### B. The Flux Condenser and its lattice

4. **One machine block and a lattice multiblock that must reach it.** The condenser is an iron machine block with no facing and a plain enable bit; the lattice is the paired multiblock piece whose clean and dirty variants differ in name and light level, whose six connection flags are derived per placement from its neighbours — side faces join lattice pieces, the down face joins only a condenser — and whose collision box follows those flags (`common/blocks/devices/BlockCondenser.java:14-19`; `common/blocks/devices/BlockCondenserLattice.java:41-49`, `:71-87`, `:159-200`). Placement and neighbour changes on either piece re-run a connectivity walk that drops any lattice block that no longer reaches a condenser or sits more than about eight-and-a-half blocks from it — seventy-four squared is the cut — and the walk hands the condenser a recheck flag by driving its lattice counter negative (`common/blocks/devices/BlockCondenserLattice.java:89-99`, `:120-133`, `:135-157`; the negative-flag write `:147-152`; the research text's eight-block detachment rule `assets/thaumcraft/lang/en_us.lang:1294`). The dirty variant is the clogged piece: clicking it with an Essentia Filter consumes the filter, turns the block back to its clean form with a pickup sound, and drops a flux crystal about half the time (`common/blocks/devices/BlockCondenserLattice.java:101-118`; the filter item name `assets/thaumcraft/lang/en_us.lang:67`, the crystal `:248`). Registration: condenser block `common/config/ConfigBlocks.java:345`, lattice and dirty `:346-347`, tile `:415`.

5. **The conversion contract: essentia in, one flux out — priced by lattice count, paced by lattice contribution.** The tile holds up to one hundred essentia and a like cap of condensed flux, both saved and synced as shorts (`common/tiles/devices/TileCondenser.java:23-32`, `:34-43`, `:45-56`). While enabled and latticed, every fifth tick it refills its essentia reserve by one unit per pass from each horizontal neighbour that is a transport holding essentia it can output; flux essentia offered this way is refused and clogs a lattice piece instead (which is the deterministic clog lever), and the top and bottom faces are not input faces at all (`common/tiles/devices/TileCondenser.java:107-134`, `:207-217`). Conversion requires: a computed interval above zero, essentia at least the computed cost, flux below the cap, the tick counter aligned to the interval, and at least one point of aura flux under the device — then it drains one aura flux, debits the cost from the reserve, adds one flux, syncs, and rolls the clog chance at one in fifty (`common/tiles/devices/TileCondenser.java:58-88`, `:90-105`). Both figures come from the geometry walk: each connected clean piece contributes one minus fifteen hundredths per connected side (dense packing discounts, per the research text), the contribution sum caps at forty, the interval is six hundred ticks minus fifteen per contribution with a floor of five ticks, and the cost is four plus the square root of the connected-piece count — so a single piece converts at a cost of five, matching the research text's five-or-more rule (`common/tiles/devices/TileCondenser.java:136-157`, `:159-205`; the player text `assets/thaumcraft/lang/en_us.lang:1294`). The condensed flux leaves only through the bottom face and only as flux essentia (`common/tiles/devices/TileCondenser.java:230-238`), and the device advertises a suction of 128 on every face but the bottom until its reserve is full, with a minimum suction of zero, so pipes feed it by its own pull (`common/tiles/devices/TileCondenser.java:219-228`). An Essentia Resonator used on it prints the cost and the interval in ticks and seconds on top of the usual contents and suction readout (`common/items/tools/ItemResonator.java:72-77`; the two readout lines `assets/thaumcraft/lang/en_us.lang:1069-1070`).

6. **The surface, the gate, and the persistence of the condenser.** The transport surface is: connectable on every face but the top, input on the four sides, output on the bottom only, contents reported as flux on every face, and refusal of everything but flux on the take call (`common/tiles/devices/TileCondenser.java:207-217`, `:230-238`, `:249-259`). The work gate is the shared device base's: placement and neighbour changes mirror redstone into the enable bit (`common/blocks/BlockTCDevice.java:54-66`, `:68-75`), and the tile's tick refuses both the refill and the conversion while disabled — a lever pauses the machine (`common/tiles/devices/TileCondenser.java:58-88`). Persistence is the two shorts plus a full re-derivation: the reserve and the flux count ride the save-and-sync NBT (`common/tiles/devices/TileCondenser.java:45-56`; the shared convention `common/tiles/TileThaumcraft.java:42-56`), while the lattice count, interval, and cost are live fields the first tick rebuilds by re-walking the lattice after any load, and every change to the reserve or the flux count is pushed to clients (`common/tiles/devices/TileCondenser.java:58-61`, `:136-157`; the push `common/tiles/TileThaumcraft.java:58-74`).

7. **The condenser's conservation surfaces — and one bytecode-confirmed oddity pair.** Four paths matter for the acceptance's "without dupes" clause. (a) The break spill: breaking any device tile of the transport family polls the aura by the transport surface's reported amount (`common/blocks/BlockTCTile.java:54-65`, `:73-76`), which for the condenser reads its flux count on every face (`common/tiles/devices/TileCondenser.java:249-259`) — so a working condenser broken mid-run converts its condensed flux straight back into aura pollution, while its essentia reserve simply dies with the tile; no drop carries either. (b) The push path: the condenser's push answer computes an acceptance figure from its reserve headroom and returns it after a sync — **without storing the unit** — so a contraption that pushes rather than gets pulled can lose essentia to a machine that reports success; the tree text was bytecode-checked and the read backend jar confirms this is faithful, not a decompiler artifact (`REF-0008` in [reference-catalog.md](reference-catalog.md); `common/tiles/devices/TileCondenser.java:240-247`). (c) The pull path's abort: the refill pass `return`s (not skips) on the first connectable neighbour that cannot output, so a face order that hits a non-outputting neighbour first starves the remaining faces for that pass — also bytecode-confirmed (`REF-0008`; `common/tiles/devices/TileCondenser.java:107-116`). (d) The clog economy: the flux-refusal clog (finding 5) and the one-in-fifty roll (`common/tiles/devices/TileCondenser.java:90-105`) are the only loss in the loop; the reserve debits exactly the computed cost per conversion, so the essenta total across a run is checkable against conversions times cost plus reserve delta (T3).

### C. The Arcane Levitator

8. **The lift contract: a range ladder, a per-entity per-tick cost, and an aura top-up in a thousand-two-hundred-point step.** The device is a wood block placeable in any of the six facings through the shared device placement rule (`common/blocks/devices/BlockLevitator.java:45-49`; the base placement `common/blocks/BlockTCDevice.java:54-66`). The tile keeps a range index over the ladder four, eight, sixteen, thirty-two (default index one — the research text's eight blocks) and a cost of two per range step — eight, sixteen, thirty-two, sixty-four internal units per entity per tick; a click on the device's key cuboid cycles the range with a key sound and a chat line, wrapping at the ladder's end (`common/tiles/devices/TileLevitator.java:29-41`, `:160-162`, `:164-175`; the click path `common/blocks/devices/BlockLevitator.java:64-76`). Whenever the internal store has fallen under ten, the server side drains up to one point from the chunk's aura and adds twelve hundred units per point actually drained, marking dirty and syncing (`common/tiles/devices/TileLevitator.java:59-63`). When enabled, the store is positive, and the resolved reach is positive, the tile lifts everything inside the box that the facing extends over: item entities, entities that report themselves pushable, and horses are eligible; a sneaking entity under an upward-facing levitator is eased down instead of lifted; other entities get a one-tenth impulse along the facing with vertical damping and a velocity clamp of about a third, and every processed entity's fall distance is cleared — the device negates fall damage inside its field (`common/tiles/devices/TileLevitator.java:64-126`). The resolving reach itself is a scan: the counter steps along the facing and the reach collapses to the first opaque block and extends as far as the ladder allows, resetting when the range changes (`common/tiles/devices/TileLevitator.java:43-58`). Client sparks draw from the same values (`common/tiles/devices/TileLevitator.java:129-145`; the client-only hitbox and highlight machinery `common/blocks/devices/BlockLevitator.java:78-91`, `:105-125`).

9. **What the levitator persists, its idle draw, and its two text gaps.** The range index and the store both ride the save-and-sync route, and every top-up or range change pushes the pair to clients; the enable bit is the shared base's redstone mirror (`common/tiles/devices/TileLevitator.java:147-158`, `:59-63`, `:164-175`; `common/blocks/BlockTCDevice.java:68-75`). The idle draw is real: the top-up is not inside the enable gate, so a freshly placed or drained levitator refills from the aura even while a lever holds it off, and the gate only stops the lifting (the T4 step watches this). Two text gaps to settle by observation: the research text's one-vis-per-second claim against a top-up that runs only under the ten-unit mark and hands out twelve hundred units per aura point — a healthy chunk makes one point last a long while, and how the Thaumometer's integer readout steps over that time is the T4 measurement — and the chat line's per-minute figure against the per-entity per-tick cost it prints (`assets/thaumcraft/lang/en_us.lang:1087`, `:1494`). The store can also dip below zero for the tick that spends past it — the spend subtracts before it breaks — and the next top-up erases the dip if the aura has a point to give (`common/tiles/devices/TileLevitator.java:116-119`, `:59-63`).

### D. The Hungry Chest and the Redstone Relay

10. **The Hungry Chest is a vanilla chest that eats on contact.** The block is a container block (not a device-base block), wood-sounded, hardness two and a half, on the mod's creative tab by its own statement, with its own horizontal facing, its own placement pair (the placement hook stores the placer's horizontal facing and then the placed-by hook re-forces the opposite of the yaw-derived facing), and an invisible render type — the chest itself is drawn by a chest-model special renderer whose lid follows the inherited chest counters (`common/blocks/devices/BlockHungryChest.java:44-53`, `:55-57`, `:59-65`, `:75-81`, `:83-91`; `client/renderers/tile/TileHungryChestRenderer.java:14-21`, `:66`, `:86`; registration `proxies/ProxyTESR.java:59`, block `common/config/ConfigBlocks.java:317`, tile `:388`). The tile is a vanilla chest tile with the neighbour pairing switched off by forcing the adjacent-check flag and with the close hook re-stated under the mod's own block-type guard (`common/tiles/devices/TileHungryChest.java:10-16`, `:22-29`). The ingestion itself is the block's collision hook: on the server, an item entity that touches the block is inserted through the top face with the shared inventory helper — which resolves a handler at the block (the vanilla-inventory hook first, then a wrapped inventory) and inserts hopper-style, filling stacks first — and the item's stack is rewritten to the leftovers, or the entity is removed outright when nothing is left, with an eating sound whenever anything was taken and a dirty mark on the tile (`common/blocks/devices/BlockHungryChest.java:113-134`; `api/ThaumcraftInvHelper.java:49-60`, `:175-183`). There is no magnet radius in the block: contact is literal. The screen is the vanilla chest screen opened on the tile, the comparator reads the inherited inventory fill, and the break drops the inventory through the vanilla helper (`common/blocks/devices/BlockHungryChest.java:102-111`, `:136-146`, `:93-100`). The device's research and recipe: the Hungry Chest entry sits at `assets/thaumcraft/research/artifice.json:19` under `BASEARTIFICE` (`:23`) and lists its recipe in the second stage (`:32`); the recipe is an arcane workbench shape at fifteen vis and one each of air and water (`common/config/ConfigRecipes.java:235`), and the device carries no output path to other inventories, matching the research text's explicit no-hopper-output note (`assets/thaumcraft/lang/en_us.lang:1533`).

11. **The Redstone Relay is the repeater family's tunable member: input threshold and output strength, both settable.** The block is a thin, zero-hardness circuit block that needs a solid top under it and drops when that support goes; placement orients it like its family (the facing side is the input side, set to the placer's opposite horizontal unless the placer sneaks) and starts it unpowered, and the output side — the one the research text marks with a redstone torch — is the facing's opposite, the only direction the power query answers on (`common/blocks/devices/BlockRedstoneRelay.java:51-62`, `:64-66`, `:85-91`, `:233-239`, `:149-159`; the notify target opposite the facing `:241-254`; the player text `assets/thaumcraft/lang/en_us.lang:1482`). Its input reading is the family's: the strength of the block it faces, including a wire's own power value; its activation test is input strength at or above a settable threshold; and its emitted strength is a settable output value clamped between one and fifteen (`common/blocks/devices/BlockRedstoneRelay.java:190-208`, `:269-275`; the value pair in the tile, defaults one and fifteen, cycles wrapping fifteen back to one `common/tiles/devices/TileRedstoneRelay.java:17-25`, `:40-59`). The two values are the two toggles: a click that ray-traces to the output-side cuboid cycles the output, the input-side cuboid cycles the threshold, both ring the key sound, re-run the state update, and notify — the click refuses only when player edit rights are off (`common/blocks/devices/BlockRedstoneRelay.java:96-121`; the click cuboids `common/tiles/devices/TileRedstoneRelay.java:62-108`). The tick behaviour is the repeater's: changes schedule a delayed state flip two ticks out with the family's priority flags (including the facing-a-repeater flag), a flip that finds its input already gone reschedules itself so short pulses come out at least two ticks wide, and the state itself lives in the enable bit, which for this block means `powered` rather than the base's redstone-disabled mirror (`common/blocks/devices/BlockRedstoneRelay.java:123-136`, `:175-188`, `:291-293`; the base rule it replaces `common/blocks/BlockTCDevice.java:68-75`). It interoperates with vanilla repeater and comparator lock checks by design (`common/blocks/devices/BlockRedstoneRelay.java:277-289`, `:305-307`), and registration is block `common/config/ConfigBlocks.java:339`, tile `:407`.

12. **The relay's persistence and its research gate.** Both settings ride the save-and-sync route as bytes and push to clients on every cycle, and the powered bit rides the block state, so a reload restores the pair exactly (`common/tiles/devices/TileRedstoneRelay.java:27-38`, `:40-59`; the shared convention `common/tiles/TileThaumcraft.java:42-56`, `:58-74`). ### E. Shared surfaces, boundaries, and dormant paths

13. **How the five use the shared device base — three ways, and one that sits outside it.** The base supplies the default state per interface, the facing property, the enabled bit defaulting on, placement with the sneak inversion for facing blocks, the neighbour-and-placement hook that mirrors `unpowered` into the enabled bit, and the metadata packing with the enabled bit at the eighth bit (`common/blocks/BlockTCDevice.java:16-31`, `:44-52`, `:54-66`, `:68-75`, `:91-115`; the bit reads `common/lib/utils/BlockStateUtils.java:16-30`). The levitator and the condenser consume it whole — their work gates read the mirrored bit and powering them stops work while the rest of the tile keeps idling (findings 5, 8, 9). The relay replaces the mirror completely with its repeater state machine (finding 11). The ear bypasses both hooks (finding 1). The hungry chest never touches the base: it is a plain container block with its own state (finding 10). The acquisition side is uniform: every device's recipe is an arcane workbench recipe gated by its research key, and every device block sits on the mod's creative tab through the base class or its own statement (`common/blocks/BlockTC.java:20`, `common/blocks/devices/BlockHungryChest.java:52`). The gate positions: the Ear entry at `assets/thaumcraft/research/artifice.json:114` under the Relay (`:118`) with both of its recipes in the second stage (`:128` — the toggle is a shapeless escalation of the base ear plus a lever, `common/config/ConfigRecipes.java:226`, and the base ear is fifteen vis and one air, `:225`); the Levitator at `assets/thaumcraft/research/artifice.json:37` under the Hungry Chest and the third metallurgy stage (`:41`) with its recipe in the second stage (`:50` — thirty-five vis and one air, `common/config/ConfigRecipes.java:223`); the Hungry Chest at fifteen vis, one earth and one water (finding 10); and the Condenser pair under the basics-category `FLUXCLEANUP` entry at `assets/thaumcraft/research/basics.json:297`, whose parents include the flux and vis-battery entries (`:301`) and whose second stage lists both recipes (`:310`) — five hundred vis with five each of air, water, and entropy for the condenser and one hundred vis with three each of earth and air for the lattice (`common/config/ConfigRecipes.java:261-262`).

14. **Dormant and edge paths a port must not copy blindly.** The relay carries an empty random-tick override that can never run because the block never asks for random ticks (`common/blocks/devices/BlockRedstoneRelay.java:93-94`). The ear's scan consumes at most one mailbox entry per tick and the mailbox itself is bounded by the tick, so multiplicity is collapsed (finding 2). The condenser's push answer syncs and marks dirty even though it changed nothing, and its fill loop's abort-on-non-outputting-neighbour is confirmed bytecode rather than a decompiler slip (`REF-0008`; `common/tiles/devices/TileCondenser.java:240-247`, `:107-116`). The levitator's store can read negative between a spend and the next refill (finding 9). The hungry chest's placed-facing path runs twice — placement hook, then placed-by — so the second write is the one that stands (`common/blocks/devices/BlockHungryChest.java:83-91`). And the mod's placement guard near active bosses is a stub that always answers false in this tree — no device placement here is ever refused by it (`common/lib/events/WorldEvents.java:41-58`).

## Multiplayer and divergence classes

15. **What crosses the wire, per device.** The ear: block-state flips for the output bit (pulse and latch alike) plus the block event that carries the sound and note particle to watchers; the pitch and the countdown never sync (`common/tiles/devices/TileArcaneEar.java:53-99`; `common/blocks/devices/BlockArcaneEar.java:147-154`). The condenser: the reserve and the flux count on every change, and nothing else — the geometry numbers are re-derived locally on both sides by the same walk (`common/tiles/devices/TileCondenser.java:58-88`, `:136-157`; the push `common/tiles/TileThaumcraft.java:58-74`). The levitator: the range and the store on every top-up and range change, and the lift impulse itself runs on both sides off those synced values (`common/tiles/devices/TileLevitator.java:59-63`, `:64-126`, `:147-158`). The relay: both settings on every cycle plus the powered-state flips (`common/tiles/devices/TileRedstoneRelay.java:40-59`). The hungry chest: the inherited chest machinery — container plus the lid counter events — and its ingestion is server-side only (`common/tiles/devices/TileHungryChest.java:22-29`; `common/blocks/devices/BlockHungryChest.java:113-134`).

16. **Divergence classes to hunt at rank one.** (a) A second client watching a condenser's flux count tick: the counts sync, but the interval cadence is invisible off-device — record what the client's readout shows across one conversion (T2). (b) The ear's window: two ears tuned alike both fire on one note, and a note in the tick an ear misses is dropped, not delayed — record end-to-end latency between the note sound and the pulse at both variants (T1). (c) The levitator's double-side impulse: reconcile a second client's view of a lifted entity against the server's (T4). (d) The relay's stretch: read a lamp's pulse widths against the thresholds (T6). (e) The hungry chest's lid counters on a two-player open/close (T5). All five devices tick off the shared aura only where noted (condenser and levitator), so cross-client aura drift belongs to `WLD-01` and shows up here as different reserve readings on different clients' readouts.

## Rank-1 capture checklist

Reference setup for every step: the clean 1.12.2 profile whose only mods are the BETA26 jar and Baubles (`REF-0002` and the "Environment feasibility" section of [reference-catalog.md](reference-catalog.md)); a fresh creative world with its seed recorded in a new `REF` entry before the session. Every device in the set is on the mod's creative tab without research (`common/blocks/BlockTC.java:20`; the hungry chest by its own statement `common/blocks/devices/BlockHungryChest.java:52`), and using a device never needs its research — only the recipes to build one do, and those gates belong to each recipe's own lane; grants, when wanted for a crafting check, happen with the operator commands `/thaumcraft research <player> <list|all|reset|<key>>` and `/thaumcraft reload` between measurements, never during one (`common/lib/CommandThaumcraft.java:64-70`, `:84-107`). Tools on hand: a Thaumometer (its readout is fed from the chunk's aura, `common/items/tools/ItemThaumometer.java:92-101`), an Essentia Resonator — which prints the condenser's cost and interval (`common/items/tools/ItemResonator.java:72-77`) — a lever or torch for redstone, chests and labelled jars, a note block with the wood/rock/sand variants needed for the tone tests, an Essentia Filter for the lattice repair, and a tube holding essence to break as a flux-seeding lever (the break-to-pollution path is [alc-02-evidence.md](alc-02-evidence.md) item 17). Start every structure from a recorded position and screenshot so the reload steps can compare exact cells.

- **T1 — The ear, its tone, and both variants.** Mount an ear on a wood surface and record the pitch cycle on sixteen right-clicks (wrapping at twenty-five), the click sound, and the note particle. Place a note block tuned by a wood block underneath next to it, play the matching pitch, and record: the ear re-playing the note, the output staying on for about half a second, a redstone lamp lighting for exactly that span, and the same note with a mismatched pitch doing nothing. Repeat with the toggle variant and record the latch behaviour (one flip per notes, two notes returning it). Test a redstone-triggered note against a player-played one. Break the mounting block and record the ear dropping (findings 1-2; bounded to one ear per variant).
- **T2 — The condenser multiblock and its readouts.** Place a condenser with one lattice on top and read the resonator: record the cost and interval it prints, then add lattices one at a time to five and read again; record a clean line detaching a piece placed beyond eight blocks. Feed a labelled jar of non-flux essentia to a side, seed aura flux by breaking an essence-holding tube in the condenser's own chunk (the aura store is chunk-scoped), and record the first conversion: the reserve falling by the printed cost, a flux essentia unit arriving in a jar on the bottom face, and the aura readout dropping by one (findings 4-5; bounded to one multiblock).
- **T3 — The condenser's conservation probe (the acceptance slice).** Run a fixed count of conversions with everything counted — reserve before and after, aura flux before and after, units drained from the bottom jar — and compare against conversions-times-cost. Then: clog a lattice by offering flux essentia with a jar, record the clog, the cost still being charged, and the repair with a filter (recording whether a flux crystal drops); arrange two neighbours, one a non-outputting machine and one a jar, and record whether the reserve still fills from the jar side; and aim a transport output at the condenser side and record whether pushed units vanish — the tree says the push answer lies, so watch the receiving jar and the reserve (findings 5-7; bounded to one multiblock, two jar fills).
- **T4 — The levitator.** Place the levitator upright with the Thaumometer held and record the idle aura draw while a lever holds it off. Toggle the range through four, eight, sixteen, thirty-two and record the chat line and the lift height each time; drop items, a chicken, and ride it as the player, recording the lift, the sneak descent, and no fall damage on the way down; spend a fixed number of entity-seconds and compare the aura drain against the two candidate economies printed in the texts (one point per second against one point per twelve hundred cost units). Then power it and record that the lifting stops while the top-up continues (target 6; findings 8-9; bounded to one device, two minutes of aura observation).
- **T5 — The hungry chest.** Place it, throw single items and a stack at its top and sides, and record what gets absorbed, the eating sound, the leftovers remaining as an item entity, the screen opening, the comparator reading, and that nothing rolls in from a distance (the reach question). Open and close the screen and record the lid animation; break it full and record the inventory dropping; reload and repeat one absorption (target 7; finding 10; bounded to one chest).
- **T6 — The relay.** Place the relay as a repeater line and record: input from the facing side only, output on the torch-marked side, the two-tick delay, and short pulses arriving at least two ticks wide. Cycle the input threshold to seven and the output strength to seven and record the exact activation and lamp strength for inputs below and at the threshold; power it with a lever held down and record the reload keeping both settings; break the support and record the drop (target 8; findings 11-12; bounded to one relay).
- **T7 — The reload and unload sweep for all five.** With each device mid-work (ear mid-pulse, condenser mid-interval with junk in the reserve, levitator mid-lift with its store part full, relay mid-pulse, chest with a partly-absorbed stack), save and reload; record every device's state survival, every count conservation, and which live fields reset. Then walk away long enough to unload each chunk and return, repeating one measurement per device (findings 3, 6, 9, 10, 12, 15; bounded to one reload and one unload per device).
- **T8 — The server-side acceptance wording.** Repeat T3's count and T5's absorption on a dedicated server with a remote client if the EULA step in [reference-catalog.md](reference-catalog.md) is taken; otherwise record on singleplayer and mark the dedicated-server clause not-run. The acceptance "activate and produce expected outputs on a server without dupes, surviving reload" fails on any count that escapes in T3 or T7 (findings 3, 5-7, 15-16).
